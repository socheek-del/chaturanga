# Chaturaji product design

## Direction: Courtyard Ledger

The interface treats four armies as neighboring courtyards around one shared table. The visual language combines warm paper, fine ledger rules, a carved four-part seal and four restrained seat colors: vermilion, leaf, brass and ink blue. Self-hosted Literata gives game headings a book-like voice; IBM Plex Sans keeps controls and records clear at small sizes.

The board remains the main focus. Seat cards show who controls each army, points and original-King status. The current die is outlined in copper; its allowed piece types appear beside the board. Captures sit in four short pools, while dice rolls and moves share a replayable action list. No visual element implies that the reconstruction's uncertain procedures are historically settled.

The home page explains the historical reconstruction and offers bot play, same-device play, private rooms and the tutorial. The tutorial alternates concise explanations and questions, then asks the learner to make a legal engine-checked move. The private room presents four stable seats, connection status, current dice, scores and an invite link.

## Accessibility and responsive behavior

- Every square and action is a keyboard-focusable button with an accessible name.
- Seat identity is shown with words and shape as well as color.
- Legal destinations use the board's shared focus and target patterns.
- Reduced-motion preferences are respected by the shared board.
- The board, dice, score list and room seats reflow into one column on narrow screens.
- Fonts and all PWA assets are self-hosted for offline use.

## Product voice

Call the rules a “source-based reconstruction” and identify documented conventions as choices. Explain the dice, scoring, promotion, Boat triumph, allied throne and third-King ending in original prose. Avoid modern publisher branding, copied diagrams and claims about popularity.
