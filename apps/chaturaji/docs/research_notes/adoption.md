# Chaturaji adoption notes

Research date: 2026-11-26.

- The available record establishes historical practice, not comparative popularity. Al-Biruni describes the dice game around 1030. Murray reports a later Sanskrit account and evidence that a four-handed diceless form was still played in India around 1900. Maria Schetelich's 2018 review likewise notes that the dice form was already becoming obsolete by the time European writers described it in the late 18th century and that the evidence is sparse.
- The current [Wikipedia article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chaturaji) presents the dice-based form as the original and describes a separate diceless variant. Neither the article nor the sources reviewed provide player counts, recurring competition participation, market share, or another basis to call one version the most popular today.
- The rollout plan therefore retains its explicit historical target: a four-seat dice reconstruction based on Al-Biruni, cross-checked against Murray's translation and analysis. This is a documented historical choice, not a popularity claim. The late-19th-century diceless form is not merged into the selected game.
