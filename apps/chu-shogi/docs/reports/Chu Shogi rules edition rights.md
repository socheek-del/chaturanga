# Choose Chu Shogi’s clearest tournament rules

Use the **Chu Shogi Renmei’s 2019 competition rules with its 2004 lion clarifications** as the product’s named rules profile. They are the best documented current association standard and have the strongest evidence of over-the-board use; available sources do not establish any rules edition as statistically most popular. The selected game is the classical 12×12 form, with 46 pieces per side, no drops, and 29 piece ranks including promoted forms. Historical texts inform the association’s modern codification, but do not settle every edge case, so the product must identify the chosen edition and label its digital conventions. Public facts about movement can support an original implementation, while contemporary rules prose, diagrams, and art should not be reused without permission. Before coding terminal logic, settle the Renmei text’s mate-versus-capture wording and record its prescribed interpretations for lion protection and repetition. ([Renmei 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Renmei 2004 lion supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm))

## The Renmei standard is the strongest documented choice

Chu Shogi has several documented rules traditions, but the evidence supports choosing one operational standard rather than merging them. The Renmei labels its rules as the 2019 edition and says its 2004 revision moved away from an earlier Okazaki-based interpretation toward the historical *Chūshōgi Shinan-shō* and *Chūshōgi Zenshū*, while adding modern clarifications. A transcribed 1703 *Shinan-shō* and a National Diet Library scan of its 1915 reprint provide traceable historical context; they are not a complete modern software specification. The product should therefore describe its profile as “Chu Shogi Renmei 2019, with the 2004 lion clarifications,” not as an uncontested reconstruction of every historical practice. ([Renmei 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Renmei 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [Renmei historical text](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/Okazaki.htm); [National Diet Library scan](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1869566))

The strongest adoption evidence is organizational rather than numerical: Renmei records show national tournaments recurring through August 2026 and a Meijin series through January 2026. Its 2022 national report listed six A-class and four B-class entrants, which confirms real but specialized over-the-board play. Lishogi supports online human-versus-human games and reported 201 members in its Chu Shogi club in November 2026; that is a platform group count, not a count of active players or a comparison of rules editions. A 2015 paper similarly described a few current players in Japan and abroad. These sources establish continuity and online interest, but no comparable player, game, or event dataset establishes that Renmei, Hodges, or Lishogi rules are “most popular.” Selecting Renmei is a documented-standard recommendation, not a popularity claim. ([Renmei tournament records](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/record.htm); [Renmei 2022 tournament reports](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/taikai/zenkoku/index.htm); [Lishogi Chu Shogi Club](https://lishogi.org/team/chu-shogi-club); [2015 Game Programming Workshop paper](https://ipsj.ixsq.nii.ac.jp/records/145794/files/IPSJ-GPWS2015019.pdf))

“Modern Chu Shogi” should not be treated as another rules setting for this product: its published redesign uses an 11×11 board and 80 pieces, and changes both movement and lion conventions. It is a distinct game design, with no adoption evidence comparable to the classical form. The Renmei 12×12 setup should be the sole v1 start position. ([Gendai Chu Shogi rules site](https://seesaawiki.jp/nari_shogi/); [Renmei 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm))

## The 12×12 game needs dedicated lion and promotion rules

Each side begins with 46 pieces on a 12×12 square board; captures remove pieces, and there are no drops. The base set includes King, Lion, Free King, Drunk Elephant, Kirin, Phoenix, Rook, Bishop, Dragon King, Dragon Horse, Vertical Mover, Side Mover, Reverse Chariot, Lance, Blind Tiger, Ferocious Leopard, Gold General, Silver General, Copper General, Go-Between, and twelve Pawns. Promotions create 29 total ranks, including Prince (the promoted Drunk Elephant), Horned Falcon, and Soaring Eagle. Horned Falcon and Soaring Eagle gain restricted two-step Lion power; **Lion Hawk is not a Chu Shogi piece** and belongs to Tenjiku Shogi. The source-derived starting array and movement table are recorded in the research notes; user-facing names and explanations should be newly written. ([Renmei piece guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm); [H.G. Muller’s setup and piece reference](https://hgm.nubati.net/rules/Chu.html); [Tenjiku Shogi piece reference](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenjiku_shogi))

Promotion is optional on entering the opponent’s four farthest ranks, and a declined piece does not simply retain an unrestricted right to promote on any later quiet move inside that zone. Under the selected Renmei reading, another opportunity comes through capturing in the zone or while leaving it, or by leaving and re-entering. A Pawn gets a further opportunity on the last rank; the Association’s correspondence rule also grants that recovery to a Lance, so an online product should adopt and disclose that explicit communication-play extension. A Pawn or Lance left unpromoted on its last rank is immobile. This promotion eligibility is stateful, so it must be represented in saved positions and replay rather than inferred from a piece’s current square alone. ([Renmei 2019 promotion rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Renmei 2004 clarification](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [H.G. Muller’s promotion comparison](https://hgm.nubati.net/MSM/ChuPromoRule.html))

Lion movement and capture restrictions define much of the game’s identity. A Lion may make up to two King steps, turn between them, capture on either leg, jump two squares, capture and return (igui), or make a non-capturing out-and-back pass (jitto) when an adjacent square is empty. A non-adjacent Lion capture is restricted when the target Lion is protected; protection includes line attacks that become effective after the move. The Renmei’s clarifications also cover an eligible intervening capture with the enemy Lion (tsukegui), a one-turn counterstrike limit after a non-Lion captures a protected Lion, and the rule that Pawns and Go-Betweens cannot serve as the intervening capture. The tutorials and move input must teach these as explicit cases, not compress them into a generic “Lion rule.” ([Renmei 2019 lion rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Renmei 2004 edge-case supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [Renmei historical text](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/Okazaki.htm))

## Public rules facts permit original implementation, not copied assets

The rules mechanics and historical facts can be independently expressed in code and original explanations, but the research found no open license for the Renmei’s modern rules pages, Hodges’s *Middle Shogi Manual*, Silverman’s guide, or the German association’s pages. The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes unprotected game methods from potentially protected expressive rules text and board art; that is U.S. guidance, not global clearance. The 1703 manual is historical, but modern transcription, translation, editorial notes, typesetting, and scans may carry separate rights or access restrictions. Recreate piece art and diagrams, write the tutorial and rules explanations from independently verified facts, and avoid copying curated problems or source wording. ([U.S. Copyright Office, Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html); [17 U.S.C. § 102](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html); [National Diet Library free-data guidance](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/dlib/standards/opendataset/))

HaChu’s repository declares CC0 for its engine source and is a possible licensed code reference if the implementation derives from it and records the provenance. That license does not cover unclear bundled artwork, logos, manuals, or third-party materials. Compatibility also cannot be assumed: Lishogi’s 2022 launch note warned that HaChu’s rules differences could make its legal moves incompatible with the site’s version. The game name is descriptive in the sources reviewed, but no official USPTO or J-PlatPat search results were verified; avoid association logos and trade dress, use distinct product branding, and make no affiliation claim. A trademark clearance conclusion remains open. ([HaChu CC0 repository](https://github.com/ddugovic/hachu); [Lishogi launch announcement](https://lishogi.org/blog/post/Y6kTwxEAAKKgP_sV); [JPO trademark search guidance](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/support/j_platpat/trademark_search.html); [USPTO search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search))

## Record the remaining conventions before engine work

The Renmei’s Japanese win clause names both checkmate and capturing the King, while Silverman’s English explanation says capture is required and checkmate alone does not end the game. That conflict affects terminal detection, bots, and the tutorial and should be resolved against a full translation or documented tournament practice before coding. The Association’s 2004 supplement does prescribe a conservative “whole Lion move” reading for the rare case where a Pawn or Go-Between was the only protector and is captured during that Lion turn; preserve it as a regression case. For multiple-Lion positions, the evidence supports judging protection on the position after the contemplated capture without recursively applying the restriction to hypothetical recaptures, while acknowledging that the sources do not provide a complete algorithm for every compound position. ([Renmei 2019 win clause](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Silverman’s guide](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/); [Renmei 2004 edge cases](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [H.G. Muller’s Lion discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Lion_moves))

Repetition is not merely a fourfold draw: the Renmei rules assign a duty to vary based on continuous attack, attacks on other pieces, and reciprocal jitto. The quiet-cycle outcome is summarized differently by secondary sources, so the product should encode the Renmei’s explicit responsibilities and document the quiet-cycle adjudication as a digital convention. The same caution applies to “bare King” endings: use the Association’s specific barren-material clause rather than importing broader English-language extensions, and offer mutual agreement as an explicit draw action. Each convention should have focused engine fixtures and be reflected in the tutorial. ([Renmei 2019 repetition and endgame rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Lishogi rules summary](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja); [Silverman’s endgame explanation](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/))

The repository’s generic Variant exposes board dimensions and opaque move strings, and board-ui can render rectangular dimensions, so the 12×12 board is an architectural fit. The difficult interface seam is Lion movement: a complete Lion turn can contain two legs and two captures, while ordinary board input selects one source and destination. Chu Shogi should have a product-specific full-turn route selector backed by engine legal moves, and the pure Variant must retain route identity, all captures, promotion eligibility, counterstrike state, and repetition history. The game is two-player, so private online rooms are applicable; server-side validation must run the same engine. A dedicated bot and interactive lessons are required for the release, with lessons exercising Lion movement, protection, promotion choices, royal pieces, and repetition. ## Conclusion

Chu Shogi is ready for implementation once the terminal condition is stated and the digital repetition and rare Lion-protection conventions are pinned to the Renmei profile. The evidence does not require changing the selected game or inventing a popularity ranking: it supports shipping the classical form under its clearest current tournament rules, with transparent notes where the record is incomplete. The codebase can host the 12×12 product, but its Lion turn model and stateful promotion rights require a custom rules engine and move-entry path. Public launch should remain separate from private-stage readiness until branding review, domain routing, and rights boundaries are resolved.
