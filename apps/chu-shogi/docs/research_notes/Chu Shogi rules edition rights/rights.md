# Chu Shogi rules edition rights

## Historical title and rules provenance

### Takeaway

Chu Shogi is an old Japanese game, but the commonly repeated medieval origin story does not establish that a surviving, reliably dated medieval source gives the complete rules now played. The earliest securely cataloged Chu Shogi-specific item found here is a 1663 puzzle work; the detailed rules lineage points to a 1591 source and later copies, with the claimed earlier ancestry and exact edition dates still uncertain.

### Cited Findings

- A commonly cited first mention is the mid-14th-century *Yūgaku ōrai*, but the Chu Shogi overview cautions that earlier references may not describe the game in its present form. This is a secondary account; I did not inspect the underlying manuscript or find a primary-source catalog record confirming the identification. [Chu Shogi overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi) · [Shogi variant chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant)
- The National Institute of Japanese Literature's Union Catalogue catalogs a printed *Chūshōgi Zushiki* (中象戯圖式), attributed to first-generation Itō Sōkan, with a 1663 preface. [NIJL Union Catalogue record](https://kokusho.nijl.ac.jp/biblio/100134541?ln=en)
- The Japanese Chu Shogi Association's bibliography describes that 1663 *Chūshōgi Zushiki* as a set of Chu Shogi mate problems, says the source was not seen by its compiler, and distinguishes it from the same-year 50-problem collection. It is therefore evidence of Chu Shogi literature in 1663, but not a located general rules manual. [Association's historical-source bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm)
- A 2020 rules article identifies *Shōgi Rokushu no Zushiki* as the first detailed presentation of Chu Shogi's board and rules, while recounting a disputed lineage: Minase Kanenari's 1591 *Shōgi Zu* allegedly copied a 1443 text, which itself allegedly copied an unknown older text. Treat the 1443 and 1591 lineage as a reported provenance claim, not as a verified surviving source. [Dr Eric Silverman, “Chu Shogi, Part I: How to Play”](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/)
- The date of the *Rokushu no Zushiki* edition is inconsistent across secondary references: the Japanese Association lists 1821, while another overview lists 1811. The original imprint was not checked, so do not state one date as settled. [Association bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm) · [Shōgi Zushiki overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgi_Zushiki)

### Inferences

- For a historical note in the product, state that Chu Shogi is attested in medieval-era references while labeling the exact first attestation and the 1443/1591 transmission story as uncertain. For the selected playable edition, identify the modern rule authority/version explicitly instead of claiming a single undisputed historical ruleset.
- Reconstruct the playable mechanics from independently checked facts and document decisions (especially lion restrictions, promotion timing, royal-piece endings, and bare-king rules); do not present modern association regulations as identical to every historical practice.

### Gaps

- The original *Yūgaku ōrai*, the alleged 1443 source, Minase's 1591 *Shōgi Zu*, and the imprint of the later *Rokushu no Zushiki* edition were not examined directly. The cataloged 1663 source is a puzzle collection, so this review does not establish the earliest surviving complete rules text.
- The 1663 catalog record identifies the work and author, but the image/service class and access conditions need separate review before reusing any scan or transcription.

## Copyright, licenses, and original-expression boundary

### Takeaway

The strongest explicit adaptation route found is HaChu's public-domain/CC0 engine source. No blanket license was found for the modern rules prose, diagrams, lessons, piece art, or the Chu Shogi Association's materials; a product can draw on game mechanics and historical facts while writing its rules, tutorials, notation, and visual design anew.

### Cited Findings

- The U.S. Copyright Office says a game idea, game name/title, and method of play are not protected by copyright, while sufficiently expressive rules text and board/container artwork can be copyrightable. This is U.S. copyright guidance, not a worldwide legal conclusion. [U.S. Copyright Office, “Games”](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The Copyright Act likewise excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, and methods of operation from copyright protection, while protecting eligible fixed expression. [17 U.S.C. §102](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)
- The Japanese Chu Shogi Association publishes a detailed rules page labeled “2019 edition,” corrected 14 September 2019. The Association homepage displays a copyright notice reserving rights; neither page offered an open reuse license in the material reviewed. [Competition rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm) · [Association homepage](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/)
- George Hodges's *Middle Shogi Manual* is a modern English-language work (1992; second edition 2002) containing rules, strategy, annotated historical games, and a large set of mating problems. Sources reviewed identify it as a published work for sale and show no open license for reproducing its prose, diagrams, or curated problem set. [Bibliographic entry](https://www.chessvariants.com/dictionary/cv-guide.pdf) · [Abstract Games article describing its contents and editions](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_18_image_correction_.pdf) · [Shogi publications price list](https://14662953492138192970.googlegroups.com/attach/1a55f35d79c52/Shogi%20Pricelist%20%28stirling%29.pdf?part=0.1&vt=ANaJVrHm9MdqR-taoN1BRKQkm7wLIwvN6-bYCqwJzQzpPWxRQYlmnNgSkQ4gB8BDuCK1_Vh3sPg20BPHjYgHPYpfbv4Zdbv5mpeqss7KihXvIr9jcBI_uT8)
- Dr Silverman's 2020 English rules article contains original explanatory prose and diagrams; no adaptation license was stated on the page reviewed. The German Chu Shogi Association maintains its own site and rules/setup material, also without an express adaptation license located in the pages reviewed. [Silverman article](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/) · [GCSA rules page](https://www.chushogi.de/rules/chu_rules_setup.htm)
- HaChu is a Chu Shogi-capable engine. Its GitHub mirror declares CC0-1.0; the mirrored manual/source description says the source code is released into the public domain. This is an express license for that engine source, not a license to unrelated prose, historical scans, puzzle collections, logos, or art bundled by other parties. [HaChu repository and CC0 license](https://github.com/ddugovic/hachu) · [Debian source page carrying HaChu's public-domain source header](https://sources.debian.org/src/hachu/0.21-7-g1c1f14a-1/hachu.c)
- The National Diet Library says its free datasets do not include the referenced digital images/recordings, and describes image datasets as covering works public domain due to expiry or images for which permission was granted. This distinction means free bibliographic metadata is not, by itself, permission to reuse a scan. [NDL Free Data Service](https://www.ndl.go.jp/en/dlib/standards/opendataset/)
- This repository's roadmap requires permission or an applicable license covering a faithful digital project for newly authored or published titles, and separately directs original UI, art, and rules explanations. [Repository chess-like roadmap](../../../../../docs/CHESS-LIKE-ROADMAP.md)

### Inferences

- A clean implementation can use factual mechanics and independently verified piece names/move powers, but should use newly written rule descriptions, tutorial narration, examples, notation explanations, diagrams, and art. Avoid copying Hodges's wording or arrangement, association text, Silverman's prose/diagrams, contemporary piece glyph sets, or curated historical mate positions/solutions.
- HaChu's CC0/public-domain source is a plausible route to the repository's licensing gate if the rules engine is derived from that source and its provenance is recorded. The permission does not extend to HaChu's bundled artwork or to third-party materials; verify each reused file and do not imply that the Association or engine author sponsors the product.
- The 17th-century rules/puzzle texts themselves are old historical works, but modern transcription, translation, editorial notes, scans, typesetting, and newly drawn reproductions can have separate rights or access terms. Prefer independently authored expression and use only image assets whose reuse status is affirmatively documented.

### Gaps

- No explicit open license was located for the Japanese Association's rule pages, GCSA pages, Hodges's manual, or Silverman's article. This review did not contact authors or organizations, as instructed.
- The exact license/provenance of HaChu's non-code repository files (including images/logo) was not established; limit the candidate reuse to source files covered by its declared license unless asset-level evidence is obtained.
- This is a source review, not legal advice or an international copyright opinion. Local counsel/owner review may be needed for the jurisdictions in which the product will be distributed.

## Name and public-branding review

### Takeaway

“Chu Shogi” is used as the common name of the historical game and literally corresponds to “Middle Shogi,” but trademark status was not conclusively cleared in official databases. An active Japanese Chu Shogi Association and a German association use closely related names, so public branding should identify the product as an independent game implementation and avoid their names, logos, and trade dress.

### Cited Findings

- The game overview gives the title as “Chu shogi” / 中将棋 and translates it as “Middle Shogi”; this supports treating the phrase as a descriptive game name, not a conclusion about trademark rights. [Chu Shogi overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi)
- The Japanese Chu Shogi Association currently presents itself under the name 日本中将棋連盟 and publishes rules, lessons, events, and an association history; its homepage carries a copyright notice. [Association homepage](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/) · [Association rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- The German Chu Shogi Association also maintains a public site describing its Chu Shogi activities and linking rules, strategy, games, and events. [GCSA homepage](https://www.chushogi.de/)
- The Japan Patent Office directs users to J-PlatPat for Japanese trademark keyword/classification searches, and the USPTO provides a public federal trademark search database. [JPO trademark search guidance](https://www.jpo.go.jp/e/support/j_platpat/trademark_search.html) · [USPTO trademark search](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search)
- The U.S. Copyright Office's statement that game names/titles are not copyright protected does not determine trademark availability or prevent consumer-confusion claims. [U.S. Copyright Office, “Games”](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html) · [USPTO guidance on why to search similar marks](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/why-search-similar-trademarks)

### Inferences

- Keep “Chu Shogi” as a plain, descriptive product/game label, pair it with the app's own distinct brand, and state that the product is independent of the Japanese and German associations. Do not reproduce logos or the associations' design assets.
- A common historical game name may be usable as a reference title, but it is not trademark clearance. Any distinctive logo, app brand, domain, store listing, or merchandise still warrants a jurisdiction-specific confusion search before public launch.

### Gaps

- I did not obtain and verify a result set for “CHU SHOGI,” “中将棋,” transliterations, or association names in the USPTO or J-PlatPat live search interfaces. The official portals are provided above; this review therefore makes no claim that the term is unregistered or risk-free.
- No searches were made of state, common-law, non-U.S. national, or regional trademark records. A full clearance would need the intended countries, services/classes, spelling variants, and final logo/domain.
