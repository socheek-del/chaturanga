# Chu Shogi rules edition and provenance

## Which edition is best supported?

### Takeaway

Use the **Japanese Chu Shogi Association (日本中将棋連盟 / Chu Shogi Renmei) 2019 competition rules**, with its 2004 clarifications, as the playable v1 ruleset. This is the clearest currently published, operational rules statement from an organization that still plays the game; it explicitly says its lion revisions follow the Edo-period *Chūshōgi Shinan-shō* and Matsuda’s *Chūshōgi Zenshū*, while adding a few present-day clarifications. Treat it as a modern association codification informed by historical sources, not as a claim that every fine point is proved to be exactly the Edo-period rule.

### Cited Findings

- The association's rules are titled “competition rules / general rules (2019 edition),” corrected 2019-09-14. They define the 12×12 board, 46 pieces per side, no drops, 21 initial piece types and 29 total types, and set out promotion, repetition, lion, and endgame provisions. [Japan Chu Shogi Association, 2019 general rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- The association says its lion-rule revision was made with reference to *Chūshōgi Shinan-shō* and the contemporary *Chūshōgi Zenshū*; the revision adopts the former's treatment as the basis and includes association clarifications. [Japan Chu Shogi Association, 2004 supplementary rules, section 3](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- The relevant historical rulebook is *Chūshōgi Shinan-shō* (中将棊指南抄), dated Genroku 16 (1703) in its closing imprint according to the association's source list. The National Diet Library catalogs its 1915 reprint in *Zatsugei Sōsho*, vol. 1, edited by Kokushokankōkai, as an internet-public, public-domain scan; the library's reference answer points to rules on print pp. 212–217 and problem material on pp. 218–232. [NDL digital scan and catalog record](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1869566); [NDL reference answer with page locators](https://crd.ndl.go.jp/reference/entry/reference/show?id=1000213532); [association's classical bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm)
- The association's transcript of the 1703 manual records the no-drops capture practice, promotion on entry/capture, lion protection, lion capture with an intervening non-pawn piece, the inability to use a pawn as that intervening piece, “igui” captures, hidden line protection, and the first-lion advantage. This is a period source as reprinted/transcribed, although it is not a modern exhaustive rules code. [Association transcription, *Chūshōgi Shinan-shō* excerpt](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/Okazaki.htm); [NDL 1915 reprint](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1869566)
- The 2004 association explanation acknowledges prior regional/Okazaki rules, describes why the association changed its rules toward the older *Shinan-shō* and *Zenshū* treatment, and records additional decisions made for contemporary play. That provenance makes the 2019 rules easier to audit than a generic English summary. [Association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- Lishogi links to the association's competition rules and offers online Chu Shogi, but its English summary simplifies or differs on details (notably lion counterstrike and repetition). It is evidence of a viable online rules implementation, not a better authority than the association's own code. [Lishogi Chu Shogi rules](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)
- George Hodges's *Middle Shogi Manual* is the leading English-language modern reference cited by Western players, but the primary manual is sold as a copyrighted publication rather than a freely accessible primary source. H.G. Muller's accessible comparison documents multiple promotion interpretations and finds the Japanese association rule least state-ambiguous for historic mating problems. [H.G. Muller, “The Chu Shogi promotion rule”](https://hgm.nubati.net/MSM/ChuPromoRule.html); [Dr Eric Silverman, Chu Shogi guide and source discussion](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/)
- The 1694 *Sho Shōgi Zushiki* / *Shōgi Zushiki* family is often cited for setup and moves, but publication dates and titles are inconsistently rendered in secondary references (1694 and 1696 both appear). I did not find a directly inspectable online copy of the specific 1694 edition in this pass, so the note below uses the NDL-accessible 1703 manual and the association's current code as the more traceable rule lineage. [Shōgi Zushiki bibliographic overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgi_Zushiki); [association's classical bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm)
- No comparable counts of players, games, or active memberships were located to establish which rules edition is most popular. The 2019 association code is selected for documentation quality and active organizational use; Lishogi demonstrates online availability but is not a population comparison. [Association rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Lishogi variant page](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)

### Inferences

- For a digital product, the best-supported interpretation is the 2019 association code plus its 2004 lion clarifications, and the source should be named in product documentation as “Chu Shogi Renmei rules (2019), based on *Shinan-shō* / *Zenshū*, with stated association clarifications.” This avoids presenting disputed modern conventions as unquestioned medieval facts. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)

### Gaps

- I did not verify every movement statement against the 1703 facsimile page by page, or locate an accessible critical edition comparing the 1703 text, the 1694/1696 *Zushiki* editions, and the 19th-century copies. The modern rule code is the safest playable selection, but historical certainty is lower for edge cases not explicit in the short historical text. [NDL catalog and scan](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1869566); [association's source bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm)

## Implementation map for the selected 12×12 rules

### Takeaway

This is a 46-piece-per-side, no-drop game with a four-rank promotion camp, many compound sliders, and three pieces with two-leg lion power. Implement the association's move/promotion and win rules as the default profile; add explicit unit-level rule examples for lion trades, promotion deferral, king/prince capture, and repetition because English summaries flatten those distinctions.

### Cited Findings

#### Movement and promotion map

- In this table, `forward` is toward the opponent; a “range” move slides through empty squares and stops at the first piece, capturing an enemy only by landing on it; a “step” moves one square; a “jump” ignores the intervening square. These are standard movement semantics in the English movement table and H.G. Muller's Betza reference; use the association's piece diagrams for its local names and categories. [Chu Shogi piece movement table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Individual_pieces); [H.G. Muller, Betza-coded piece table](https://hgm.nubati.net/rules/Chu.html); [association piece guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm)

  |---|---:|---|---|
  | King | 1 | Step one square in any of 8 directions | None |
  | Lion | 1 | Two consecutive King steps; may stop after the first, change direction between legs, capture on either leg, jump to a square within two squares, capture-and-return, or pass by going to an empty adjacent square and back | None |
  | Free King (often “Queen”) | 1 | Range any of 8 directions | None |

- Promoted ranks and movement: Prince moves as a King and is royal; Free Boar ranges sideways and diagonally; Flying Ox ranges forward/backward and diagonally; Flying Stag ranges forward/backward and steps one square in any direction; Whale ranges forward/backward and backward diagonals; White Horse ranges forward/backward and forward diagonals; Soaring Eagle ranges in all directions except forward diagonals and has two-leg lion power on forward diagonals; Horned Falcon ranges in all directions except straight forward and has two-leg lion power straight forward. Other promotions above reuse a rank already present initially. Red/reversed pieces do not promote a second time. [Association piece guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm); [English movement table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Individual_pieces); [association says the full game has 29 ranks and identifies piece classes](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- The English aliases “Free King” / “Queen,” “Dragon Horse,” “Dragon King,” “Reverse Chariot,” etc. vary across sources. Treat the Japanese rank and its movement as the identity; the “Tokin” label for a promoted pawn is a naming convention for the Gold-General movement, not a separate new movement. [German Chu Shogi Association terminology notes](https://www.chushogi.de/rules/chu_rules_pieces.htm); [piece and promotion table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Individual_pieces)

#### Promotion

- Promotion zone is the opponent's four farthest ranks. Under the association rule, a promotable piece gets an optional promotion when it moves from outside into that zone; if it declines, it may promote on a later move only by capturing (including capture while leaving the zone) or after leaving and re-entering the zone. The underlying 1703 text is also summarized as allowing promotion on entry and on capture in the zone, rather than unrestricted in-zone non-capture promotion. [Association 2019 rules, section 4](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association transcript of *Shinan-shō*](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/Okazaki.htm); [Lishogi's association-linked concise rule](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)
- Pawn has one extra optional promotion opportunity if it reaches the farthest rank unpromoted, or when it captures after declining promotion. If an unpromoted pawn or lance reaches the farthest rank without promoting, it is an immobile “dead piece.” In the association's rules, a lance does not receive the pawn's extra second chance in ordinary over-the-board play, but correspondence play explicitly extends that recovery to lances; for a digital product, use that stated communication-play extension and document it. [Association 2019 rules, section 4](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association explanation of the communication-only lance clarification](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- King, Free King, and Lion do not promote. Promotion is permanent and a promoted piece cannot promote again. [Association 2019 rules, section 4](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [German Chu Shogi Association piece table](https://www.chushogi.de/rules/chu_rules_pieces.htm)

#### Lion, lion power, and the “Lion Hawk” question

- Chu Shogi has the Lion, and Lion-power promotions called Horned Falcon (promoted Dragon Horse) and Soaring Eagle (promoted Dragon King). **It does not have a Lion Hawk.** “Lion Hawk” is a piece of Tenjiku Shogi, a different larger shogi variant; do not add it to Chu Shogi. [Association piece list and movement guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm); [H.G. Muller Chu piece list](https://hgm.nubati.net/rules/Chu.html); [Tenjiku Shogi piece discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tenjiku_shogi)
- Lion power: the Lion performs up to two King steps in a turn. It may continue after capturing on the first leg, may turn between legs, and may capture up to two pieces. It may also make an unobstructed two-square jump while ignoring an intermediate piece. Igui is a first-leg capture followed by a return to the starting square. Jitto is a non-capturing adjacent step followed by a return; it is a legal pass when at least one adjacent square is empty. The Horned Falcon has the two-leg/jump/igui/jitto form only on the forward orthogonal ray; the Soaring Eagle has it only on the two forward diagonal rays. [Association 2019 rules, section 8](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association piece guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm); [English move descriptions](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Lion)
- A Lion may capture an adjacent enemy Lion regardless of protection. A non-adjacent Lion capture is barred when the capturing Lion could be immediately recaptured; the association calls a normal defender “Lion's foot” and also recognizes a hidden/x-ray protector when a line piece could capture the Lion after its movement. The exception is “tsukegui/kuisoe”: on one Lion move, capture an eligible intervening piece and the enemy Lion. A Pawn or Go-Between is never eligible as the intervening piece. A Lion captured by a non-Lion piece triggers a one-turn counterstrike rule: if the capturing side's Lion is protected, the opponent may not immediately take that Lion with a non-Lion. The association says a Kirin that captures a Lion and promotes to a Lion receives the corresponding “first Lion” treatment. [Association 2019 rules, section 7](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association 2004 edge-case supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- For checking whether a Lion is protected, evaluate the post-capture board and include hidden line attacks; do not recursively apply Lion-trading legality to hypothetical recaptures. Historical descriptions explicitly mention hidden protection, while exact multi-Lion recursive cases are not described by the old text. [Association 2019 rules, section 7](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [English discussion of hidden protection and multi-Lion limitations](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Lion_moves)

#### Other engine and tutorial rules

- There is no Shogi-style “must keep the King out of attack” constraint: the essential objective is to capture the opposing royal piece(s), and players need not announce check. If the opponent has promoted a Drunk Elephant to Prince, both King and Prince are royal targets and both must be eliminated. The association's 2019 win clause also says checkmating or capturing the King wins, so the exact digital terminal timing deserves an explicit decision (see gaps). [Association 2019 rules, section 2](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Silverman guide](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/)
- The 2019 association rules treat a fourfold repeated position as requiring the responsible side to vary: continuous check makes the checker vary; if just one player attacks pieces in the cycle, that attacker varies; when both sides jitto, the first passer varies. Wikipedia's summary of the association's additional rule says a draw may be claimed when neither side attacks anything; use that as a provisional reading for a quiet cycle, and verify it against the full competition context. [Association 2019 rules, section 6](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Chu Shogi repetition summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Repetition)
- Association endgame rules include a “barren” material result: when only both Kings and one further eligible piece remain, King plus that piece beats a lone King; Pawn/Go-Between must safely promote first, and dead Pawn/Lance pieces do not count. When neither side can mate, players may agree to impasse/draw and replay with first/second roles reversed. The breadth and timing of “bare King” wins are not consistently described in English sources (see gaps). [Association 2019 rules, section 2](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- The playable tutorial should teach “capture the royal pieces” rather than importing Western chess check restrictions. It should separately teach the Drunk Elephant → Prince second royal, the option to delay promotion, Lion two-leg and igui, jitto pass, Lion foot/hidden foot, tsukegui, the no-drop capture rule, and the 4th-repetition responsibilities. These are the mechanics that distinguish the chosen association rules from standard shogi. [Association rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association piece guide](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/koma.htm); [Lishogi overview](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)

### Inferences

- The standard setup is implementation-ready from the coordinates, and the rule data can be represented as piece movement definitions plus two kinds of state: whether a piece remains eligible to promote after declining an entry opportunity, and a one-ply lion-capture event for the counterstrike rule. The association sources support both decisions. [H.G. Muller setup list](https://hgm.nubati.net/rules/Chu.html); [association promotion and lion rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- Use named in-product rules and examples for JCSA-specific exceptions instead of presenting only generic “lion rules”; a user needs to distinguish ordinary Lion, Lion-power promotions, adjacent-vs-nonadjacent Lion capture, a protected Lion, and tsukegui to make legal moves. [Association lion rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association lion supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)

### Gaps

- The Japanese association's concise win code names both “mate” and King capture, while the English sources often describe only capturing all royal pieces. Confirm whether the digital game should end on a formal no-escape mate state or only on actual royal capture; this affects bot and terminal logic. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Lishogi description](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)
- The 2004 association supplement expressly leaves the case where a Pawn or Go-Between is the only “foot” protecting a Lion and is captured during the same Lion move unresolved: one interpretation removes protection immediately, the other judges protection from the beginning of the whole turn, and the association follows the latter for now. Implement the association's stated “whole move” reading, but keep a regression position for this convention. [Association 2004 edge-case supplement, case 4](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- Several-Lion positions are recognized as difficult; the association gives decisions for selected examples, but the historical text does not establish a complete recursive precedence algorithm. Treat a Lion-capture restriction as based on the position after the contemplated capture and do not recursively legalize/ban hypothetical recaptures unless a source-backed case requires it. [Association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [English discussion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Lion_moves)
- Repetition handling differs in simplified implementations: Lishogi's English page summarizes fourfold repetition as a draw with a perpetual-attack exception, while the association's tournament code specifies which side must vary in several cases. Conformance should follow the association's full responsibilities and its quiet-cycle clause rather than that shortcut. [Lishogi repetition summary](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja); [association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm)
- Bare-King conventions are inconsistent: the association's current formal wording gives the barren endgame condition at both Kings plus one eligible piece; Silverman describes a broader “strip the opponent down to one royal” victory; H.G. Muller labels general extensions disputed. Use the precise association clause unless the owner selects another stated convention. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Silverman guide](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/); [H.G. Muller, Chu rule page](https://rules.moddable.games/shogi/variants/chu-shogi/)
- The 1703 source and later rulebooks differ in at least some edge interpretations, and the association itself notes that Go-Between promotion conventions changed from regional postwar practice. The historical book does not define every condition a software engine must decide, so any convention beyond its explicit language should be labeled as the selected current association clarification. [Association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [association historical bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm)

## Unresolved conventions and evidence limits

### Takeaway

Several disputed details are small in frequency but high-impact for engine correctness. The source-backed default should be JCSA 2019/2004, with the explicit interpretations below recorded as product conventions; the available evidence does not support claiming a single uncontested “historical” rule set in every edge position.

### Cited Findings

- **Promotion eligibility after a declined promotion differs among trusted sources.** JCSA permits non-capture promotion on entry, then requires a capture or leaving/re-entering before another attempt. H.G. Muller documents that the *Middle Shogi Manual*, Wikipedia, and JCSA differ; the first two permit later in-zone non-capture promotion under different state rules. He argues that JCSA avoids hidden eligibility state in historical mate problems. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [H.G. Muller promotion analysis](https://hgm.nubati.net/MSM/ChuPromoRule.html); [Wikipedia promotion summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Promotion)
- **Lion trading is a historically documented idea with modern formalization.** The 1703 text gives the basic “foot,” intervening capture, hidden line protector, and first-Lion advantage concepts; the association's 2004 amendments clarify adjacent Lions, precedence between tsukegui and first-Lion protection, and that only the association's own protected Lion triggers its counterstrike protection. Some compound capture cases remain expressly underdetermined. [1703 source transcription](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/Okazaki.htm); [association 2004 amendments and edge cases](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- **JCSA repetition is not merely shogi's fourfold rule.** Its rules distinguish continuous royal attack, other attack cycles, and back-and-forth jitto, assigning the duty to vary to a specific side. Wikipedia summarizes the quiet-cycle outcome as a claimable draw, while Lishogi's short English page gives a draw outside its described perpetual-attack case; treat these as secondary summaries and follow the full association code where it speaks clearly. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Wikipedia repetition summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chu_shogi#Repetition); [Lishogi summary](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)
- **Bare-King and terminal win language are not fully aligned across sources.** JCSA's formal code says mate or capture of the King and sets a specific barren-material rule; English guide material sometimes uses a broader bare-King rule, while other summaries say bare-King extensions are disputed. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Silverman guide](https://drericsilverman.com/2020/04/05/chu-shogi-part-i-how-to-play/); [H.G. Muller rule summary](https://rules.moddable.games/shogi/variants/chu-shogi/)
- **One old-source bibliography detail remains unsettled.** The association lists *Shō Shōgi Zushiki* / *Shōgi Zushiki* materials with dates around 1694–1696 and notes that later broad variant books are not always in agreement on piece powers; the directly accessible NDL item for this research is instead the 1915 reprint of the dated 1703 *Shinan-shō*. [Association bibliography](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/koramu/koramu-shiryou.htm); [NDL 1915 scan](https://dl.ndl.go.jp/pid/1869566); [bibliographic overview of Zushiki titles](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sh%C5%8Dgi_Zushiki)

### Inferences

- Product rules should be versioned as a named rules profile, e.g. `chu-shogi-renmei-2019` with the 2004 lion clarifications and the expressly permitted correspondence/online Lance extension. This makes later rule changes possible without silently changing saved games or tutorials. [Association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm)
- The first implementation should keep the controversial same-move protection case, forced-mate-vs-capture terminal trigger, baring threshold, and repeated-position outcome as explicit documented decisions and automated engine fixtures rather than burying them in move generation. [Association 2004 supplement](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule_hosoku2004.htm); [association 2019 rules](https://www.chushogi-renmei.com/kouza/rule2007.htm); [Lishogi summary](https://lishogi.org/variant/chushogi?lang=ja)

### Gaps

- No player-count, federation membership, or tournament dataset was found to support a numerical claim that the association, Western, or online rule edition is “most popular.”
- No rights-holder was contacted; this work only paraphrases public sources and supplies no protected prose or diagrams.
