# Dameo editions and popularity

## Does Dameo have documented variants?

### Takeaway

The Wikipedia chess-like-games list and Dameo’s inventor-presented rules treat Dameo as one game, created in 2000, with an 8×8 board and 18 men per player. A community server exposes experimental 10×10 board setups and an Anti-Dameo option, but labels these community/experimental choices; the sources do not establish them as historic editions or as comparably adopted forms.

### Cited Findings

- The Wikipedia list has one linked entry, “Dameo (2000),” without a variant or popularity note. The Dameo article describes the 8×8 game and does not identify a separate historic edition; the article itself carries a notice that it relies heavily on primary sources. [List of abstract strategy games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_abstract_strategy_games), [Dameo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dameo)
- Christian Freeling’s MindSports “About Dameo” says he encountered Croda in 2000 and that combining Croda’s forward/diagonal man movement and straight-only captures with his linear-movement idea produced Dameo. It describes the origin of one named game, not a family of editions. [About Dameo](https://mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/64-about-dameo)
- Freeling’s canonical rules page defines linear movement as advancing an unbroken line of same-color men by one square; it specifies forward man movement, queenwise kings, compulsory orthogonal captures, multiple captures, removal at the end of a sequence, and maximum capture. Its initial-position diagram is the standard setup. [MindSports rules](https://mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/65-rules)
- Abstract Play labels its default as “Default board” and offers “10x10 board (4 rows; experimental),” “10x10 board (3 rows; experimental),” and “Anti-Dameo Community.” This is evidence of community-designed options, not evidence that they are canonical or popular. [Abstract Play Dameo](https://play.abstractplay.com/games/dameo)
- BoardGameGeek’s Dameo page reports “Variants (0)” for its user-submitted variant section. This describes that site’s database, not all possible rule experiments. The page’s core listing identifies Dameo (2000) as an 8×8, two-player abstract game. [BoardGameGeek: Dameo](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/24698/dameo)
- The World Draughts Federation’s Draughts Atlas lists Dameo as a single entry among “inventive cousins” and describes it by the line-movement mechanic; its visible static profile does not identify a competing Dameo edition. [FMJD Draughts Atlas](https://www.fmjd.org/variants/)

### Inferences

- For a first digital product, “Dameo” should mean the canonical MindSports 8×8, 18-men-per-side game. The 10×10 arrangements and Anti-Dameo win condition should be treated as optional community experiments, outside the selected first version.
- The existence of an option in a platform’s variant selector is not evidence that users play it or that the inventor endorsed it.

### Gaps

- No source found that gives the history, rules adoption, or usage counts for Abstract Play’s experimental 10×10 setups or Anti-Dameo.
- No separate 8×8 Dameo rules edition or formal edition chronology was located.

## Is any edition demonstrated to be the most popular?

### Takeaway

No. The standard Dameo game has measurable online activity and a BoardGameGeek audience, but available statistics do not compare the canonical game with the community experiments. The evidence supports “most documented/default,” not “proven most popular.”

### Cited Findings

- The PlayStrategy Dameo library page describes live, correspondence, rated, and casual play and lists Dameo in its MSO online Grand Prix. When retrieved on 2026-11-25, the page showed 3,973 total games and 325 games in the previous month. These are platform-specific counts, and the page does not split play by experimental board or win-condition option. [PlayStrategy Dameo](https://playstrategy.org/library/dameo)
- BoardGameGeek’s page showed 83 ratings, 49 comments, and site ranks of 11,861 overall and 364 in Abstract at retrieval. These indicate a modest recorded board-game audience on BGG, not comparative preference among Dameo rule options. [BoardGameGeek: Dameo](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/24698/dameo)
- Freeling’s MindSports profile presents a single inventor-authored ruleset. His characterization of Dameo as the “most modern and best ‘sport weapon’” among draughts variants is explicitly his opinion, not a popularity measure. [About Dameo](https://mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/64-about-dameo)
- The Wikipedia list gives only a name and year for Dameo. It contains no popularity or variant-specific adoption evidence. [List of abstract strategy games](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_abstract_strategy_games)

### Inferences

- Dameo is actively played online, but the standard-game totals cannot be compared with experimental options because the platform does not report those options separately.
- No popularity winner can responsibly be selected among the standard and experimental forms. The standard is the evidence-backed default because the inventor’s rule page and independent catalogues describe it as the game.

### Gaps

- No public, variant-specific activity counts, tournament participation comparison, sales/ownership comparison, or survey of player preference was found.
- Online totals and BGG ratings do not establish global or historical popularity.

## Which form should the digital product select?

### Takeaway

Select the inventor’s standard 8×8 Dameo rules and explicitly avoid claiming it is statistically the most popular form. This is the only form supported as the canonical/default game across the inventor’s rules page and the major independent catalogs found.

### Cited Findings

- The creator’s rules page documents a single 8×8 initial position; 18 men per side; forward single-man and linear moves; queenwise king movement; compulsory orthogonal capture; compulsory continuation; maximum-number capture with men and kings equal; and end-of-sequence removal. [MindSports rules](https://mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/65-rules)
- BoardGameGeek describes the 2000 game as 8×8 with 18 pieces per player and lists online implementations, including Abstract Play, Boardspace, and igGameCenter. [BoardGameGeek: Dameo](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/24698/dameo)
- PlayStrategy’s named Dameo game uses all 64 squares and reports online activity for that library game; the page presents experimental community forms separately on its site. [PlayStrategy Dameo](https://playstrategy.org/library/dameo), [Abstract Play Dameo](https://play.abstractplay.com/games/dameo)

### Inferences

- Canonical 8×8 rules minimize unverified assumptions and give the product one stable rules target. Alternative board sizes and Anti-Dameo can be evaluated later if reliable usage evidence appears.
- In product planning, describe the choice as “the canonical, best-documented standard edition,” not “the most popular variant.”

### Gaps

- None of the reviewed sources establishes comparative popularity between the standard 8×8 game and the community experiments.
