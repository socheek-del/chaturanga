# Dameo rights boundary

## What copyright covers in a game

### Takeaway

The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes a game's uncopyrightable idea, title, and method of play from copyrightable expression such as a sufficiently original rules explanation or board illustration. That supports independently implementing the rules while writing new prose and drawing original interface and piece art; the U.S. guidance is not a worldwide legal clearance.

### Cited Findings

- The U.S. Copyright Office says a game idea, its name/title, and methods for playing it are not protected by copyright, while the written rules and graphic art on a board or box may be copyrightable if they contain sufficient expression. [U.S. Copyright Office, Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The Copyright Office's Compendium says claims in a game should be limited to the work's text, artwork, or photographs; it also states that copyright does not protect the underlying game idea, title, or procedure/method of play. [U.S. Copyright Office, Compendium, §714](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/redlines/chap700.pdf)
- Circular 33 explains that copyright protects only original expression used to describe or illustrate an idea or method, not the method itself. The circular also says that, generally, a page's format/layout is not copyrightable, while creative selection and arrangement may be. [U.S. Copyright Office, Circular 33](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ33.pdf)
- UK government guidance similarly distinguishes unprotected ideas from fixed expression and lists writing, photographs, illustrations and online content among copyrightable creative work. [Business.gov.uk, Using copyright to protect creative work](https://www.business.gov.uk/support/intellectual-property/using-copyright-protect-creative-work/)

### Inferences

- Independently implement the abstract move/capture procedures and board topology, but write the rules, tutorial and help text from scratch. Do not copy MindSports' explanations, diagrams, sample positions, or imagery.
- The historic 8×8 grid and starting arrangement are rules facts; a source's particular rendered board, decorative treatment, pieces, typography, and page design should be treated as separate expression and recreated originally.
- Copyright rules differ by jurisdiction. The cited U.S. and UK sources support a scoped engineering decision, not a conclusion that every use is lawful worldwide.

### Gaps

- No jurisdiction-specific legal opinion was obtained for the product's distribution countries.
- Copyright status of each individual MindSports diagram/image was not independently catalogued; avoid reusing all source assets regardless.

## Dameo creator, publication, and source notices

### Takeaway

MindSports' first-person history attributes Dameo's creation to Christian Freeling after he encountered Croda in 2000, and its official rules are published on MindSports. The accessible About page bears a `Dameo © MindSports` notice; no reuse license was located on the cited rules/about pages.

### Cited Findings

- MindSports' “How I invented … Dameo” history describes the creator encountering Croda in 2000 and assembling Dameo from it with linear movement; this is first-person creator testimony hosted on the rules publisher's site. [MindSports, Dameo history](https://www.mindsports.nl/index.php/how-i-invented-games-and-why-not/organicity?start=2)
- MindSports publishes the rules and an interactive play/AI version on its Dameo pages. [MindSports, Dameo rules](https://www.mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/65-rules); [MindSports, Dameo](https://www.mindsports.nl/index.php/on-the-evolution-of-draughts-variants/draughts-variants/509-dameo)
- The official About Dameo page says the game arose in 2000 and displays `Dameo © MindSports` followed by a site-wide © notice. [MindSports, About Dameo](https://www.mindsports.nl/index.php/arena/dameo/64-about-dameo)
- A 2018 survey by the World Draughts Federation identifies its Dameo section with Christian Freeling and summarizes the rules as a distinct 8×8, 18-men-per-side form with forward linear movement and maximum capture. [FMJD, A Guide to Checkers Families and Rules](https://www.fmjd.org/downloads/variants/Checkers_families_and_rules_28-9-2018.pdf)

### Inferences

- Attribute the game historically to Christian Freeling and cite MindSports as the rules source in internal research and any factual credits. Do not imply MindSports or Freeling endorsed this digital product.
- The © notice is evidence of an asserted rights notice on the site's material; it does not establish that the abstract rules are copyrightable, that MindSports owns every element, or that the notice grants permission to reuse page text/art.
- Since no explicit open license was found in the pages reviewed, treat the prose, diagrams, screenshots, logos and illustrations as unavailable for reuse unless separate permission is obtained.

### Gaps

- No explicit license or reuse permission for MindSports' Dameo page material was located; a wider site terms/licensing review was not completed.
- The sources identify Freeling as creator and MindSports as the publisher/site, but do not establish the ownership chain for every image or any contractual rights transfer.

## Title, brand, and clearance boundary

### Takeaway

Copyright exclusion for a game's title does not clear it for branding. USPTO guidance treats words and designs that identify a product source as trademarks and recommends clearance searching; no trademark clearance for the name `Dameo` was performed.

### Cited Findings

- USPTO defines a trademark as a word, phrase, symbol, design, or combination that identifies the source of goods or services, and explains that rights relate to use with particular goods/services rather than ownership of a word in the abstract. [USPTO, What is a trademark?](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/basics/what-trademark)
- USPTO says a federal trademark clearance search is important before applying and cautions that a complete clearance search involves more than one database. [USPTO, Federal trademark searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)
- USPTO's title-of-a-single-work guidance has separate trademark rules for creative-work titles and lists computer software and computer games among examples treated as a series of works for that policy. This reinforces that the copyright rule about titles should not be read as a trademark clearance. [USPTO, Title of a single creative work](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/laws/title-single-work-refusal-and-how-overcome-refusal)

### Inferences

- Use original product branding, logos, board/piece art, and visual styling; avoid MindSports marks and any wording that suggests sponsorship or affiliation.
- Before public promotion, search and assess relevant trademark registers and common-law use for `Dameo` and confusingly similar names in the intended goods/services and territories. If the product retains “Dameo” as the game identifier, separate that factual game reference from the product's own brand and get appropriate clearance for the actual presentation.

### Gaps

- This research did not run a comprehensive trademark clearance in the U.S., EU, UK, Thailand, or other markets, and it does not establish whether `Dameo` is available for software/game branding.
- This is a scoped research note, not legal advice or worldwide copyright/trademark clearance.
