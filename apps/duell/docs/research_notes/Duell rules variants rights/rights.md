# Duell rights boundary

## Who created/published the 1976 Duell and who may hold rights?

### Takeaway
The U.S. Duell product was published by Lakeside, while the game apparatus patent names Geoffrey Hayes as inventor and Toy Dev Ltd as assignee. The currently controlling rights holder for the game, product name, and package/rules artwork is unresolved; neither Lakeside's publisher role nor the patent record proves present ownership.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. patent for the dice-board apparatus identifies Geoffrey Hayes as inventor and Toy Dev Ltd as original/current assignee in the indexed record; its status is shown as expired, with Google expressly cautioning that status is an assumption rather than a legal conclusion. The grant describes the 9×8 board, numbered dice, master die, rolling movement, one change of direction, captures, and alternative board/piece forms. — [US Patent 3,929,337](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3929337A/en)
- The game entry associates Duell with Geoffrey Hayes and lists Lakeside among its publishers, while available commercial copies identify the 1976 Lakeside edition. These are catalog/retail records, not chain-of-title evidence. — [BoardGameGeek: Duell](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/2409/duell), [Noble Knight Games: Lakeside Duell](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2147993286/Duell)
- Hasbro's official corporate timeline says it acquired Tonka, including Kenner and Parker Brothers divisions, in 1991. It does not say Lakeside or the Duell rights transferred to Hasbro. — [Hasbro history timeline](https://cdn.hasbro.com/faaa3d5019832273570327fcdc971786a399f134/3f48867d684706bf14abe585a5714b83.pdf)

### Inferences
- The patent suggests the apparatus concept was once claimed by Toy Dev Ltd, but expiration of that patent does not establish the status or ownership of copyrights, trademarks, contracts, or other rights.
- Treat Toy Dev Ltd / Geoffrey Hayes, Lakeside / Leisure Dynamics, and Hasbro / Parker Brothers as leads for rights-holder investigation only. Do not represent any of them as the current rights holder without transfer/ownership evidence.

### Gaps
- No authoritative transfer record, current successor/owner for Toy Dev Ltd, or current owner of all Duell game materials was identified.
- The exact current status and owner of the historic U.S. DUELL mark were not confirmed in USPTO TSDR; a secondary trademark listing reports historical serial 73088833 filed by Leisure Dynamics, but that is insufficient for clearance. — [USPTO TSDR status service](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/)

## Does an existing license or permission authorize a digital game?

### Takeaway
No applicable digital-game license or permission was found. The public patent and rules materials are not a license for this project, and no relevant rights-holder permission was identified.

### Cited Findings
- The patent record documents an apparatus invention and marks the indexed patent as expired; it does not grant a software license or establish rights over separately protected materials. The legal status notice cautions that the status is not a legal conclusion. — [US Patent 3,929,337](https://patents.google.com/patent/US3929337A/en)
- Hasbro's digital-content guidelines apply subject to an executed license agreement and state that, beyond limited promotional apps, licensed property cannot be used in interactive/digital games absent express contractual rights. This is Hasbro policy for Hasbro-licensed property, not evidence that Hasbro owns Duell. — [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines)
- Hasbro's request form says a use is not authorized until Hasbro issues a consent letter and the requester signs and returns it. This route is relevant only if Hasbro confirms it controls the rights at issue. — [Hasbro Request Form](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/request-form)

### Inferences
- Because current ownership and permissions could not be verified, the repo requirement to establish a permission or applicable license before scheduling implementation is unmet.
- Do not rely on a decades-old patent's apparent expiration, open web access to rules scans, or public-domain-like assumptions as a substitute for a documented rights review.

### Gaps
- No rights holder was contacted; no license terms or digital adaptation permission were located.
- A trademark and chain-of-title search, including archival/corporate succession records for Toy Dev Ltd and Leisure Dynamics, remains outstanding.

## Which materials can be authored independently, and what is the implementation gate?

### Takeaway
The U.S. Copyright Office says game ideas and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while sufficiently expressive rules text and game-board/container art can be. This general U.S. guidance does not establish worldwide clearance or resolve patent, trademark, contract, or ownership questions; under the repository's explicit permission gate, do not implement Duell until rights are documented.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office states that the game idea, title, and play methods are not protected by copyright, while explanatory rules text and board/container graphic art can be copyrightable. — [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The Copyright Office says only a copyright owner may prepare or authorize a new version of a protected work; it also explains how to investigate historic copyright ownership and why notices/derivative editions can have separate rights. — [U.S. Copyright Office: Fair Use FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-fairuse.html), [Circular 22: How to Investigate Copyright Status](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ22.pdf)
- The Copyright Office provides public records collections for 1978-present and pre-1978 records, including the Virtual Card Catalog and the Catalog of Copyright Entries. A targeted search of those records was not completed in this work. — [Copyright Public Records Portal](https://www.copyright.gov/public-records/)

### Inferences
- If the rights gate is later cleared, author fresh rule explanations, tutorial examples, board/piece art, layout, and branding; do not reuse scanned instructions, diagrams, package art, or distinctive product trade dress.
- Before implementation, identify the rights holder(s) for the selected 1976 edition and obtain written permission expressly covering a free, public digital adaptation under GPL-3.0, or document an applicable license that covers the full project. Confirm name/mark clearance separately before public branding. Record territorial scope and any attribution/approval conditions.

### Gaps
- No global legal analysis was performed; copyrightability and terms vary by jurisdiction.
- No counsel-reviewed conclusion was reached about expired patent claims, historic trademarks, abandoned marks, or any present use in commerce.
- The project owner has not supplied written permission or a license.
