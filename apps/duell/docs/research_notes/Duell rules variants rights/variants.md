# Duell editions and popularity

## Which game and edition does the roadmap mean?

### Takeaway

The entry is Geoffrey Hayes’s dice-as-pieces abstract game, first reviewed in 1976 as **Conquest** and sold under several regional names. For the roadmap’s “standard 1976 edition,” use the Lakeside U.S. **Duell** edition and its 8×9 classic rules. Later **Tactix** is a related reissue with a distinct 9×9 variant; it should not replace the requested edition. There is not enough evidence to call any version most popular.

### Cited Findings

- David Parlett’s retrospective identifies Geoffrey Hayes as inventor of *Conquest*, published by Denys Fisher Games; Parlett says his review appeared in *Games & Puzzles* no. 48, May 1976, and describes an 8×9 board, eight ordinary dice plus a key die per player, tumbling movement by the initial top face, at most one right-angle turn, and king capture or occupation of the key square as wins — [The Incompleat Gamester: Conquest](https://www.parlettgames.uk/gamester/conquest.html).
- A surviving Parker German-language instruction scan is branded Parker and gives the same core movement and objective. It dates this Parker edition to 1975 in the filename/collection description, while the game’s contemporary review predates the Lakeside 1976 release — [Parker Duell instructions (scan)](https://www.spiele4us.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/duell-von-parker-1975-gebraucht-g003201720.pdf).
- A specialist retailer’s catalog identifies the U.S. Lakeside boxed edition as *Duell*, published in 1976, for two players — [Noble Knight Games: Duell](https://www.nobleknight.com/P/2147993286/Duell). This supports the roadmap’s edition label, though it is a modern resale catalog, not the original publisher’s catalog.
- Parlett lists regional/reissue names including *The George vs. Mildred Dice Game*, *Alea*, *Tactix*, and German *Duell*. A modern community catalog also lists *Conquest*, *Genius Attack*, *Tactix*, and other aliases; these names should be treated as catalog clues, not proof each edition has identical rules — [Parlett](https://www.parlettgames.uk/gamester/conquest.html), [BoardGameGeek: Duell](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/2409/duell).
- The later Schmidt Spiele *Tactix* appeared in 1998/99 and uses a 9×9 board. A review distinguishes the 8×9 “Duell” rules from 9×9 “Tactix”; it also reports a rule difference: a king cannot capture in Duell but can in Tactix — [LUDING: Duell der Würfel: Tactix](https://www.luding.org/reviews/bpk/Tactix.html), [Schmidt Spiele Tactix listing](https://www.spiele4us.de/spiel/tactix/).
- The roadmap-requested 1976 target is closest to the classic 8×9 Lakeside edition. A 1976 magazine review covers the contemporary UK *Conquest*, rather than the later 9×9 *Tactix* — [Games & Puzzles, May 1976 no. 48](https://archive.org/details/sim_games-and-puzzles_1976-05_48).

### Inferences

- The U.S. 1976 Lakeside *Duell* and the 1976-reviewed *Conquest* appear to be regional editions of one core 8×9 rules family, while *Tactix* is a later, modified derivative. The 1976 Lakeside choice follows the roadmap’s explicit year/edition instruction; it is not an evidence-based popularity ranking.
- A SourceForge page reproducing a 2010 hobbyist’s history says Lakeside’s English rules let players choose who starts, unlike the Parker version’s fixed red start. Treat this as a possible edition-level convention to verify against the Lakeside leaflet, not as established primary evidence — [Duell SourceForge files/history](https://sourceforge.net/projects/duell/files/).

### Gaps

- No original Lakeside publisher archive/catalog or complete Lakeside English rule leaflet was located in this research pass. The precise 1976 Lakeside setup diagram and first-player convention should be verified before engine fixtures.
- The title “Duell” appears in unrelated later games, including a 2004 fencing card game; use the creator and dice-grid mechanics to disambiguate it from the roadmap’s 1976 entry.

## What does adoption evidence establish?

### Takeaway

The evidence establishes that the game circulated internationally, was reissued, and retained a small enthusiast following. It does not establish a most-popular title, region, or rule version. Use the specified 1976 Lakeside form as the best-documented roadmap target, and describe it that way rather than calling it most popular.

### Cited Findings

- Parlett says the game was rebranded and appeared in different countries under multiple titles; he characterizes its subsequent publishing history as piecemeal — [The Incompleat Gamester: Conquest](https://www.parlettgames.uk/gamester/conquest.html).
- In its Tactix review, LUDING says the earlier abstract dice game did not achieve great popularity but had a persistent following; this is qualitative reviewer judgment, not a sales or player-count measure — [LUDING: Duell der Würfel: Tactix](https://www.luding.org/reviews/bpk/Tactix.html).
- BoardGameGeek’s live catalog page showed 306 ratings when checked on 2026-11-26. This measures activity on one hobby website; it is not a population estimate, and the page groups multiple editions and aliases — [BoardGameGeek: Duell](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/2409/duell/ratings).
- The 1976 *Games & Puzzles* review and subsequent localized releases demonstrate publication and critical attention, but provide no comparative sales data — [Games & Puzzles, May 1976 no. 48](https://archive.org/details/sim_games-and-puzzles_1976-05_48), [Parlett](https://www.parlettgames.uk/gamester/conquest.html).

### Inferences

- The broadest defensible claim is “the 1976 Lakeside edition is the roadmap-selected, well-documented standard form.” Available sources do not justify “most popular.”
- The existence of a later 9×9 *Tactix* reissue shows a designer/publisher revision and continued availability, not that its rules superseded or outperformed the 8×9 rules.

### Gaps

- No print-run, sales, tournament participation, club membership, or cross-edition play-count data were found.
- BGG ratings and listings are self-selected, edition-aggregated, and unsuitable for ranking historical popularity.
