# Game of the Generals rules, adoption and rights

**Research date:** 2026-11-26  
**Decision:** Block implementation pending an applicable digital-adaptation license or written permission. No contact has been made.

## Selected rules and adoption

Use the original hidden-rank game for two opposing players and a neutral arbiter. The official organization
rules specify an 8×9 board, 21 pieces per army arranged in the nearest three ranks, and six initially empty
squares in that deployment area. Every piece moves one square orthogonally. A challenge moves onto an enemy
piece's square: rank order removes the lower piece and the winner occupies that square; equal ranks remove
both. The Spy has its special rank interaction with all pieces except the Private. The Flag wins if captured;
reaching the opponent's back row also wins after it survives the required response. The official rules phrase
this as surviving one more turn, with no adjacent enemy able to challenge it. A digital product would need
separate per-seat views so an opponent cannot inspect hidden ranks, and a server-authoritative arbiter so
challenge results cannot be forged. Verify every rank interaction against the official table before any
future engine work.

The creator's first-person history says he conceived the game in 1967 and it was publicly introduced in 1972
(its first newspaper release was October 1972). The product-page summary calls it a 1970 game. Use the
creator's first-person chronology for provenance: conceived 1967, released publicly 1972. The history says it
had been played by millions and organized in thousands of clubs by the time it was written, but no comparable
adoption data distinguish a rules variant or physical set edition. This is the original rules target, not a
popularity claim among variants.

Before any future engine work, reconcile the official rules for the full rank inventory, rank precedence,
piece movement exceptions, challenge outcomes, Flag capture and back-rank arrival. Do not copy a current
digital adaptation's code, artwork, rules text or interface.

## Rights review

The creator and current product/distribution sources indicate a modern authored and commercially published
title. A distributor page says Pasola held the patent, but this research did not establish the patent record,
its dates/jurisdiction, current ownership of other rights, or a public digital-adaptation license. Henerals
describes itself as an independent implementation and explicitly does not claim endorsement. Its existence
does not grant permission to this project. Under the repository gate, implementation waits for applicable
permission or a license. No contact was made; this is a project gate rather than a legal conclusion.
