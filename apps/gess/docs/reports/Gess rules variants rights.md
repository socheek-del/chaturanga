# Trace Gess’s Standard Rules and Rights

The strongest documented baseline for Chaturanga is the **1994 Archimedeans Gess rules**, but the available evidence does not show that this edition is the most popular. It is played on an 18×18 board with 43 stones per side; players move changing 3×3 formations, and victory depends on preserving a ring pattern. Several end conditions and one setup diagram still need a deliberate digital ruling or source transcription. More decisively, **no license or permission for a digital adaptation was found**, so implementation must wait for written rights confirmation or an applicable license under this repository’s requirements. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [1994 *Scientific American* article](https://wkbpic.com/wkbx/SA/1994/199411.pdf))

## The 1994 Archimedeans rules are the clearest baseline

Paul Bolchover’s repost identifies the rules as “Gess the Game,” published in *Eureka* 53 in 1994 and attributed to the Archimedeans’ Puzzles and Games Ring. Ian Stewart’s contemporaneous *Scientific American* article independently describes the game and shows the opening formation. This makes the Archimedeans rules the best-documented selection for the roadmap, rather than a proven popularity winner. The 18×18 playable grid and an SGF 20×20 diagram with an unplayable border are equivalent board encodings, not separate rule variants. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [*Scientific American*, November 1994](https://wkbpic.com/wkbx/SA/1994/199411.pdf), [SGF Gess specification](https://red-bean.com/sgf/gess.html))

The published setup uses **43 stones of each color**: six single-stone pawns and six back-row formations arranged as rook, bishop, queen, ring, bishop, rook from each player’s left to right; Black moves first. The primary repost omits its referenced diagrams, so the exact starting cells must be transcribed from the original *Eureka* material or checked carefully against the contemporary scan before a setup fixture is created. The available evidence does not establish comparable sales, tournament, club, or player-count data for competing editions. BoardGameGeek’s 22 ratings and 18 comments, checked on 2026-11-26, and a documented 1995 freeware implementation show some dissemination, not a reliable measure of global adoption. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [*Scientific American* scan](https://wkbpic.com/wkbx/SA/1994/199411.pdf), [BoardGameGeek Gess entry](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/12862/gess), [1995 Atari freeware listing](https://www.atariuptodate.de/en/12786/gess))

## Formation footprints slide, collide, and can leave the board

A formation is any 3×3 footprint containing at least one stone of the player to move and no opposing stones. Its eight outer cells enable movement directions; the center determines maximum range, which is unlimited when occupied and capped at three cells when empty. The formation moves as a whole, may stop before an obstruction, and cannot pass through stones. When its first translated position overlaps other stones, it stops and removes all stones in that overlap, regardless of color. Footprints may start or finish partly off-board, but not wholly outside; stones carried beyond the edge disappear. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [*Scientific American* scan](https://wkbpic.com/wkbx/SA/1994/199411.pdf))

A ring is a same-color 3×3 pattern with all eight outer cells occupied and an empty center. It is a board pattern rather than a persistent king piece; a player may have multiple rings. At the end of a move, a player with no ring loses. If a move destroys both players’ last rings, the mover loses because the rules check the mover first. Bolchover’s definition also permits a center-only footprint: it is valid but has no enabled direction. Wikipedia’s summary adds an outer-stone requirement, which conflicts with this primary-source example; a digital rules contract should follow the author-posted rule and document the discrepancy. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [Wikipedia Gess summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gess))

The rules do not define what happens when a player has no legal move or specify repetition or long-game draws. These cases need explicit digital conventions before engine implementation and must appear in the tutorial if they affect play. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss))

## No existing permission covers a digital adaptation

The 1995 repost says the editor and Archimedeans allowed the author to repost the article. That is narrow permission for publication of the text, not a grant to create a faithful digital game. A secondary rules reference displays a 1994 copyright notice for the Archimedeans’ Puzzles and Games Ring, but the current rights holder was not verified. Looney Labs’ electronic-games policy does not govern Gess: the historical source attributes the game to the Archimedeans, not Looney Labs. ([Bolchover’s rules repost](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss), [Games of Soldiers: Gess](https://www.di.fc.ul.pt/~jpn/gv/gess.htm), [Looney Labs electronic-games policy](https://www.looneylabs.com/looney-labs-electronic-arcade))

General U.S. guidance distinguishes game procedures from expressive materials: the U.S. Copyright Office says methods of play are not protected by copyright while rules text and board art may be; U.S. law likewise excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, and methods of operation from copyright protection. That guidance does not establish a license for this project or resolve rights in other jurisdictions. If permission is obtained, all rules prose, tutorial examples, board and piece art, branding, and interface should still be independently authored, with trademark clearance before public branding. ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html), [17 U.S.C. §102](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html), [Archimedeans publications and editor contact](https://archim.org.uk/publications/))

## Conclusion

Use the 1994 Archimedeans rules as the candidate standard because they are the clearest surviving source, and describe them as best documented rather than most popular. Record the setup diagram, center-only footprint conflict, and missing no-move/repetition outcomes before any rules engine is designed. **Gess remains blocked from implementation** until the Archimedeans confirm who controls the relevant rights and provide written permission for this free GPL-3.0 digital adaptation, or an applicable license is established; no request has been sent and no permission has been received.
