# Rights boundary

## Does an existing license authorize a faithful digital Gess adaptation?

### Takeaway
No permission or license for a digital Gess adaptation was found. Gess is attributed to the Puzzles and Games Ring of the Archimedeans, not Looney Labs; the old Usenet repost's permission was only permission to repost the article text. Under the repository's rule requiring permission or an applicable license before scheduling a faithful digital implementation, treat implementation as blocked until rights are confirmed.

### Cited Findings
- The original rules article was published as “Gess the Game” in *Eureka* issue 53, the journal of the Archimedeans, and the reposter explicitly thanked the *Eureka* editor and the Archimedeans for permission to post that message. This is narrow reposting permission and does not state an adaptation or online-game license. [Historical rules post](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss)
- A rules reference attributes Gess to the Puzzles and Games Ring of the Archimedeans and displays “Copyright (c) 1994 Puzzles and Games Ring of The Archimedeans.” It is hosted by a third-party university site, so this is evidence of an asserted rights notice, not proof of the present owner or a license. [Games of Soldiers: Gess](https://www.di.fc.ul.pt/~jpn/gv/gess.htm)
- The Archimedeans describe *Eureka* as their journal and provide an archive; the same page directs inquiries about articles to the *Eureka* editor at `archim-eureka-editor@srcf.net`. [The Archimedeans: Publications](https://archim.org.uk/publications/)
- Looney Labs' electronic-games policy asks for permission before a freeware electronic version of one of its own games is made available online. But Gess's source attribution points to the Archimedeans, not Looney Labs, so that policy is not a license or a permission channel for Gess. [Looney Labs Electronic Arcade policy](https://www.looneylabs.com/looney-labs-electronic-arcade); [historical Gess rules post](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss)

### Inferences
- Send a written permission request to the Archimedeans (initially through the current *Eureka* editor) asking whether they control the relevant rights and whether they authorize an independently authored, free, GPL-3.0 digital implementation, including tutorials, bot play, and online play. Do not infer authorization from free access to the article or from a permission granted to another person to repost it.
- If the society says the rights belong to named authors or another party, identify and obtain permission from that rights holder before engine implementation. No permission request has been sent as part of this research.

### Gaps
- The current owner of any rights in the 1994 article or game material, and whether authors assigned rights to the society, were not established from accessible records.
- No public license granting digital adaptation rights was located, and there is no response from a rights holder authorizing this project.

## Which materials should be independently authored?

### Takeaway
Even where the functional rules can be independently implemented, the 1994 article's wording, diagrams, illustrations, and page design should not be copied. Create original tutorial prose, diagrams, board/piece rendering, UI, sounds, and branding; document factual mechanics in fresh language.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office says the idea for a game, its title, and methods of play are not protected by copyright; it also says rules text and board/container graphic art may be copyrightable. [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- U.S. Copyright Act §102(b) excludes ideas, procedures, processes, systems, and methods of operation from copyright protection, while §102(a) recognizes literary and pictorial/graphic works as copyrightable subject matter. [17 U.S.C. §102](https://www.copyright.gov/title17/92chap1.html)
- UK government guidance similarly says copyright protects expression rather than the idea behind a work, and specifically gives manual text and illustrations as protected expression. [GOV.UK: Knowledge assets classes and types](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/knowledge-asset-management-in-government/knowledge-assets-classes-and-types-annex-a)
- The original *Eureka* text was reproduced by a 1995 poster with express permission from the editor and society; this shows the online copy had permission for that posting, not blanket reuse rights for its text or diagrams. [Historical rules post](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss)

### Inferences
- Describe the game behavior from verified facts in new words and generate all tutorial examples and board diagrams from original app assets. Avoid scans, screenshots, tracing, distinctive graphic layouts, copied rulebook prose, and Archimedeans/*Eureka* marks.
- The sources summarize U.S. and UK copyright principles, not a jurisdiction-by-jurisdiction opinion. They do not determine whether a particular digital presentation, diagram, or close textual paraphrase infringes.

### Gaps
- No complete license or rights statement for *Eureka* issue 53 or the Gess article was found in the currently accessible archive information.
- No jurisdiction-specific legal review of the proposed board depiction or adaptation has been performed.

## Is “Gess” cleared for the product name and public branding?

### Takeaway
No trademark clearance was completed. The U.S. Copyright Office says titles are not protected by copyright, but that does not resolve trademark or passing-off questions. Run a relevant trademark clearance search and get advice before public product branding; use original art and avoid implying endorsement.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office states the name or title of a game is not protected by copyright. [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The USPTO advises searching exact wording and variants, then considering whether marks and the associated goods/services could create a likelihood of confusion; it warns that a database search alone cannot guarantee registration. [USPTO: Federal trademark searching](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/search/federal-trademark-searching)
- The accessible Gess sources identify “Gess” as the game name and associate the original rules with the Archimedeans; they do not establish trademark ownership, registration status, or a right to brand a public PWA with that name. [Historical rules post](https://groups.google.com/g/rec.games.abstract/c/tuSTCVg__Ss); [Games of Soldiers: Gess](https://www.di.fc.ul.pt/~jpn/gv/gess.htm)

### Inferences
- Public branding should remain a release gate until a search in relevant markets and classes is reviewed. A descriptive source credit may be appropriate after rights review, but it should not suggest sponsorship or endorsement.

### Gaps
- No complete U.S., UK, EU, or worldwide clearance search for “Gess” and confusingly similar marks was performed.
- No trademark owner or permitted branding terms for the game were identified.
