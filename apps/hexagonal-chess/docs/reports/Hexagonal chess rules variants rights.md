# Choose Gliński’s 91-Cell Hexagonal Chess Rules

Gliński’s **91-cell hexagonal chess** is the best-supported choice for this catalog: specialist and academic sources identify it as the best-known and historically most widely played form, though no reliable current popularity ranking exists. Its regular three-colour hex board, three bishops per player, unusual pawn rules, and ¾–¼ stalemate score distinguish it from other hexagonal chess games. The accessible rules literature supports the main movement system, but the original setup diagram still needs a coordinate-by-coordinate transcription, and en passant needs confirmation against the 1973 rulebook. The sources also do not establish Gliński’s complete draw rules. Most importantly, no applicable digital-adaptation license or current rights holder was verified, so implementation is blocked under the repository’s rights gate pending clearance and a separate name review. ## Historical evidence favors Gliński’s form over neighboring variants

Pritchard calls Gliński’s game the best known and probably most widely played hexagonal chess, and notes that the 91-cell board was used more often than other hex-board sizes. A 2019 University of South Wales thesis independently calls it the most common and most popular form in the family. Federation histories add evidence of editions, British and European organizations, and championship activity. Together, these sources support selecting Gliński’s rules as the catalog default and describing them as historically prominent; they do not establish a current player-count ranking or audited popularity total. ([Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf), [Harris, 2019 thesis](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/files/30748392/LHarris_Thesis_Main.pdf), [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf))

McCooey is a distinct ruleset, not an alternate setup to mix into Gliński’s game. It shares a similar 91-cell board but starts with seven rather than nine pawns per side and changes pawn capture and opening movement. Other hexagonal chess forms use different board shapes or sizes. Keep the selected product explicitly tied to Gliński’s 1973 rules, and avoid using McCooey’s pawn rules or setup. ([Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf), [Harris, 2019 thesis](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/files/30748392/LHarris_Thesis_Main.pdf))

## The rules combine six-direction movement with special pawn and stalemate rules

Gliński’s board is a regular hexagon of **91 cells**, six cells along each side, divided into three colours. Each player has 18 pieces: an ordinary chess army plus one bishop and one pawn, for three bishops and nine pawns. The 1973 rulebook’s catalog record and the historical federation review identify Gliński’s book as the formal rule source, while Pritchard supplies the accessible detailed movement account. ([WorldCat, 1973 rulebook](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Rules-of-hexagonal-chess-with-examples-of-first-openings/oclc/1094334), [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf), [Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf))

Rooks travel along the six lines parallel to the board edges; bishops follow the six lines parallel to its vertices and stay on their colour; queens combine those paths. The king moves one cell in up to 12 directions, and knights leap in two parts over intervening pieces. Pawns advance along their file, capture on either forward oblique, can move two cells from their starting cell, and promote on the opponent’s back line. Gliński’s game has no castling. Pritchard also says a pawn that captures inward onto another pawn’s initial cell retains its two-cell option because it has not effectively advanced. ([Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf))

The distinctive ending rule is **¾ point for the player who delivers stalemate and ¼ for the stalemated player** in match play. Some modern platforms instead treat stalemate as a draw; that is a platform choice, not evidence of a rule change. Pritchard’s account supports pawn double-step behavior, while later detailed summaries and Ludii’s rules model include en passant; the original 1973 wording must be checked before en passant is fixed as part of the selected edition. ([Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf), [Ludii, “Glinski Chess”](https://ludii.games/details.php?keyword=Glinski+Chess), [Green Chess, Gliński rules](https://www.greenchess.net/en/rules.php?v=glinski))

## The starting array and remaining draw rules need verification

The exact starting cells should be transcribed from the original Gliński diagram and independently checked before the engine fixture is written. The detailed summary’s file coordinates can help orient a digital board, but they are not a substitute for the 1973 diagram. The original rulebook is cataloged as a 1973 London publication, and the English Chess Federation describes it as Gliński’s official rulebook; the searchable sources reviewed here do not provide a dependable complete coordinate list. ([WorldCat, 1973 rulebook](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Rules-of-hexagonal-chess-with-examples-of-first-openings/oclc/1094334), [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf), [Wikipedia, “Hexagonal chess”](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hexagonal_chess))

Modern pages conflict about whether pawns have a two-cell opening move and en passant: some omit both, while Pritchard’s Gliński account supports the double step and the detailed summaries model en passant. Treat those pages as simplified or alternate conventions unless the original rulebook confirms otherwise. Pritchard documents stalemate scoring, but the consulted sources do not settle repetition, move-count, insufficient-material, or agreed-draw rules. Do not silently import the full orthodox-chess draw menu. ([Pritchard and Beasley, chapter 22](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf), [Moddable Gliński rules](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/glinski/), [Plyyard Hexchess rules](https://hexchess.plyyard.com/rules/), [Ludii, “Glinski Chess”](https://ludii.games/details.php?keyword=Glinski+Chess))
