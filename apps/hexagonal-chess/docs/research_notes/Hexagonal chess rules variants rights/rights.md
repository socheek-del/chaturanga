# Gliński Hexagonal Chess rights

## Authorship and publication

### Takeaway

The best-documented formal rules were authored by Władysław Gliński and published by Hexagonal Chess Publications in 1973. The current owner of those text and illustration rights, the present status of the publisher, and any successor organization were not established in the sources reviewed. No source located grants a digital adaptation license.

### Cited Findings
- WorldCat records *Rules of Hexagonal Chess with Examples of First Openings* as a 1973 English-language book by Władysław Gliński, published in London by Hexagonal Chess Publications. [WorldCat catalogue record](https://search.worldcat.org/title/Rules-of-hexagonal-chess-with-examples-of-first-openings/oclc/1094334)
- A Polish technical-history article reports that Gliński and William Edmunds established Hexagonal Chess Publications in 1973 and that the rules book had seven English and French editions by 1977. This is secondary history, not a record of the company’s current legal status or any transfer of rights. [Młody Technik: Gliński’s hexagonal chess](https://mlodytechnik.pl/eksperymenty-i-zadania-szkolne/szachy/30503-szachy-heksagonalne-glinskiego)
- A 2001 specialist article says Gliński died in February 1990, that the rules/theory books were out of print, and that the International Hexagonal Chess Federation (IHCF) had been formed in 1980 and reorganized in 1996. It does not identify a copyright successor or offer a digital-use license. [Abstract Games, Issue 7](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_7.pdf)
- The English Chess Federation’s 2024 historical feature describes the 1982 *Growth of Hexagonal Chess* as an IHCF publication and the 1973 Gliński book as his official rulebook. It is historical reporting, not an adaptation grant. [English Chess Federation, ChessMoves October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf)

### Inferences
- The 1973 book is a protected authored publication unless its rights term has expired or a relevant exception/license applies; its being out of print or available second-hand does not itself grant reuse rights.
- Neither the publisher’s historic existence nor IHCF’s historic activity establishes who currently owns the book, diagrams, or any name rights.

### Gaps
- No assignment, estate, successor-publisher, current IHCF permission page, written adaptation permission, or game-specific open license was located. This does not prove that none exists.
- A death date appears in secondary sources; no primary biographical or estate record establishing the rights successor was located.

## Digital adaptation, copyright, patents, and branding

### Takeaway

No source reviewed supplies an applicable license or written permission for a faithful digital adaptation. UK official guidance distinguishes ideas from their expression, but the original rules book, diagrams, examples, and artwork may carry separate rights; that distinction is not a worldwide rights clearance. Under this repository’s `AGENTS.md`, implementation should remain blocked until permission or an applicable license covering the project is established, with a separate name/mark review before public branding.

### Cited Findings
- UK IPO guidance states that copyright arises automatically for original literary and artistic works and that copyright owners control acts including adaptation and putting protected work on the internet. [GOV.UK: How copyright protects your work](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/overview)
- UK business/IP guidance states that copyright protects expression rather than the underlying idea. This supports independently implementing mechanics and authoring new text/art as a possible route, but does not decide whether a particular faithful game adaptation is lawful in every relevant jurisdiction. [Business.gov.uk: Using copyright to protect creative work](https://www.business.gov.uk/support/intellectual-property/using-copyright-protect-creative-work/)
- UK copyright terms generally last until 70 years after an identified author’s death for literary, dramatic, musical, or artistic work, subject to exceptions. If Gliński’s reported 1990 death date is correct and the standard term applies to his book, its text and artwork would not reach that term end until 31 December 2060. This is conditional, not a determination of the work’s status, ownership, or applicable law elsewhere. [Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, section 12](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/section/12); [Abstract Games, Issue 7](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_7.pdf)
- UK IPO says a UK patent can last up to 20 years from filing, with required renewals. Secondary sources report historic Gliński patents, but this research did not verify a Gliński-specific patent record, its claims, or its status in an official patent register. No conclusion about patent expiry or scope is used here. [UK IPO: Patent formalities](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/formalities-manual-online-version/chapter-1-patents-and-formalities); [GOV.UK: search for a patent](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-patent)
- UK government guidance provides a public search for registered trademarks, and the Trade Marks Act defines words and personal names as signs capable of being trademarks. No targeted trademark-register clearance was completed in this task; “Gliński” or related game branding is not cleared. [GOV.UK: Search for a trade mark](https://www.gov.uk/search-for-trademark); [Trade Marks Act 1994, section 1](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1994/26/body/made/data.htm)

### Inferences
- The lack of a found digital license, the unknown current rights holder, and the repository’s required rights gate make this a no-go for engine and product implementation until clearance or an applicable license is documented.
- Even after a mechanics-only legal basis is established, the project should write its own rules/tutorial text and create original board, piece, and promotional artwork. Public use of the game name should wait for trademark/name review.
- A reported historic patent, even if an official register later confirms it expired, would not by itself establish copyright permission, ownership of the book’s expression, or name clearance.

### Gaps
- No present-day rights-holder contact or current IHCF licensing channel was verified; no one was contacted.
- No jurisdiction-wide legal analysis was performed. UK and other countries may treat relevant rights and exceptions differently; the official UK IPO says its general guidance is not a substitute for legal advice.
- The UK and international trademark registers were not searched for the game name, author’s name, or related marks.
