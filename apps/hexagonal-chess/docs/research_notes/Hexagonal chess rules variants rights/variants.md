# Gliński Hexagonal Chess: Variant and Adoption Evidence

## Is Gliński’s 91-cell game the best-known version?

### Takeaway

Yes. The Gliński form is the strongest catalog default: a specialist chess-variant encyclopedia calls it the best known and probably the most widely played, and says its 91-cell board has been used more often than other hexagonal chess boards. A 2019 University of South Wales PhD thesis likewise calls it the most common and most popular hexagonal chess variant. These are well-supported expert descriptions, but neither is a representative present-day participation survey.

### Cited Findings

- David B. Pritchard’s chapter on hexagonal boards says Gliński’s game is the best known and probably most widely played; it also says the 91-cell board had been used more often than other hex-board sizes. John Beasley hosts the out-of-print *Classified Encyclopedia of Chess Variants* with the agreement of Pritchard’s daughter. — [Pritchard, *The Classified Encyclopedia of Chess Variants*, chapter 22 (2007)](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf); [Beasley’s encyclopedia note](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc.htm)
- The thesis *Combinatorial Problems on the Chessboard* states that Gliński’s is the most common hex-board chess game and “the most popular” of these variants, while describing the shared board and movement foundations of other forms. — [Liam Harry Harris, University of South Wales PhD thesis (2019), chapter 4](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/files/30748392/LHarris_Thesis_Main.pdf)
- An English Chess Federation magazine article based on the IHCF’s 1982 *Growth of Hexagonal Chess* booklet describes Gliński’s 91-cell, three-colour board, three bishops per side, the 1973 official rulebook, European growth, British federation and championship formation, and later tournaments. — [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf)

### Inferences

- The catalog can select Gliński’s rules as the best-known documented form with stronger evidence than a bare Wikipedia claim: Pritchard compares the hexagonal family directly and Harris’s academic thesis independently uses a superlative.
- “Best-known” and historical “most widely played” are defensible descriptions. Avoid saying it is currently the most popular, since the sources do not provide current comparative counts.

### Gaps

- No representative current player survey or current comparative sales data for Gliński, McCooey, Shafran, and other hex variants was found.
- Pritchard’s reported player and sales figures are explicitly secondhand estimates; their underlying survey or sales records were not independently located.

## How does Gliński’s game differ from neighboring hex-chess versions?

### Takeaway

“Hexagonal chess” names a family of games. Gliński is the common reference point, while McCooey is a separate variant that deliberately changes the setup and pawns on the same 91-cell board. Other notable forms change board size or orientation, so they are not interchangeable editions of Gliński’s rules.

### Cited Findings

- Pritchard’s survey covers boards from 43 to 169 cells and says that inventors did not agree on overall board size and shape. It separately lists McCooey and Honeycutt’s 1979 game, Shafran’s 70-cell board, de Vasa’s diamond board, Brusky’s 84-cell board, and other forms. — [Pritchard, chapter 22 (2007)](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf)
- Gliński’s standard uses a vertically oriented regular 91-cell hexagon, three cell colours, three bishops and nine pawns per side. — [Pritchard, chapter 22 (2007)](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf); [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf)
- McCooey and Honeycutt developed their variant in 1978–79 on a similar 91-cell board. The starting array is more compact and each side has seven rather than nine pawns; pawns capture forward diagonally, and the central pawn lacks an opening double move. — [Pritchard, chapter 22 (2007)](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf); [C’Escacs variant comparison](https://cescacs.orgfree.com/en/historical-milestones.html)
- The 2019 thesis distinguishes these as separate versions with different starting positions while observing that many variants reuse Gliński-style board/movement ideas. — [Harris, 2019 thesis, chapter 4](https://pure.southwales.ac.uk/files/30748392/LHarris_Thesis_Main.pdf)

### Inferences

- The roadmap’s Gliński default should pin the full 91-cell setup, three-colour bishop assignments and Gliński pawn rules; it should not silently use McCooey’s compact array or diagonal pawn captures.
- Shafran and other differently shaped boards are distinct rulesets rather than “variants” to mix into the v1 product.

### Gaps

- The accessible primary Gliński rulebook and the exact final rules variant need confirmation in the parallel primary-rules research. Variant popularity evidence does not determine details such as stalemate treatment.

## What historical adoption evidence supports a popularity claim?

### Takeaway

There is credible evidence of substantial historical reach, especially in Poland and Eastern Europe: publications, reported set sales/player counts, federations, and decades of championship records. The large numerical estimates should be reported as attributed claims rather than verified totals; adoption peaked decades ago and later declined.

### Cited Findings

- Pritchard says Gliński launched the game in Britain in 1949 and reports, with qualifying language, over 500,000 players in Eastern Europe and 130,000 sets sold in Poland within a few months. The same account describes British, European and candidate tournaments, an International Hexagonal Chess Federation, and decline after Gliński’s death. — [Pritchard, chapter 22 (2007)](https://www.chessvariants.com/books.dir/cecv/203212.pdf)
- A Polish-language specialist article says Gliński’s 1973 rules book reached seven English and French editions by 1977, and records a 1976 British championship and national federation creation. — [Młody Technik, “Szachy heksagonalne Glińskiego”](https://mlodytechnik.pl/eksperymenty-i-zadania-szkolne/szachy/30503-szachy-heksagonalne-glinskiego)
- The English Chess Federation’s 2024 archival article describes BBC coverage in 1975, the first British congress/championship in 1976, further European tournaments and a 1982 IHCF publication. It says no officially recognized world championship had occurred. — [English Chess Federation, *ChessMoves*, October 2024](https://www.englishchess.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/ChessMoves-Oct-2024.pdf)
- A 2022 *Abstract Games* history reports British and European championships, Hungarian national championships from 1982 onward, the 1984 international tournament, and a 1990–91 event called the first and only World Championship. It attributes the half-million-player claim to Pritchard and says the game fell from its popularity peak after Gliński died. — [Kerry Handscomb, *Abstract Games*, issue 24 (2022)](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_24.pdf)

### Inferences

- The historical record supports choosing Gliński over McCooey as the catalog representative: earlier and broader dissemination is supported by independent variant references, book editions, federation activity and tournament history.
- Use careful wording such as “historically the best-known / probably most widely played hexagonal chess version.” Attribute “half a million players” and “130,000 sets” to Pritchard’s report instead of presenting them as audited statistics.
- The 1990–91 “World Championship” described by *Abstract Games* can coexist with the ECF article’s statement about no officially recognized world championship; the sources do not establish recognition by an external mainstream chess authority.

### Gaps

- No original sales ledger, player census or reliable comparative count for McCooey was found.
- “Most popular” reflects historical prominence, not a measured current ranking. Contemporary online-play numbers were not established from the reviewed sources.
