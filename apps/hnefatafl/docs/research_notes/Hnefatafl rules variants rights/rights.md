# Rights boundary for Copenhagen Hnefatafl

## Who created the selected rules, and what materials are protected?

### Takeaway

Copenhagen Hnefatafl is a modern ruleset outlined in 2012 by Aage Nielsen, Adam Bartley, and Tim Millar; Adam Bartley is credited with the English text and diagrams. This is distinct from the ancient Hnefatafl family. The primary rules page does not offer a reuse license and carries an “all rights reserved” site notice.

### Cited Findings

- The official Copenhagen rules identify Aage Nielsen (Denmark), Adam Bartley (Norway), and Tim Millar (UK) as the 2012 rules contributors, and credit Adam Bartley for the English text and diagrams. The page footer says “Copyright © 1998–2026 Aage Nielsen. All rights reserved.” — [Copenhagen Hnefatafl 11×11 rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php)
- The site's history says Copenhagen 11×11 was developed from January to June 2012 as an extension of the Fetlar rules. — [History of the aagenielsen.dk Hnefatafl website](https://aagenielsen.dk/site_history.php)
- A separate public rules page reproduces the Copenhagen rules with creator attribution and credits its own current page copyright to David Lawrence Campbell. The page has no visible open-content license for its rule prose/diagrams. — [Hnefatafl.org rules and attribution](https://hnefatafl.org/rules.html)
- Aage Nielsen's site marks its rules collection and site pages with an all-rights-reserved footer; no digital adaptation grant or Creative Commons/open-content notice was located on the Copenhagen rules pages. — [Aage Nielsen's rules index](https://aagenielsen.dk/tafl_rules.php), [Copenhagen rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php)

### Inferences

- Several contributors are associated with the modern rules, and the available sources do not explain assignment, joint ownership, or who may license a digital adaptation. Do not assume Aage Nielsen alone can clear every component.
- The text, diagrams, board illustrations, game artwork, and website assets should be treated as third-party expression. Write all tutorial/rules prose independently and create original board/piece art unless a license is verified.

### Gaps

- No current rights-holder statement, contributor agreement, assignment, or license for adapting the 2012 Copenhagen rules into a digital game was found.
- No trademark search was completed for “Copenhagen Hnefatafl,” “Hnefatafl,” or a product brand. The absence of a result here would not establish worldwide availability.

## Can the game mechanics be implemented independently?

### Takeaway

Some official copyright guidance distinguishes game methods from expressive materials: in the United States, the Copyright Office says game titles and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while rules text and board art may be. UK guidance says copyright controls copying/adaptation/online use of protected works. These general statements do not resolve rights worldwide or determine whether this specific modern ruleset has other relevant protections.

### Cited Findings

- The U.S. Copyright Office states that a game's name/title and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while written rules and board/container artwork may qualify when sufficiently original. — [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- UK guidance says copyright arises automatically in original written and artistic works and controls copying, distribution, adaptation, and making works available online. — [UK Intellectual Property Office: How copyright protects your work](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/overview)
- The UK guidance says copyright protection may apply overseas through international agreements and that term and law can differ by country. — [UK Intellectual Property Office: How copyright protects your work](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/overview)

### Inferences

- A developer might have a legal basis to independently code game procedures while authoring fresh rules explanations, lessons, illustrations, and interface. The sources above are jurisdiction-specific, general guidance; they do not themselves grant a license to the Copenhagen rules or provide a worldwide clearance determination.
- Copenhagen includes recent authored choices beyond the historic game. The repo's explicit permission/license gate is more conservative than relying on a mechanics-only copyright argument, so independent authorship alone does not satisfy that internal gate.

### Gaps

- No jurisdiction-specific legal review was completed for an online product hosted on Melon and available internationally.
- Patent, design-right, unfair-competition, contract, and trademark searches were not completed. No conclusion about those rights should be inferred from the copyright guidance.

## Does an existing open-source implementation clear the rights gate?

### Takeaway

A separate Copenhagen Hnefatafl engine is published under AGPL-3.0-or-later, which can provide a code-reuse license for that software subject to its terms. That license does not establish permission to copy the rule authors' text, diagrams, art, brand, or rule contributions, and it is not a license granted by the Copenhagen rules contributors.

### Cited Findings

- The current `hnefatafl-copenhagen` crate (6.4.1) identifies itself as an engine/client/server/AI, and its source header states AGPL-3.0-or-later. — [Docs.rs current crate source](https://docs.rs/hnefatafl-copenhagen/latest/src/hnefatafl_copenhagen/lib.rs.html)
- Its maintainer announced changing the software license to AGPLv3-or-later in January 2026. — [Maintainer release announcement](https://users.rust-lang.org/t/hnefatafl-copenhagen-release/137111)
- The official rules attribute the 2012 rules to Nielsen, Bartley, and Millar and the English text/diagrams to Bartley; the engine's AGPL metadata identifies David Campbell as software copyright holder, not as the rules contributors' licensing representative. — [Copenhagen rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php), [crate metadata](https://docs.rs/hnefatafl-copenhagen/latest/src/hnefatafl_copenhagen/lib.rs.html)

### Inferences

- Reusing that engine could authorize copying/modifying its software code if the project complies with AGPL-3.0-or-later; code licensing and rules/content clearance are separate questions.
- The game's current app could also be considered a reference implementation, but its existence and open-source license do not prove that the three rules contributors licensed all expression or rule-set adaptation rights to the engine author.

### Gaps

- The boundaries of the engine's own rule documentation/content license and any permissions from the 2012 rule contributors were not verified.
- No current rights-holder permission for a free, public, GPL-3.0 Chaturanga product was found.

## Repository gate

### Takeaway

**No-go for implementation under this repository's AGENTS.md at this time.** The game mechanics may be independently implementable in some jurisdictions, but the repo requires an established permission or applicable license for a faithful digital project. No such Copenhagen-specific permission/license or rights-holder confirmation was located; keep branding separately gated and do not reuse third-party text/art.

### Cited Findings

- The repository's [AGENTS.md](../../../../../AGENTS.md) requires permission or an applicable license covering a faithful digital project before scheduling newly authored or published titles.
- The primary rules page's all-rights-reserved notice and authorship attribution do not grant digital adaptation permission. — [Copenhagen Hnefatafl rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php)
- General U.S./UK copyright guidance distinguishes game methods from expression but does not supply permission from the rules contributors or clear all jurisdictions/rights. — [U.S. Copyright Office](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html), [UK Intellectual Property Office](https://www.gov.uk/copyright/overview)

### Inferences

- Under the internal gate, proceed only after written permission from the relevant rights holder(s) or an applicable license expressly covers the intended digital adaptation. A code license from a separate engine project is not enough by itself.
- No one was contacted. A future rights request should identify all three rules contributors and ask who owns/licences the Copenhagen rule set, text/diagram, and mark rights for a free, open-source online game.

### Gaps

- The relevant current rights holder(s), contributors' rights arrangements, and product-name availability remain unresolved.
