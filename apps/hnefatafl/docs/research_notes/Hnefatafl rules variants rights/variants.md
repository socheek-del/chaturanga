# Hnefatafl variants and adoption

## Why Copenhagen 11×11 is the documented tournament choice

### Takeaway

Copenhagen is a defensible selection for this roadmap because its authors describe it as the modern tournament variant, and the 2026 World Tafl Federation Championship page runs a dedicated 11×11 Copenhagen championship line. Fetlar was the earlier modern tournament form and remains a distinct, established option. The evidence supports “documented tournament standard,” not a claim that Copenhagen is the most popular Hnefatafl ruleset in general.

### Cited Findings

- Aage Nielsen's Copenhagen rules page says the 11×11 rules were outlined in 2012 by Aage Nielsen, Adam Bartley, and Tim Millar; the page is updated 21 August 2026. [Copenhagen rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php)
- Nielsen's game-rules index calls Copenhagen 11×11 “the modern tournament variant,” and links separate rules for Fetlar 11×11 as “the classic modern variant.” [Modern and historical Hnefatafl rules index](https://aagenielsen.dk/tafl_rules.php)
- The 2026 World Tafl Federation Championship page lists Copenhagen Hnefatafl 11×11 as a separate tournament line, publishes standings, and awards the “World Tafl Federation Champion” title for that line. It separately runs a historical Hnefatafl line and race-game line. [World Tafl Federation Championship 2026](https://aagenielsen.dk/wtfturnering2026.php)
- A printed 2025 rules guide based on Nielsen's site says Copenhagen was introduced to improve the game based on Fetlar rules, adding the exit-fort, shieldwall, and related corner-square capture provisions. [Copenhagen Hnefatafl 11×11 guide](https://aagenielsen.dk/Copenhagen_Hnefatafl_11x11.pdf)

### Inferences

- The roadmap's description of Copenhagen as a tournament standard is supported by the variant authors' own rules index and the current tournament organizer's event structure.
- It is appropriate to select Copenhagen for a new product without representing it as the historically original Hnefatafl or the only tournament variant.

### Gaps

- I found no independent federation constitution or governing-body record establishing that the World Tafl Federation is a legally constituted federation; phrase the claim around the tournament page's naming and practice.
- No comprehensive tournament history or cross-platform database compares all active rule variants over time.

## Differences from Fetlar and historical reconstructions

### Takeaway

Copenhagen and Fetlar share the 11×11 strong-king foundation and many basic rules. Copenhagen adds the shieldwall and exit-fort mechanics and adjudicates perpetual repetition against the defender; Fetlar permits a draw by repetition and has no Copenhagen-style exit-fort or shieldwall. Historical-weak-king reconstructions are a separate rules family, not small Copenhagen tweaks.

### Cited Findings

- Both Copenhagen and Fetlar use 11×11 boards, two players with attackers moving first, rook-like orthogonal movement, a king escaping to one of four corners, restricted throne/corner squares, and a four-sided king capture near the throne. [Copenhagen rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php); [Fetlar rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/fetlar_rules_en.php)
- The Copenhagen rules include edge shieldwall capture, exit-fort victory for defenders, encirclement victory for attackers, and a loss for White/defenders on perpetual repetition. A player with no legal move loses; positions where neither side can end the game are drawn. [Copenhagen rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/copenhagen_rules.php)
- The Fetlar rules page attributes its rules to the Fetlar Hnefatafl Panel in 2007. It permits draws through repetitions or draw forts and does not list the Copenhagen shieldwall or exit-fort rules. [Fetlar rules](https://aagenielsen.dk/fetlar_rules_en.php)
- Nielsen's historical reconstruction instead uses a weak king captured from two sides, reflecting a separate interpretation of historical play; his rules index classifies it separately from the strong-king modern variants. [Historical Hnefatafl index](https://aagenielsen.dk/tafl_rules.php)
- Nielsen's history page says Copenhagen was built on Fetlar, whereas his 2025 guide contrasts strong-king Copenhagen with historical weak-king variants and explains the different escape/capture balance. [Copenhagen history guide](https://aagenielsen.dk/Copenhagen_Hnefatafl_11x11.pdf)

### Inferences

- Tutorials, bots, and rules validation should explicitly teach Copenhagen's shieldwall, exit-fort, encirclement, repetition, king capture and restricted-square rules; implementing only a generic “Viking chess” ruleset would change the selected edition.
- “Copenhagen” should be labeled as a modern ruleset developed by named contemporary contributors; it should not be presented as a direct reconstruction of a single ancient rules text.

### Gaps

- The online Copenhagen page and printed translation are by contributors to the rules; independent tournament rulebooks from additional organizers would strengthen cross-organization standard evidence.
- The exit-fort rule requires an operational adjudication definition. The textual rule says the king must touch the edge, have a legal move, and be in a fort the attackers cannot break, but it does not fully specify a computational proof procedure.

## Adoption evidence and popularity limits

### Takeaway

Copenhagen has current organized tournament use. A single 2026 event page lists 27 entrants in its Copenhagen tournament and 18 in the historical line, but these are organizer-reported figures for different competitions, not a representative popularity survey. No reliable evidence establishes Copenhagen as the most-played variant across online platforms, clubs, and physical games.

### Cited Findings

- The World Tafl Federation Championship 2026 page lists 27 named Copenhagen players and 18 named historical-line players, each in its respective tournament section. The page reports its tournament activity and titles but does not define the entries as a global participation dataset. [World Tafl Federation Championship 2026](https://aagenielsen.dk/wtfturnering2026.php)
- Nielsen's rules index calls Copenhagen the “modern tournament variant,” while labeling Fetlar the classic modern variant; this is a useful organizer/developer classification, not measured prevalence. [Modern and historical Hnefatafl rules index](https://aagenielsen.dk/tafl_rules.php)
- The 2026 tournament page contains separate results and historical tournament lines, demonstrating that both modern Copenhagen and historical reconstructions remain active within this organizer's community. [World Tafl Federation Championship 2026](https://aagenielsen.dk/wtfturnering2026.php)

### Inferences

- Copenhagen is the safest catalog default because the roadmap requests a documented tournament standard, not because the small tournament sample proves the most-popular ranking.
- Report adoption as ongoing organized play and distinguish it from historical popularity claims about ancient Hnefatafl.

### Gaps

- I found no comparable audited counts of active players, games, tournament starts, box sales, or installations across Copenhagen, Fetlar, Tablut, and other variants.
- The 27-versus-18 entrant comparison is a sample from one organizer's separate 2026 tournament lines; it cannot establish an overall variant popularity ratio.
