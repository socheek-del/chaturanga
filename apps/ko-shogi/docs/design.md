# Ko Shogi interface direction

## Field atlas

Treat the interface as a present-day strategy field atlas: calm enough to read a dense 19×19 position, with the energy of a campaign map when a shot or compound route is active. Make the game feel like a serious study tool, not a museum facsimile. The product must identify the rules as a reconstruction and must not suggest endorsement by an archive or association.

## Visual system

- Use warm, uncoated-paper surfaces and deep blue-black ink, with vermilion reserved for captures, shots, promotion decisions, and the active route. A muted mineral green marks safe destinations. Keep contrast strong enough for the tiny seals on a 19×19 board.
- Give the board the visual weight of a printed map: a thin double-rule frame, precise coordinates on all four edges, a fine square grid, and restrained topographic contour lines outside the playing area. Do not use a checkerboard.
- Draw every piece as an original, high-contrast seal with a short Latin abbreviation. Distinguish color by both ink/background and border treatment. Promotion gets a small vermilion corner stamp. The glyph atlas explains the complete short-name key.
- Use an editorial serif for titles and lesson headings, paired with a compact humanist sans for controls, coordinates, and move notation. Load fonts from the product itself so the installed PWA renders offline.
- Keep surfaces mostly flat and paper-like. Use a single inset border and short, deliberate hover/selection transitions; avoid glossy cards, thick toy buttons, and decorative animation that competes with long routes.

## Layout and interaction

- On desktop, use a narrow left rail for game context and the board as the dominant central element. A right-side ledger holds turn status, route variants, shot targets, moves, save controls and the game’s selected lesson.
- On phones, place the board first, then a compact status strip, actions and ledger. Keep touch targets usable while allowing the board to pan and zoom without turning a 19×19 position into miniature text.
- Show legal ordinary destinations with mineral-green dots. Render compound routes as numbered vermilion path marks; when a shooter lands, ask the player to select one clear shot target before committing the entire turn.
- Give promotions a small choice sheet at the end of the move. Explain irreversible versus reversible promotion and the priest-loss restriction in plain language at the point of decision.
- The tutorial uses the same board and action controls as a live game. Its current objective appears as a short field note, and correct actions earn a quiet route confirmation rather than a game-like reward animation.

## Voice

Use concise, literal English. Distinguish source-backed rules from choices made to make play complete. Use “reconstructed rules” in the title and tutorial introduction. The unit called a Governor and the two Priest forms have distinct seals and names in every view.
