# Choose a Reconstructed Ko Shogi Profile

Ko Shogi should be planned as a **historical reconstruction based on Ogyū Sorai’s 1770 *Kōshōgifu* and, where needed, the 1773 *Kōshōgifu Gukai* commentary**, not as a proven popular edition or tournament standard. The modern rules summary describes a 19×19 intersection board and 90 pieces per side, but the historical texts leave gaps on several mechanics, and modern summaries give conflicting totals for distinct moves. The evidence does not establish a historical or present-day popularity winner; one shogi-variant overview says there is no evidence the game was ever played, a claim that should remain qualified rather than treated as proof. A product needs its own source-linked rules contract, with each reconstruction choice visible to players. The 1770 rules-content gate is clear for using the historical mechanics; image reuse conditions remain separate. **Do not schedule engine implementation until the complete rules chart is transcribed and reviewed.**

## 2026-11-27 primary-source and rights check

Tōhoku University’s catalog identifies Sorai’s *Kōshōgifu* as a 1770 Japanese book and exposes a 14-image IIIF scan. Visual review of the scan confirms that the setup order is given in the text and that the later pages contain movement descriptions and diagrams. Those pages have not yet been transcribed piece by piece, so they establish source availability rather than rules completeness. The separate-volume bibliography identifies Bun’yū’s *Gukai* as the 1773 second volume. Noda City Library’s record labels its copy 1770; retain this as a catalog discrepancy and do not treat it alone as evidence that the commentary was printed that year. ([Tōhoku University catalog](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232), [German Union Catalogue, entry 283](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2327/2397_VOHD_XXVII5.pdf?sequence=1), [Noda City Library](https://www.library-noda.jp/homepage/digilib/shogi/01.html))

The rules-content permission gate is met for mechanics derived from Sorai’s 1770 text: the National Diet Library dates him 1666–1728, and Japan’s Copyright Act generally protects a known author’s work for 70 years after death. This is the project’s source-rights inference, not blanket clearance for later expression or digitized images. Tōhoku University states that image reuse conditions vary by item; no archive scans will be shipped, traced, or committed. The 1773 commentary must be separately checked if it supplies any rule adopted in the product. ([National Diet Library authority record](https://id.ndl.go.jp/auth/ndlna/00271301), [Japan Copyright Act, Article 51](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/clj/cl2.html), [Tōhoku University image-use conditions](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/use_of_materials))

The remaining block is rules verification: independently transcribe the full setup and every base/promoted movement, compare the commentary, and explain the 53-versus-65 move-count discrepancy. Until then, keep `ko-shogi-001` blocked before engine code. All files were reviewed in a temporary workspace; no scan image was added to the repository.

## The 1770 and 1773 texts support reconstruction, not one settled edition

The strongest documentary baseline is Sorai’s *Kōshōgifu*, catalogued as an Edo-period work published in 1770, plus the later *Gukai* commentary. A University of Tokyo account identifies the first as Sorai’s complex rules book and describes the commentary as a separate explanatory text. Catalogs disagree on the commentary’s date: Noda City Library labels a copy 1770, while the German Union Catalogue, Risshō University, and Kanazawa City Library support 1773. The 1773 date is therefore the better-supported choice, with the Noda listing retained as a catalog discrepancy. ([University of Tokyo account](https://www.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/info/about/booklet-gazette/bulletin/637/open/637-01-1.html), [Tōhoku University catalog](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232), [German Union Catalogue, entry 283](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2327/2397_VOHD_XXVII5.pdf?sequence=1), [Noda City Library](https://www.library-noda.jp/homepage/digilib/shogi/01.html), [Kanazawa City Library](https://www2.lib.kanazawa.ishikawa.jp/kinsei/wahon.htm))

The present detailed English specification is a modern synthesis, not a critical translation of the source pair. It describes **a 19×19 Go-style intersection board, 90 pieces per side, 34 starting types, no drops, shooting, multi-leg captures, and linked promotions**. Yet the English article says there are 65 distinct moves while a separate overview says 53. The available notes do not resolve that difference or independently transcribe every piece diagram. The game therefore needs a full piece-by-piece chart reviewed against usable Japanese source copies before an engine can claim complete rules coverage. ([Ko Shogi rules summary](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi), [Shogi variant overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant))

Several mechanics need explicit decisions before the rules contract is frozen. The sources differ on whether shooting must follow a move; the meaning of Banner-and-Drum role substitution is unexplained; Poison Flame’s burning effect is described differently across the texts; the range of certain five-intersection effects is unclear; and Governor appears in the modern profile although the notes report it absent from both historical texts. The modern summary supplies playable interpretations, but using them would be an inference. A product should call itself “Ko Shogi, reconstructed rules,” identify the selected source for each mechanic, and document these uncertainties rather than present every convention as certain historical fact. ([Ko Shogi source-difference notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi#Notes))

## No evidence supports a popularity claim

The target Wikipedia catalog lists **Ko Shogi separately from “Shogi variants”** and assigns it a bare 1997 date, while the historical sources establish a 1770 printed rules text and a later commentary. The date’s meaning is not explained by the sources reviewed and should not be used as the game’s origin date. The project roadmap’s choice of Chu Shogi for the umbrella “Shogi variants” entry and Ko Shogi as its own historical game is consistent with that separation. ([Wikipedia list section](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_abstract_strategy_games#Chess_and_chess-like_games), [Ko Shogi history](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi), [Tōhoku University catalog](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232))

The “no evidence it was ever played” statement comes from an encyclopedia overview, not a complete historical proof. The review found archival catalogs and modern listings, but no participation counts, recurring Ko Shogi tournament results, recognized contemporary association rules, or comparative activity data. A modern listing establishes that a game can be found, not that it has an active player base. **No Ko Shogi edition can honestly be called the most popular on this evidence**; the defensible selection criterion is the reconstruction most clearly tied to the surviving texts. ([Shogi variant overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant#Historical_variants), [Analog.Games listing](https://analog.games/games/2cbfde7b-d449-446b-8df7-0a0c497acb14/ko-shogi))

## Clean-room implementation and rights boundaries

The research supports a clean-room path: derive mechanics from historical sources, then write original code, explanations, notation, and diagrams. The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes a game’s method of play from protectable written or visual expression, and treats program logic differently from original software expression; this is a U.S. summary, not a global legal clearance. Japan’s statute protects creative expression and adaptation rights, so the project should not copy the modern English article’s wording, table layout, examples, or diagrams. ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html), [U.S. Copyright Office: Computer Programs](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-programs.html), [Copyright Law of Japan, arts. 2 and 27](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/doc/LawOfJapan_202603.pdf))

The original 1770 rules text is public domain under the applicable author-life term: Sorai died in 1728, and Japan’s Copyright Act generally gives a known author's work a 70-year term after death. This is the project’s source-rights inference, not legal advice or clearance for later expression. Tōhoku University says image reuse terms are item-specific; this project will not republish or trace the archive scans. Waseda likewise requires advance application to reuse its scan. The 1773 commentary needs a separate source-rights check if it supplies adopted rules not found in the 1770 text. The Commons setup SVG is available under CC BY 3.0, but original artwork avoids that attribution obligation. The modern History.chess reconstruction's author and license remain unverified, so its prose and diagrams should not be reused. ([National Diet Library authority record](https://id.ndl.go.jp/auth/ndlna/00271301), [Japan Copyright Act, Article 51](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/clj/cl2.html), [Tōhoku image-use conditions](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/use_of_materials), [Waseda catalog](https://www.wul.waseda.ac.jp/kotenseki/html/wo09/wo09_00837/index.html), [Waseda image-use policy](https://www.waseda.jp/library/en/user/using-images/?wovn=en), [Commons setup image](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:KoShogiSente.svg), [History.chess reconstruction](http://history.chess.free.fr/koshogi.htm))

## Conclusion

Treat Ko Shogi as a source-gated reconstruction, not a popularity-based variant selection. A 14-image scan of the 1770 text is available for research, and its source-rights gate is clear for mechanics. The remaining work is to transcribe every setup cell and movement, compare the 1773 commentary where it clarifies the rules, explain the 53-versus-65 count, and record playable conventions for unresolved mechanics. No rights-holder contact was made and no archive image was committed. Keep Ko Shogi blocked before engine code until that rules work is complete.
