# Ko Shogi rights and source boundaries

Research note for implementation planning, updated 2026-11-27. This is a conservative product-research note, not legal advice. The project will publish a playable implementation, tutorial text, and original visual assets, so each source's text, scans, and graphics should be treated separately.

## Q1. Can the historical game rules be implemented, and what limits apply to modern reconstructions?

**Takeaway.** Build the rules engine from mechanics independently derived from historical sources, with original prose, notation, code, and diagrams. Do not copy the modern English rule article, its tables, piece diagrams, or translations. The documented historical sources themselves leave some mechanics ambiguous, so settle and document the chosen interpretation before calling it an implementation of a particular ruleset.

**Cited Findings.** The U.S. Copyright Office says a game's idea, name, and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while a sufficiently original written rules description or game artwork may be protected ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)). It also says copyright can protect original software expression but does not protect ideas, algorithms, systems, methods, or program logic ([U.S. Copyright Office: Computer Programs](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-programs.html)). Japan's Copyright Act defines a work as a creatively produced expression of thought or sentiment and grants authors translation and adaptation rights ([Copyright Law of Japan, arts. 2, 27](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/doc/LawOfJapan_202603.pdf)).

The 1770 *Kōshōgifu* is catalogued by Waseda as a work by Ogyū Sorai, published in 1770 ([Waseda Kotenseki catalog](https://www.wul.waseda.ac.jp/kotenseki/html/wo09/wo09_00837/index.html)). The 1773 *Kōshōgifugukai* has a National Institute of Japanese Literature catalog record ([NIJL catalog record 100169203](https://kokusho.nijl.ac.jp/biblio/100169203)). The English Wikipedia article identifies differences and unresolved points between these two records, including shooting conditions and mechanics not specified consistently; it is a modern, collaborative reconstruction rather than the historical source itself ([Ko shogi article and notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)). Wikipedia text is licensed CC BY-SA 4.0, and reused or adapted text requires attribution and share-alike licensing ([Wikimedia Terms of Use, §7](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_Use?useformat=mobile)).

**Inferences.** Copyright rules differ by jurisdiction; the cited U.S. rule-method distinction is not a global clearance. Under a conservative cross-border policy, use historical rule facts as research input, independently express each mechanic, and avoid copying a modern author's selection, explanation, notation, examples, or graphic arrangement. Record which primary-source passage supports each mechanic and label any unresolved reconstruction choice in the project's rules documentation. A clean-room implementation is a lower-risk route, not a guarantee of clearance.

**Gaps.** The authorship, license, and permission status of the modern reconstruction at [History.chess: Ko Shogi](http://history.chess.free.fr/koshogi.htm) could not be verified. No permission inquiry was made. The exact mechanics should be checked against usable copies of both historical sources before a selected rules profile is finalized.

## Q2. May the historical texts, scans, and diagrams be reused?

**Takeaway.** The eighteenth-century texts are old enough that their original expression is outside the ordinary Japanese author-life copyright term, but that does not clear a modern scan or a library's website terms. Use the books as research sources; do not bundle or reproduce scans until the exact item's reuse status is confirmed.

**Cited Findings.** The University of Tokyo identifies Ogyū Sorai as 1666–1728 ([University of Tokyo profile](https://www.u-tokyo.ac.jp/gen03/kouhou/1611/03column.html)); Japan's Act generally protects a work for 70 years after the author's death ([Copyright Law of Japan, arts. 51, 57](https://www.cric.or.jp/english/doc/LawOfJapan_202603.pdf)). Waseda's catalog identifies its 1770 scan and states “Copyright Waseda University Library” ([Waseda catalog record](https://www.wul.waseda.ac.jp/kotenseki/html/wo09/wo09_00837/index.html)). Waseda says use of collection images—including reproduction on a homepage—requires an advance application and completion of the required formalities ([Waseda image-use policy](https://www.waseda.jp/library/en/user/using-images/?wovn=en)).

NIJL warns that digitized-image conditions vary by holding institution; it says an image without a Public Domain or Creative Commons mark requires permission from the owner for reuse ([NIJL image-use conditions](https://kokusho.nijl.ac.jp/page/en/usage.html?ln=en)). Its record for *Kōshōgifugukai* is discoverable, but the item's actual rights mark and holder-specific terms were not verified during this research ([NIJL catalog record 100169203](https://kokusho.nijl.ac.jp/biblio/100169203)).

**Inferences.** Sorai died in 1728 and Bun’yū in 1763 (the commentary author/date are catalogued at [Kotobank](https://kotobank.jp/word/%E6%96%87%E9%9B%84-16912)). Both original texts are outside Japan's general 70-year author-life term, so using their mechanics as rules facts clears this project's content gate. Treat a digital image as a separate asset with separate terms: public-domain status of the underlying book does not override a repository's stated contractual/use conditions. Cite bibliographic records and link users to the hosting institution instead of republishing source scans.

**Gaps.** Waseda's catalog metadata and image-use policy are not a substitute for item-specific permission or legal analysis of the scan. The NIJL record's image license/rights icon and owner terms remain unchecked. Do not download for redistribution, embed, or modify either library scan until the current item page clearly authorizes that use.

### 2026-11-27 source-rights decision

The Tōhoku University record for Sorai's 1770 *Kōshōgifu* exposes a 14-image scan and links to item-specific image terms. Its general policy says image reuse terms vary by content. Sorai is dated 1666–1728 by the National Diet Library; the commentary author Bun’yū is dated 1700–1763 in biographical records. Japan's Copyright Act generally protects a known author's work for 70 years after death, so both original books clear this project's rules-content gate as sources of mechanics. This does not clear a library scan or modern expression. No archive image is included, traced, or redistributed. ## Q3. What is the safe policy for code, piece art, setup diagrams, and tutorial content?

**Takeaway.** Write the engine and tutorial from the team's documented rule model; commission or create original piece art and diagrams. If reusing any open image or text, preserve source-level attribution and license notices, and review whether the license fits the product's distribution before shipping.

**Cited Findings.** Original program code is copyrightable expression, while its algorithms, logic, system, and methods are not protected under the U.S. Copyright Office's computer-program guidance ([U.S. Copyright Office: Computer Programs](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-programs.html)). Game descriptions and board art can also contain protectable literary or pictorial expression ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)).

The commonly linked initial-setup SVG on Wikimedia Commons was created by TKR101010 in 2010 and is licensed CC BY 3.0, which allows sharing and adaptation subject to credit and indication of changes ([Commons file record](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:KoShogiSente.svg)). Wikipedia's prose is CC BY-SA 4.0 and modified or adapted text must remain under that license with a notice and attribution ([Wikimedia Terms of Use, §7](https://foundation.wikimedia.org/wiki/Terms_of_Use?useformat=mobile)).

**Inferences.** Create code from a written, source-linked rules specification without transcribing another implementation. Draw board layouts and pieces from independently recorded facts (such as board size, starting coordinates, and historical piece names), not by tracing a modern diagram or reproducing a scan. Keep a provenance file for every non-original asset: creator, source URL, exact license/terms, attribution string, modifications, and compatibility review. The Commons setup image is an available licensed option only if the product is prepared to meet CC BY attribution terms; an original diagram is the simpler provenance path.

**Gaps.** No Ko Shogi-specific modern rules-engine license, piece-font license, or standalone game-logo trademark status was verified. There is no identified contemporary Ko Shogi software that has been cleared for code reuse. Resolve these only if the implementation later wants to reuse a particular asset or software package.
