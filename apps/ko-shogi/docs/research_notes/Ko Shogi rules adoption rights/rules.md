# Ko Shogi Rules and Provenance

## 2026-11-27 rules-gate completion

The source gate is now complete for implementation. The seven-line setup has been transcribed into center-out coordinates, checked against the 1770 scan, and counted to 90 pieces per side. All 34 starting forms and their 31 promotion forms now have a documented movement profile in [`packages/ko-shogi/RULES.md`](../../../../packages/ko-shogi/RULES.md); source passages, profile differences, the 53/65 reconciliation, and digital decisions are recorded in [`transcription.md`](transcription.md). The 1773 commentary’s source-content gate also clears: Bun’yū is dated 1700–1763, so the general Japanese 70-year author term expired long ago. No scan is being reused.

The table follows the detailed English profile where the old text is unclear or its reading conflicts with that profile. The result is explicitly a reconstruction, not a newly asserted historical standard. Scholarly translation and the exact meaning of a few old lines remain open questions, but they no longer block implementing the named profile.

## 2026-11-27 source access update

The Tōhoku University IIIF scan of Sorai's 1770 *Kōshōgifu* is now accessible at the record's manifest. Visual review of all 14 images confirms that the setup is described in the text and that the later pages contain movement descriptions and diagrams. The rules have not yet been transcribed individually. The 1773 *Gukai* is a separately catalogued second volume by Bun’yū, according to the German Union Catalogue; the Noda record's 1770 date remains a catalog discrepancy. Use the 1770 volume as the rules baseline and compare the second volume only where it clarifies a rule. [Tōhoku catalog and IIIF manifest](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232); [German Union Catalogue, entry 283](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2327/2397_VOHD_XXVII5.pdf?sequence=1); [Noda catalog](https://www.library-noda.jp/homepage/digilib/shogi/01.html)

The scan images were fetched only to temporary storage for visual inspection and are not included in this repository. Continue to write an independent source-derived rules profile and original diagrams.

## Historical basis and defensible rules profile

### Takeaway

Treat Ko Shogi as a documented Edo-period design with a modern playable reconstruction, not as a game with an established popular edition. The strongest profile for implementation is the detailed modern reconstruction cross-referenced to Ogyū Sorai’s 1770 *Kōshōgifu* and the 1773 *Kōshōgifu Gukai*; the reconstruction itself records material disagreements between those texts.

### Cited Findings

- The University of Tokyo’s 2022 account identifies *Kōshōgifu* as Sorai’s own rules book for a game he devised, calls the rules complex, and says an Edo-period explanatory commentary, *Kōshōgifu Gukai*, was produced. It reports that Sorai’s preface framed the game as a way to teach beginners military terminology through piece names. [University of Tokyo, Komaba Faculty Bulletin, issue 637 (2022-06-01)](https://www.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/info/about/booklet-gazette/bulletin/637/open/637-01-1.html)
- Tōhoku University’s catalog records a digitized Japanese book titled *廣象棋譜*, attributed to Ogyū Sorai, dated Meiwa 7 (1770), published by Fushimiya Uhee in Edo; the record says it has 14 monochrome image pages and was digitized through NIJL’s historical texts project. [Tōhoku University digital archive catalog](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232)
- Waseda University Library catalogs Sorai’s *広象棋譜 / 物茂卿 著* as an Edo-period 1770 publication. The scan link is listed by the Ko Shogi article, but direct automated access to the Waseda PDF returned 403 during this research. [Waseda University Library record](https://www.wul.waseda.ac.jp/kotenseki/html/wo09/wo09_00837/index.html); [linked scan](https://archive.wul.waseda.ac.jp/kosho/wo09/wo09_00837/wo09_00837.pdf)
- German Union Catalogue bibliography describes *Kōshōgifu* (volume 1, 1770) and *Kōshōgifu Gukai* / *Kōshōgi Zukai* (volume 2, 1773), attributes the first to Ogyū Sorai and the second to Bun’yū, and notes the second volume contains commentary and diagrams. [Verzeichnis der orientalischen Handschriften in Deutschland, vol. XXVII/5, entry 283](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2327/2397_VOHD_XXVII5.pdf?sequence=1)
- Catalog dates for the commentary conflict: the Noda City Library record labels its *Kōshōgifu Gukai* copy as 1770, while the German catalog, Risshō University Library holdings list, and Kanazawa City Library catalog identify the commentary as An’ei 2 (1773). Use 1773 as the better-supported printed date, while retaining the Noda record as a cataloging discrepancy. [Noda City Library record](https://www.library-noda.jp/homepage/digilib/shogi/01.html); [Risshō University Library history / holdings list](https://www.ris.ac.jp/library/kosho/kosho10c.pdf); [Kanazawa City Library catalog](https://www2.lib.kanazawa.ishikawa.jp/kinsei/wahon.htm)
- The current detailed English rules synthesis is the Ko Shogi article, which cites the 1770 book and 1773 commentary and explicitly notes textual gaps. It is a useful implementation specification but not itself a primary source; its own article banner says the individual-piece layout needs cleanup. [Ko Shogi article, accessed 2026-11-26](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- The Shogi variant overview says there is no evidence Ko Shogi was ever played. I found no primary game record in this review; the surviving record I could verify establishes a rules book and commentary, not actual play or popularity. Treat “never played” as a qualified modern-source claim, not a proven historical fact. [Shogi variant overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant)

### Inferences

- The selected implementation target should be named “Ko Shogi, reconstructed rules” and document every adjudication that follows the modern synthesis rather than a directly checked translation of the originals. This keeps the historical design distinct from the present-day playable profile. [University of Tokyo account](https://www.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/info/about/booklet-gazette/bulletin/637/open/637-01-1.html); [Ko Shogi article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- “Most popular version” cannot be established from the located evidence; the 1770/1773 textual reconstruction is the most defensible candidate because it is tied to identifiable historical rule texts. [Tōhoku University catalog](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232); [German Union Catalogue entry 283](https://rep.adw-goe.de/bitstream/handle/11858/2327/2397_VOHD_XXVII5.pdf?sequence=1)

### Gaps

- No scholarly critical translation or comprehensive edition comparison was located. Disputed interpretations are therefore retained as labeled digital conventions rather than claims of historical certainty. The archive records remain available for later source review. [Tōhoku scan record](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232); [NIJL commentary catalog](https://kokusho.nijl.ac.jp/biblio/100169203)

## Reconstructed game specification

### Takeaway

The modern profile is a two-player, 19×19 intersection game with 90 pieces per side and no drops. It has an unusually large, direction-specific move set, shooting, multi-leg captures, and a promotion system with cross-piece dependencies; implement by transcribing the piece diagrams/table, not by simplifying the game into standard shogi rules.

### Cited Findings

- The board is a 19-rank by 19-file grid of 361 intersections. Each side has 90 pieces of 34 starting types. The detailed profile lists 31 promotion forms (65 forms total); 53 is reproducible as 53 distinct movement profiles after merging 12 repeated patterns. The counting definition is an inference, not an explanation found in either catalog. See [`transcription.md`](transcription.md). [Ko Shogi article: equipment and rules](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi); [Shogi variant overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant)
- The starting inventory per player is: General ×1; Clerk ×1; Staff Officer ×1; Aide-de-camp ×2; Sumo Wrestler ×2; Aide ×2; Staff ×2; Chief of Staff ×2; Engineer ×2; Taoist Priest ×1; Spiritual Monk ×1; Advance Guard ×2; Middle Troop ×1; Drum ×1; Banner ×1; Sentry ×2; Millenary ×2; Quartermaster ×2; Centuria ×2; Rear Guard ×2; Frankish Cannon ×1; Elephant ×6; Longbow ×4; Crossbow ×4; Cannon ×4; Cavalryman ×8; Cavalry ×2; Pawn ×6; Patrol Unit ×3; Shield ×6; Shield Unit ×3; Chariot ×8; Chariot Unit ×2; Vanguard ×1. The listed counts sum to 90; the one-side initial layout is diagrammed in the rules article, with the opposite player’s formation oriented to face it. [Ko Shogi article: inventory and setup diagram](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- The original setup text lists seven center-out lines; the coordinate transcription in [`transcription.md`](transcription.md) matches the listed inventory and gives 90 pieces. The source diagrams are not copied or traced. [Tōhoku University scan record](https://touda.tohoku.ac.jp/collection/database/library/10010000019232)
- The promoted forms listed in the modern reconstruction are: Clerk→Master-at-Arms; Staff Officer→Banner-and-Drum; Aide-de-camp→Quartermaster; Aide→Town Brigade; Staff→Village Brigade; Chief of Staff→Vice Commander; Engineer→Poison Flame; Taoist Priest→Five-li Fog; Spiritual Monk→“Immaculate Light?”; Advance Guard→Skyward Net; Middle Troop→Governor; Drum→Thunderclap; Banner→Flag Waver; Sentry→Centuria; Millenary→Dragon Ascending; Quartermaster→Tiger Wing; Centuria→War Hawk; Rear Guard→Earthward Net; Frankish Cannon→“Chariot of the Gods?”; Longbow→Longbow Cavalryman; Crossbow→Crossbow Cavalryman; Cannon→Gun Carriage; Cavalryman→Cavalry; Cavalry→Winged Horse; Pawn→Patrol Unit; Patrol Unit→“Commissar?”; Shield→Shield Unit; Shield Unit→Heavenly Fortress; Chariot→Chariot Unit; Chariot Unit→Millenary; Vanguard→“Commissar?”. General, Sumo Wrestler, and Elephant do not promote. The question marks and article caveat that many English translations are suggestions must be preserved in product terminology review. [Ko Shogi article: promotion table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Black moves first, turns alternate, and a turn consists of moving a piece, with optional/triggered captures and promotions. Ordinary capture removes an opposing piece by displacement; captured pieces are not dropped back into play. [Ko Shogi article: rules and gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Move families in the reconstruction are step, limited-range, jump, range, and shooting. Step movement can be blocked by an adjacent piece; limited-range moves travel a bounded number of intersections; jumpers pass over intervening pieces; range pieces slide along lines and stop on capture or before friendly blockers. The named initial jumpers are Clerk, Staff Officer, Taoist Priest, Spiritual Monk, Cavalryman and Cavalry; initial range pieces are Advance Guard, Millenary, Quartermaster, Centuria, Rear Guard, Elephant, Patrol Unit, Shield Unit and Chariot Unit. Each piece’s directions/ranges are individual and diagrammed. [Ko Shogi article: movement rules and diagrams](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- The six initial shooting types are Taoist Priest, Spiritual Monk, Frankish Cannon, Longbow, Crossbow and Cannon. Shooting removes a target without moving; the general reconstruction says most shooting cannot pass intervening pieces, while Cannon can. The Frankish Cannon’s shot can kill twice. The article notes the original book is unclear about whether shooting must follow movement; the commentary supplies a must-move rule. [Ko Shogi article: shooting and note 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Special movement includes a two-move/double-capture ability for Sumo Wrestler and Cavalry; multiple-capture/igui abilities for several promoted pieces; Poison Flame burning adjacent allied and enemy pieces on arrival; and Skyward/Earthward Net immobilizing enemies along their ranging directions. [Ko Shogi article: special mechanics](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Promotion is allowed when a promotable, not-yet-promoted piece moves into, out of, or wholly within the opponent’s six farthest ranks; the General, Sumo Wrestler and Elephant are excluded. Extra triggers include capturing certain command pieces, a step mover taking specified powerful pieces, and a Cavalryman capturing a Frankish Cannon. Clerk→Master-at-Arms promotion also promotes allied Advance/Rear Guards and destroys enemy Poison Flames. [Ko Shogi article: promotion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- The objective is to remove the opposing General and either Middle Troop or Banner; if the Middle Troop has become Governor, capture of General and Banner leaves Governor as the remaining commander, so it must also be captured. No check evasion is required, but perpetual check against the sole objective piece is prohibited. Four repetitions of the same position with the same player to move are a no-contest; illegal move is loss in the article’s profile. [Ko Shogi article: objective and endings](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)

### Inferences

- A tutorial should start with the unusual board model (intersections, not squares), no-drop capture, turn/capture basics, and then teach movement in digestible families before introducing shooting, coupled promotions, and command-piece victory. This order follows the mechanics and prerequisites in the modern rules description. [Ko Shogi article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- The engine should represent a move as a turn-level sequence of movement legs, shot/capture events, side effects and promotion transitions; treating every legal action as one origin/destination pair would fail to express multi-captures and burns. [Ko Shogi article: special mechanics and notation](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)

### Gaps

- The playable movement/promotion chart is complete in `packages/ko-shogi/RULES.md`. A future scholarly review may refine the source reading for the Chief of Staff and other disputed clauses; the current implementation follows its named English-profile convention. The catalogs themselves do not define how their 53/65 counts were produced.

## Textual ambiguities and implementation decisions

### Takeaway

Several rules are not fully fixed by the surviving sources or modern interpretation. Their v1 choices are now recorded in `packages/ko-shogi/RULES.md` and the interactive tutorial must identify them as reconstruction conventions rather than imply certainty about Sorai’s text.

### Cited Findings

- Shooting: the modern article says *Gukai* requires the shooter to move, while *Kōshōgifu* does not state that requirement clearly and leaves shooting circumstances ambiguous. [Ko Shogi article, note 1](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Poison Flame: the article quotes *Kōshōgifu* as saying everything adjacent is killed regardless of side, including Taoist Priest and Spiritual Monk; it says the original text explains they burn before they can shoot, while the commentary omits this point. [Ko Shogi article, note 2](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Banner-and-Drum: *Kōshōgifu* says the promoted piece may “take over” the Banner and Drum roles but gives no further explanation; *Gukai* omits the rule. [Ko Shogi article, note 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Five-intersection range: the article says the original wording for effects that disable weapons or revert Five-li Fog could mean all intersections reachable in five king steps (an area) rather than a line/ray. The modern page also describes the net’s immobilizing effect by directed lines. [Ko Shogi article, notes 4–6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Gun Carriage and Chariot of the Gods are clearly specified only in *Gukai*; the article says *Kōshōgifu* suggests but does not directly state them. The article further infers shooting-capable pieces can capture by displacement unless specifically prohibited, based on the source’s explicit exception for certain targets. [Ko Shogi article, notes 7–8](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Governor is absent as a named piece in both *Kōshōgifu* and *Gukai*, according to the article’s note; the modern rules nevertheless make Governor a promoted Middle Troop and an extra commanding piece. [Ko Shogi article, note 9 and promotion table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Several promoted English labels are marked uncertain (“Immaculate Light?”, “Chariot of the Gods?”, “Commissar?”), and the article explicitly says many English translations are suggestions only. [Ko Shogi article: piece table](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)

### Inferences

- For an initial digital rules profile, the simplest transparent choice is to follow the modern article’s main body consistently, with a rules note marking it as a reconstruction and linking each of the above disputed choices to the underlying textual ambiguity. This is an implementation recommendation, not a claim that the modern article’s rulings are historically certain. [Ko Shogi article](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- Rules with cross-piece effects should be modeled as explicit transitions (for example, Taoist Priest captured ⇒ Drum/Banner cannot promote and revert if already promoted; Immaculate Light within five intersections ⇒ Five-li Fog reverts; Clerk promotion ⇒ allied guards promote and enemy Poison Flame is removed). Those transitions are described by the modern profile but should be checked against the original text before final conformance claims. [Ko Shogi article: promotion and notes](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)

### Gaps

- The exact original definitions of “within five intersections,” Banner-and-Drum role substitution, and shoot/no-move turn handling remain unsettled in the consulted English account. [Ko Shogi article, notes 1, 3–6](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ko_shogi)
- No independent playtest rulebook, official federation, or widely recognized contemporary competition rule set was located; a digital adaptation cannot be called the popular standard on this evidence. [University of Tokyo account](https://www.c.u-tokyo.ac.jp/info/about/booklet-gazette/bulletin/637/open/637-01-1.html); [Shogi variant overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Shogi_variant)
