# Reconcile Kruzno’s two distinct hex games

The catalog entry “Kruzno” and the roadmap’s “linked hex-checkers game” point to two different rulesets. Wikipedia describes Mark Palko’s commercial 2005 Kruzno, with rook, knight and bishop pieces and an intransitive capture cycle; Kruzno’s official site separately presents “Kruzno Checkers,” an American-checkers-derived game played on the same board. The roadmap’s wording most likely intends the Checkers variant, but a faithful product plan must explicitly record that interpretation rather than silently substitute it for the catalog game. Adoption evidence is local and sparse, so neither ruleset can be called the most popular. More importantly, no digital-adaptation license or permission was found, and the current rights chain is unverified; implementation is blocked under the repository’s rights gate. ([Wikipedia: Kruzno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruzno_(board_game)), [Kruzno Checkers official rules](https://www.kruzno.com/hexcheckers.html), [CHESS-LIKE-ROADMAP](../../../../docs/CHESS-LIKE-ROADMAP.md))

## The roadmap phrase most likely means Kruzno Checkers

The Wikipedia entry links to the base Kruzno game: a two-player strategy game attributed to Mark Palko and reported as first published in 2005. Its board uses a hexagonal arrangement, while the official site treats the board as a platform for several separate games and labels its checkers rules “Kruzno Checkers.” That is a materially distinct variant, not another name for the base game. The roadmap’s request to document “the linked hex-checkers game's setup and jumps” therefore most literally points to Kruzno Checkers; the accompanying instruction to reconcile the catalog and linked article acknowledges the mismatch. The source trail does not authorize treating both games as one implementation. ([Wikipedia: Kruzno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruzno_(board_game)), [Kruzno Game Store](https://www.kruzno.com/stored.html), [Kruzno Checkers](https://www.kruzno.com/hexcheckers.html), [roadmap](../../../../docs/CHESS-LIKE-ROADMAP.md))

## Base Kruzno’s chess pieces use intransitive captures

Wikipedia’s described “most common variant” starts with three rooks, three knights and three bishops per player. Rooks capture knights, knights capture bishops, and bishops capture rooks. Pieces may jump in a sequence, capturing only opposing pieces they defeat in that cycle; a piece that reaches the outer ring must move inward on its next turn unless blocked. Palko describes the board as “6 x 6 x 6,” but the sources reviewed do not supply exact coordinates, full movement geometry, complete initial placement, or a winning condition. A secondary catalogue also reports the reverse capture cycle, and the article cites a 1999 book despite claiming the game was first published in 2005. The base game therefore lacks enough verified rules to support an engine contract without a primary rules source. ([Wikipedia: Kruzno](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruzno_(board_game)), [Palko, 20 Dec 2017](https://observationalepidemiology.blogspot.com/2017/12/), [Társasjátékok listing](https://tarsasjatekok.com/tarsasjatek/kruzno-2004), [Región Malohont](https://regionmalohont.sk/volny-cas/hra-kruzno-outdorova-aktivita-kruzno/))

## Kruzno Checkers has adjacent moves and mandatory jumps

The official Checkers page describes twelve opening pieces shown in a diagram, but its text does not clarify whether that means twelve total or per side. On a non-capturing turn, a piece moves one space to an adjacent hexagon; promotion is not used. Pieces that move onto the shaded perimeter must move back inward on their next turn unless opposing pieces block them. Captures follow American checkers: jump an opponent into an open space immediately beyond it. A capture is mandatory, but when multiple captures are available, the player chooses; the text does not require the maximum capture. The page’s diagrams were not accessible as text, and the rules do not specify whether a capture chain must continue, the exact board mask, or how the game ends. Those missing details prevent a faithful setup fixture or complete tutorial. ([Kruzno Checkers official rules](https://www.kruzno.com/hexcheckers.html))

## Local adoption does not establish a popularity winner

Palko’s posts describe developing and selling a small production run, and the Slovak village of Kružno reportedly bought three sets and installed a public outdoor board. These facts establish a small, documented following, not representative usage. A secondary catalogue reports reduced-piece and three-player variants, but the evidence does not include their primary rules. No sales totals, tournament records, broad player counts, or reliable comparisons between base Kruzno and Kruzno Checkers surfaced. Consequently, the nine-piece base game’s “most common” label is not a demonstrated ranking, and there is no evidence that Kruzno Checkers is more popular. ([Palko, 2014 rules post](https://youdothemathkthrucalculus.blogspot.com/2014/12/), [Palko, 2017 post](https://observationalepidemiology.blogspot.com/2017/12/), [Región Malohont](https://regionmalohont.sk/volny-cas/hra-kruzno-outdorova-aktivita-kruzno/), [Társasjátékok listing](https://tarsasjatekok.com/tarsasjatek/kruzno-2004))

## No evidence grants digital adaptation rights

Palko says he developed Kruzno and sold it; the official site offers the board and publishes separate rules, but neither source grants permission for a faithful digital adaptation. The current copyright/design ownership chain was not verified. A secondary trademark database reports that Palko’s U.S. KRUZNO registration was cancelled in 2022, but that does not resolve unregistered rights or clear the name worldwide; the cancellation record itself was not confirmed through the USPTO file. General guidance distinguishes game mechanics from protected text and artwork, but it is not a license from the creator and does not satisfy the repository’s requirement to establish permission or an applicable license before implementation. No one has been contacted. ([Palko, 2014 rules post](https://youdothemathkthrucalculus.blogspot.com/2014/12/), [Kruzno official site](https://www.kruzno.com/index.html), [USPTO trademark-status guidance](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply/check-status-view-documents), [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html))

## Conclusion

The roadmap likely intended Kruzno Checkers because it explicitly asks for a hex-checkers setup and jumps; Wikipedia’s catalog link, however, identifies the separate base game. Keep that distinction in the product record and do not design one engine from fragments of both. Even after choosing the intended game, its complete setup and ending rules need primary verification. Under AGENTS.md, block implementation until applicable rights clearance is established; any eventual tutorial, artwork, board diagrams and branding should be original unless separately licensed.
