# Kruzno Rights Boundary

## Which Kruzno and who created it?

### Takeaway

The catalog entry is Mark Palko’s commercially published Kruzno hex-board strategy game, first published in 2005 according to the creator and descriptive sources. The official Kruzno site also has a separate game titled “Kruzno Checkers”; that linked rules page is a derivative checkers variant for the Kruzno board, not the same ruleset as the main Kruzno game.

### Cited Findings
- Wikipedia’s Kruzno (board game) entry describes a two-player abstract game on a hexagonal board and says it was first published in 2005 by a company of the same name; it names the usual 3-rook/3-knight/3-bishop setup, intransitive capture and jump mechanics — [Wikipedia: Kruzno (board game)](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kruzno_(board_game)).
- Mark Palko’s own 2014 blog post says “a game I developed” and that he was selling it, then publishes his rules — [Palko, “Rules of Kruzno — updated”](https://youdothemathkthrucalculus.blogspot.com/2014/12/).
- A 2017 post on Palko’s other blog describes Kružno as a game he developed from hex-board work and says he produced and tried selling a small batch — [Palko, “A few more words about the official game of Kružno, Slovakia”](https://observationalepidemiology.blogspot.com/2017/12/).
- The official Kruzno site presents Kruzno as a game available to buy or make, with a store offering a Kruzno set and instructions — [Kruzno official site](https://www.kruzno.com/index.html), [Kruzno Game Store](https://www.kruzno.com/stored.html).
- The separate official “Kruzno Checkers” page labels that game a hex-checkers variant specifically designed for a Kruzno board, with its own 12-piece opening, border-move restriction and compulsory captures — [Kruzno Checkers rules](https://www.kruzno.com/hexcheckers.html).
- A regional Slovak information page says Mark Palko invented the game and first published it in 2005; it describes Kružno municipality’s purchase of three sets in 2011 — [Region Malohont, “Hra Kružno”](https://regionmalohont.sk/volny-cas/hra-kruzno-outdorova-aktivita-kruzno/).

### Inferences
- The likely creator and initial commercial rights holder for the main Kruzno game is Mark W. Palko. This is supported by his own authorship/sales statements and the official store, but it does not prove the current ownership of every copyright, design, or contract right.
- The roadmap’s linked article and the main game need reconciliation: “Kruzno Checkers” is explicitly a separate variant, whereas the Wikipedia entry describes the main Kruzno game with three chess-piece types and non-transitive capture.

### Gaps
- I found no signed assignment, company ownership record, or other chain-of-title document identifying who currently controls all adaptation rights.
- The official site’s contact page could not be retrieved by the research browser. No one was contacted.

## Permission and current rights

### Takeaway

No license or written permission for a digital adaptation was found. Palko’s public rules posting and the official site’s purchase/make-yourself language do not grant permission to create or publish a faithful digital game.

### Cited Findings
- Palko’s 2014 post publicly provides rules while stating he developed and sold Kruzno; it contains no digital adaptation permission or license terms in the accessible text — [Palko, “Rules of Kruzno — updated”](https://youdothemathkthrucalculus.blogspot.com/2014/12/).
- Kruzno.com’s accessible home, store, and Kruzno Checkers rules pages provide game information and sales/materials, but no digital-use grant or software license was located on those pages — [Kruzno official site](https://www.kruzno.com/index.html), [Game Store](https://www.kruzno.com/stored.html), [Kruzno Checkers rules](https://www.kruzno.com/hexcheckers.html).
- Trademark database mirrors report that Mark W. Palko filed a KRUZNO mark for board games in 2014, registered in 2015, and that the registration was cancelled in January 2022 for failure to file continued-use documents — [TrademarkElite record for USPTO serial 86442010](https://www.trademarkelite.com/trademark/trademark-detail/86442010/KRUZNO). The USPTO says TSDR is its system for checking application/registration status and documents — [USPTO: checking trademark status](https://www.uspto.gov/trademarks/apply/check-status-view-documents).
- A 2006 KRUZNO application by Palko appears in trademark-index search results as abandoned in 2007 — [Justia filing-date index](https://trademarks.justia.com/browse-by-date/2006/june/22/27.html). These database records are U.S.-specific and do not establish the status of unregistered/common-law uses or rights in other territories.

### Inferences
- The 2022 cancellation removes that U.S. federal registration as an active registration according to the secondary record, but it is not global name clearance and does not prove that all trademark rights ended.
- Public availability of rules is not itself an open-source or digital-adaptation license. No game-specific license was stated on the primary pages reviewed.

### Gaps
- I could not retrieve the USPTO file record itself through the browser’s JavaScript interface, so the registration status and record history rely on the linked trademark database mirror; the official TSDR status must be checked directly before public naming.
- The current copyright/design owner and any later assignment are unknown. No permission request was sent.

## What an independent implementation can and cannot reuse

### Takeaway

General IP guidance distinguishes a game’s mechanics from its expressive materials, but this does not create an affirmative license for this project or establish worldwide clearance. The repository’s `AGENTS.md` requires permission or an applicable license covering a faithful adaptation before implementation; no such grant was found.

### Cited Findings
- The British Library Business & IP Centre guide says the exact wording of game instructions and original board/piece drawings may be protected by copyright, while describing the game’s ideas behind its rules separately; it also notes designs and trademarks can protect visual appearance and names — [British Library BIPC, “Intellectual Property Guide: Board Games” (updated October 2025)](https://www.bl.uk/files/v5dwkion/production/2d9a4f4d3d5ad6973353ff1732819fba62d701ef.pdf/bipc-ip-guide-board-games.pdf?dl=).
- The German Patent and Trade Mark Office says a game consisting only of rules or a new board division is not patentable as a technical invention, and explains that names, designs and copyright are separate questions — [DPMA FAQ: protecting a game](https://www.deutsches-patentamt.de/english/services/customer_care_services/faq/index.html).

### Inferences
- Even if a rules-only implementation can be legally made in some jurisdictions, that general proposition is not a license from Palko and does not establish that a faithful digital adaptation meets the repository’s stricter gate.
- If rights clearance is later established, independently write every rules/tutor string, create new board and piece artwork, do not copy official diagrams or visual branding, and clear the title separately before public use.

### Gaps
- These general sources do not determine all applicable law for a globally available online game, and the current rights holder has not been confirmed.
- No applicable license, waiver, or written permission covering digital adaptation was found. Under repo `AGENTS.md`, recommendation is to block implementation until that is established or the owner changes the documented rights gate.
