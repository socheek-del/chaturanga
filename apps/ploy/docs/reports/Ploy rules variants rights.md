# Select Ploy’s Core Rules and Rights

Use **Frank Thibault’s 1970 3M Ploy rules** as the best-supported baseline, with two-player play as the main product mode and no claim that it is the most popular. The original manual also documents four-player free-for-all and partnership modes, making those authentic multiplayer candidates. The core game uses a 9×9-point board, 15 directional pieces per side, and alternating movement or rotation actions; a Shield may move and rotate in one turn. Captures occur by landing on an opposing piece, and a player wins by capturing the enemy Commander or all other opposing pieces. However, no Ploy-specific ownership chain or digital-adaptation permission was found. Under the repository’s rights gate, implementation must wait for rights clearance or an applicable license; Hasbro’s general digital policy does not prove that it owns Ploy.

## The 1970 3M manual supports two-player and four-player play

The scanned English manual is marked 1970 by 3M, credits the two-player opening to Green, and describes both two-player and four-player play. An *Abstract Games* account identifies Frank Thibault as designer, dates the game to 1970, and recommends two-player Ploy as “the main version.” This is an editor’s characterization of the principal form, not evidence that it outdraws the other modes in sales or play. Choose two-player for the first implementation if rights clear, and preserve the four-player modes as separate original rule options. ([3M, *How to Play Ploy* (1970 manual scan)](https://spellenlab.be/media/files/7c3fa0cf9e586530107f8bfbc644cc9c6155cddd.pdf), [*Abstract Games*, Issue 6, Summer 2001](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_6.pdf))

The two-player game has **one Commander, six Lances, five Probes, and three Shields per player**. Its 9×9-point board connects circular intersections with horizontal, vertical, and diagonal paths; the original diagram specifies each piece’s starting orientation. A Commander travels one intersection, a Lance one to three, a Probe one or two, and a Shield one. Pieces move only in directions marked by their indicators and along connected board paths. A player can instead rotate a piece’s facing; the Shield alone may combine a movement and rotation in a turn. The manual’s setup diagram should be transcribed into a fixture rather than reconstructed by eye. ([3M manual](https://spellenlab.be/media/files/7c3fa0cf9e586530107f8bfbc644cc9c6155cddd.pdf))

## The original four-seat modes are valid multiplayer candidates

The 3M manual provides distinct diagrams for four-player individual and partnership games. Each seat has nine pieces: one Commander, two Lances, three Probes, and three Shields. The four-player individual mode begins with Green and proceeds clockwise. If a Commander is captured, that player exits and the captor takes control of the defeated player’s remaining pieces; the manual also explains elimination when the non-Commander pieces are all captured. Partnership play pairs seats A/C and B/D and allows one partner to continue after the other leaves. Those mechanics make local and private online multiplayer applicable, but require seat-aware turns, elimination, ownership transfers, and tutorials beyond the two-player mode. Tensi’s later transcription preserves these formats but describes the partnership arrangement differently, so follow the original leaflet’s diagrams and seat labels. ([3M manual](https://spellenlab.be/media/files/7c3fa0cf9e586530107f8bfbc644cc9c6155cddd.pdf), [Tensi, Ploy rules and program](https://www.tensi.eu/thomas/programming/games/ploy/ploy_doc-english.html))

## Adoption records do not prove a popularity winner

Ploy appeared in the 3M Bookshelf line in 1970 and was republished by Schmidt in 1982 under the title *Imperium*. A specialist 2001 account says that Ploy was out of print and names two-player as its main version. The Luding catalog lists editions for two to four players. These records establish publication, reissue, and continuing catalog interest; they do not compare total participation across player counts or editions. BoardGameGeek showed 418 ratings and 228 comments on 2026-11-26, but that self-selected site activity is not a representative player count or sales measure. The defensible product choice is therefore “best-supported two-player default,” not “most popular variant.” ([*Abstract Games*, Issue 6](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_6.pdf), [Luding: Ploy](https://www.luding.org/cgi-bin/GameData.py/ENgameid/93), [BoardGameGeek: Ploy](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/1591/ploy))

The surviving English scan does not state the allowed increment for a rotation action. A later German Ploy/Imperium leaflet says one 45° segment, while Tensi’s transcription permits any multiple of 45°; these descriptions may represent different editions. The exact relationship between the four-player partnership’s colors and seat labels is also not sufficiently clear in the transcriptions. Neither the English pages reviewed nor the notes specify no-legal-move, repetition, or draw outcomes. If work resumes, verify these details against the original 1970 diagrams and leaflet before writing an engine contract. ([3M English manual](https://spellenlab.be/media/files/7c3fa0cf9e586530107f8bfbc644cc9c6155cddd.pdf), [Schmidt Ploy/Imperium manual scan](https://www.spiele4us.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/ploy-gebraucht-g003801590.pdf), [Tensi rules transcription](https://www.tensi.eu/thomas/programming/games/ploy/ploy_doc-english.html))

## No digital-adaptation permission or Ploy-specific owner was verified

The evidence identifies Frank Thibault as designer and 3M as the 1970 publisher. 3M later sold its Bookshelf line to Avalon Hill, which Hasbro acquired, and an Avalon Hill Ploy edition exists. But these facts do not establish that Ploy-specific copyrights, marks, or adaptation rights passed through that chain; no assignment or present-day owner confirmation was found. Hasbro’s digital-content guidelines require an applicable signed license and prior written approval for licensed properties, but they do not name Ploy or show that Hasbro controls it. A historical U.S. PLOY mark is reported canceled in 1987, which does not clear the name worldwide or resolve other rights. ([The Strong, Ploy artifact](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/board-game-ploy-strategic-game-of-maneuver-and-capture-3m-company/NAE--BkKphDUIw?hl=en), [Minnesota History, 3M Bookshelf Games](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/64/v64i04back.pdf), [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines), [historical PLOY trademark record](https://trademarks.justia.com/732/26/ploy-73226844.html))

U.S. Copyright Office guidance says game procedures and methods are not protected by copyright, while expressive rules text and artwork may be. That is useful for planning independently written lessons, original board and piece art, and an original interface, but it neither resolves rights in other jurisdictions nor identifies the owner or licensing route for Ploy. No permission or license for a free, open-source digital adaptation was located. The repository therefore blocks implementation until the rights holder and chain of title are verified and written permission or an applicable license covers the intended adaptation; public branding needs separate name clearance. ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html), [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines))

## Conclusion

Ploy’s rules support a clear product structure: a two-player main mode, with four-player free-for-all and partnership as genuine extensions. The 1970 leaflet supplies enough to identify the central movement and capture model, but the missing rotation limit and unclear seat mapping would still need direct source verification before rules implementation.

The decisive dependency is rights, not game design: no evidence currently grants this project digital adaptation permission, and the likely corporate chain is not a verified chain of title. Keep Ploy blocked before implementation and continue to the next eligible roadmap entry while rights are clarified.
