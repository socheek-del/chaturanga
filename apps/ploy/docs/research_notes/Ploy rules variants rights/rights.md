# Ploy Rights Boundary

## Who created and published Ploy, and what is known about rights ownership?

### Takeaway
Ploy is a 1970 3M Bookshelf game commonly attributed to designer Frank Thibault. 3M later sold its Bookshelf line to Avalon Hill, and Avalon Hill was later acquired by Hasbro, but the available public sources do not trace Ploy-specific copyright, trademark, or adaptation rights through those transactions to a present-day owner.

### Cited Findings
- The Strong National Museum of Play’s collection entry identifies the artifact as *Ploy: Strategic Game of Maneuver and Capture*, attributes the object to 3M Company, and dates it to 1970. [The Strong, via Google Arts & Culture](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/board-game-ploy-strategic-game-of-maneuver-and-capture-3m-company/NAE--BkKphDUIw?hl=en)
- A contemporaneous game-history article credits Frank Thibault as Ploy’s inventor and says it entered the 3M line in 1970. [Abstract Games, issue 6](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_6.pdf)
- A later interview also describes Thibault as Ploy’s inventor, but says nothing about his Ploy rights assignment to 3M or any later transfer. [The Big Game Hunter: Frank Thibault](https://thebiggamehunter.com/inventors/game-designers/thibault-frank/)
- The Minnesota Historical Society says 3M sold its Bookshelf line to Avalon Hill in 1976 and Avalon Hill was purchased by Hasbro in 1998. The article does not identify which Ploy-specific rights, if any, transferred in either transaction. [Minnesota History, “3M Bookshelf Game Series”](https://storage.googleapis.com/mnhs-org-support/mn_history_articles/64/v64i04back.pdf)
- A collector/archive record lists a 1977 Avalon Hill Ploy edition/revision after the 3M release. This supports continued publication by Avalon Hill, but is not a contract or chain-of-title record. [Avalon Hill Company Index, transcribed at BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/wiki/page/Avalon_Hill_Company_Index%2C_1952-1980)

### Inferences
- Frank Thibault’s authorship and 3M’s publication are well supported, but neither fact by itself establishes who currently owns any expressive materials or whether Ploy was among the specific assets transferred onward.
- Hasbro is a possible party to investigate because it acquired Avalon Hill, but the collection-level acquisition history does not prove Hasbro owns or controls Ploy-specific rights.

### Gaps
- No assignment, license, acquisition schedule, or present-day rights-holder confirmation for Ploy was located.
- Public sources conflict on the date 3M sold its game line (some report 1974; the Minnesota Historical Society reports 1976). The precise transaction terms and their treatment of Ploy were not found.

## Is there permission or a license for a digital adaptation?

### Takeaway
No digital-adaptation license or written permission for Ploy was located. Hasbro’s digital-content rules require an applicable license agreement and prior written approval, but they do not establish that Hasbro owns Ploy or that a Ploy license is available.

### Cited Findings
- Hasbro’s official digital guidelines state that the rules govern digital use of Licensed Property subject to an applicable License Agreement, and require prior written approval for digital content. They also say that, absent an express license grant, licensees have no right to use Licensed Property in interactive or digital games. [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines)
- The guidelines are expressly conditioned on an existing applicable license agreement; they do not identify Ploy as Hasbro property or grant permission to third parties. [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines)
- The 3M site’s current IP page reserves rights in content displayed through its current website, but it does not identify Ploy or speak to ownership of the historical game. [3M Legal, Copyright & Property Rights](https://www.3m.com/3M/en_US/company-us/legal-information/)
- A historical U.S. PLOY word-mark registration for board-game equipment is reported as cancelled under Section 8 in 1987. This is a secondary transcription of the public trademark record, not a current comprehensive trademark search. [Justia trademark record, Serial No. 73226844](https://trademarks.justia.com/732/26/ploy-73226844.html); [USPTO TSDR search](https://tsdr.uspto.gov/#caseNumber=73226844&caseSearchType=US_APPLICATION&caseType=DEFAULT&searchType=statusSearch)

### Inferences
- The Hasbro guidelines become relevant only if Hasbro or its licensor confirms it controls Ploy rights and is willing to license the intended use. The owner and licensing route must be verified first.
- A cancelled U.S. registration does not establish that the name is clear in every territory, that no common-law mark exists, or that copyrights in rules text/art have expired.

### Gaps
- No permission or license for a free, open-source, multiplayer digital adaptation was found.
- The current rights holder, applicable licensing contact, territorial scope, and any terms allowing GPL-3.0 distribution remain unknown.

## What can be independently authored, and what must be cleared?

### Takeaway
U.S. Copyright Office guidance says game ideas, titles, and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while sufficiently original rules text and visual artwork can be protected. This U.S.-specific distinction does not establish worldwide clearance or override the repository’s requirement to document permission or an applicable license before implementing a newly published game.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office says a game’s idea, name/title, and methods of play are not protected by copyright; rule text and graphic art may be copyrightable. [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The Office’s Compendium says copyright does not protect game ideas, titles, procedures, processes, or methods of operation, and protects only particular expression in literary/artistic elements. [U.S. Copyright Office Compendium, Chapter 700](https://www.copyright.gov/comp3/chap700/ch700-literary-works.pdf)
- Hasbro’s guidelines treat Licensed Property broadly, including gameplay elements, adaptations, marks, names, logos, and look-and-feel; licensed uses require a signed license and approvals. [Hasbro Digital Content Guidelines](https://docs.hasbro.com/en-us/legal/DigitalContentGuidelines)

### Inferences
- If a future implementation is authorized, write all rules prose and tutorial material from independently verified facts, create original board/piece art and interface, and avoid 3M/Avalon Hill packaging, logos, character imagery, or distinctive product trade dress.
- The U.S. copyright rules provide a potential basis for independently implementing gameplay methods in the United States, but do not establish a safe, worldwide route for this project or satisfy its stricter repository gate by themselves.

### Gaps
- No global trademark/design-right review was performed. The current state of Ploy-related marks outside the single historical U.S. registration is unknown.
- No Ploy-specific registration/assignment search for copyrights, designs, contracts, or publisher agreements was completed; the available sources do not identify a comprehensive public record for those rights.
- Written rights clearance or confirmation that an applicable license/public-domain status covers this faithful digital project is needed before implementation; separate title/brand clearance is needed before public branding.
