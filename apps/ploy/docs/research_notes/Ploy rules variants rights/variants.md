# Ploy Editions and Adoption

## Which edition and player count should be selected?

### Takeaway

Use Frank Thibault’s 1970 3M Bookshelf Game rules as the base edition. The original rule sheet supports two-player and four-player play, including individual and partnership formats. A specialist abstract-games editor explicitly recommended treating two-player Ploy as the main version; use that as the default ruleset, without claiming it is objectively the most popular. The four-player formats are documented original modes and should remain a multiplayer implementation candidate.

### Cited Findings

- Ploy was designed by Frank Thibault and added to 3M’s Bookshelf Games in 1970. It was later republished by Schmidt Spiel + Freizeit in 1982; it was out of print by the time of this 2001 account. — [Abstract Games, Issue 6, Summer 2001](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_6.pdf)
- The reproduced 3M rule sheet says the game supports 2 or 4 players, provides a separate two-player setup, a four-player individual setup, and a partnership setup. It bears the notice “Copyright © 1970 by 3M Company.” — [1970 Ploy rule sheet scan](https://spellenlab.be/media/files/7c3fa0cf9e586530107f8bfbc644cc9c6155cddd.pdf)
- The 2001 specialist account says Ploy is for two or four players and recommends the two-player game as “the main version.” The statement is the editor’s assessment, not measured adoption data. — [Abstract Games, Issue 6, Summer 2001](https://www.abstractgames.org/uploads/1/1/6/4/116462923/abstract_games_issue_6.pdf)
- The Luding catalog lists Ploy as a 1970 Frank Thibault / 3M game and a 1982 Schmidt edition. Its entry lists 2–4 players. — [Luding: Ploy](https://www.luding.org/cgi-bin/GameData.py/ENgameid/93)
- The 1982 Schmidt publication is also titled *Imperium* in a surviving Schmidt instruction scan; this is a republication/name change, not a separately evidenced rules system. — [Schmidt “Imperium Ploy” instruction scan](https://www.spiele4us.de/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/ploy-gebraucht-g003801590.pdf)

### Inferences

- A product should launch with the standard two-player setup and rules as the primary experience, while the original 3M four-player individual and partnership modes are applicable extensions for eventual multiplayer support. The sources describe them as modes of the same game, not as competing editions.
- “Most popular” cannot be assigned to two-player versus four-player from the available records. Choosing two-player is a best-supported product default because a specialist editor singled it out as the main version, not because comparative play data prove it dominates.

### Gaps

- No original sales, print-run, tournament participation, or longitudinal player-count data comparing two-player, four-player individual, and partnership formats were found.
- The 2001 specialist source names a longer 1975 article by David Wells in *Modern Board Games*, but that full article was not inspected in this track.

## What does the available adoption evidence establish?

### Takeaway

Ploy was distributed across more than one publisher and is still represented in collector and hobby databases, but this establishes historical publication and continuing niche interest only. No reliable evidence establishes that it was more popular than another version or game.

### Cited Findings

- BoardGameGeek’s page showed 418 ratings, 228 comments, 38 images, a user rating of 6.3, and ranks of 9,314 overall / 425 abstract when checked on 2026-11-26. These are self-selected site activity and ranking measures, not sales, total players, or representative popularity data. — [BoardGameGeek: Ploy](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/1591/ploy)
- The Strong National Museum of Play records a 1970 3M Ploy artifact (object 110.11066). Its page says 3M’s broader Bookshelf Games were made from 1961–1976 and notes their present-day status as collectibles in the board-game community; it supplies no Ploy sales or player totals. — [The Strong, via Google Arts & Culture: Ploy](https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/board-game-ploy-strategic-game-of-maneuver-and-capture-3m-company/NAE--BkKphDUIw?hl=en)
- The AGPI Archives catalog records a 1970 3M Bookshelf Game edition and links to museum and collection records, including a Canadian Museum of History artifact. Catalog presence demonstrates preservation/collection, not popularity. — [AGPI Archives: Ploy](https://gamecatalog.org/game/28602)

### Inferences

- Ploy had enough commercial and collector life to be republished under Schmidt, and it retains a visible hobby audience. The data are too narrow and biased to support any popularity ranking among its modes or editions.

### Gaps

- Public comparative sales, print run, geographic reach, recurring tournament, or active-player statistics were not located. BoardGameGeek and collection counts cannot fill those gaps.
