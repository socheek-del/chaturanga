# Rithmomachia variant selection

## Decision

The product follows the **common medieval/Renaissance rules** identified as the “most common
version” in the linked Wikipedia Gameplay section. Its article distinguishes these rules from
Fulke's 16th-century variant. Historical sources describe substantial differences between
teachers and editions, and they do not provide comparable player counts. The selection uses the
best available popularity signal; it is not a measured popularity ranking.

This replaces the earlier provisional choice of “Selenus 1616 reconstruction.” Selenus-based
Mebben rules remain a comparison source only: their movement pattern differs from the selected
common version (orthogonal circles, diagonal triangles, all-direction squares).

## Supporting evidence

- Wikipedia says the rules immediately below describe the most common version, played through
  much of the Middle Ages and Renaissance; it separately notes Fulke's 16th-century variant.
- [Wikipedia, Gameplay](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rithmomachia#Gameplay) gives the selected
  movement family and the meeting, assault, ambuscade, and siege captures.
- [University of Cape Town: *Rhythmomachia and its Rules*](https://open.uct.ac.za/server/api/core/bitstreams/82db129d-89a2-4f1a-8a8a-de0f5df55724/content)
  records an 8×16 board, a 24-square army setup, numeric values, and historical variations.
- [American Mathematical Society: *The Battle of Numbers*](https://mathvoices.ams.org/featurecolumn/2021/07/01/the-battle-of-numbers/)
  provides the even/odd number tables, pyramids, and comparison with Lever/Fulke movement and
  capture rules.
- [Mebben: Selenus reconstruction](https://www.jducoeur.org/game-hist/mebben.ryth.html)
  expressly describes a 17th-century movement family, which is not the selected default.
