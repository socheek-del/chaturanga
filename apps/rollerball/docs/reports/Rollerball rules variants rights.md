# Trace Rollerball’s Rules, Board, and Rights

The roadmap entry is **Jean-Louis Cazaux’s 40-square Rollerball chess**, not the unrelated 1975 Invicta tilt-maze game. Pritchard’s 2007 encyclopedia dates the variant to 1998, while a later transcription says 1999. It has one identifiable ruleset, and the scattered reference-book, software, and student-project mentions do not establish a popularity winner. The 7×7 board loses its central 3×3, leaving two rings where a piece’s location determines its movement paths and rebounds. Checkmate and a clockwise King race can win; stalemate and threefold repetition draw. The creator’s original rules page was unavailable, the setup transcription is derivative, and no digital-adaptation license surfaced, so the repository’s rights gate blocks implementation pending clearance. ([Pritchard, *Classified Encyclopedia of Chess Variants*, entry 16.3](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc/138146.pdf), [Cazaux’s Chess Variant Pages entry](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html), [French full-rules PDF](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/Rollerball_Fr.pdf))

## The roadmap entry is Cazaux’s 40-square chess game

Pritchard’s encyclopedia attributes Rollerball to Cazaux and dates it to 1998. The named Chess Variant Pages entry is the underlying rules source; another rules transcription gives 1999. Keep the date discrepancy visible, but identify the game by its creator and 40-square board. A similarly named 1975 Invicta game is a tilt-maze dexterity game, not chess. ([Pritchard, chapter 16](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc/138146.pdf), [Chess Variant Pages: Rollerball](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html), [Moddable rules transcription](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/), [BoardGameGeek: Roller Ball](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/338743/roller-ball))

Sources identify one Cazaux ruleset rather than competing editions. Pritchard’s entry, the creator-attributed rules page, a 2011 explanation, and later digital implementations describe the same 40-square game. This shows circulation but provides no tournament base, player counts, or comparative adoption measures, so neither “most popular” nor a current popularity ranking is supportable. ([Pritchard, chapter 16](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc/138146.pdf), [Abstract Strategy Games, “Rollerball Chess”](https://abstractstrategygames.blogspot.com/2011/01/arena-chess.html), [JoclyBoard supported games](https://github.com/jcfrog/joclyboard/blob/master/README.md), [CityUHK thesis](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/oaps/cs2019-4514-cky542.pdf))

## Two rings shape the forty playable squares

The board is a 7×7 grid with the central nine squares removed: a **24-square outer ring** surrounds a **16-square inner ring**. Each player starts with a King, Bishop, two Rooks, and two Pawns. The transcribed array is rotationally symmetric, but it comes from a later transcription of Cazaux’s diagram; check it against the creator’s original image before turning it into an engine fixture. ([Moddable rules transcription](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/), [French full-rules PDF](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/Rollerball_Fr.pdf))

Movement depends on the ring and direction of travel. Rooks slide along ring-specific orthogonal paths, with a single 90° rebound available at an outer corner; Bishops move forward diagonally and may rebound once at an inner or outer edge. The King moves one square in any direction and cannot castle. Pawns move one step forward on their ring or diagonally toward either ring, capture by displacement, and promote on either opposing Pawn’s starting square; they have no initial double-step. Because the accessible descriptions do not formally define the board graph or every rebound route, geometry needs fixture-level verification before implementation. ([French full-rules PDF](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/Rollerball_Fr.pdf), [Moddable rules transcription](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/))

## The King race depends on its route

Checkmate wins, as does moving a King to the opposing King’s starting square **after travelling clockwise**. Stalemate and threefold repetition are draws. The clockwise-race wording is not formalized in the sources: it could refer to the approach side, completing a circuit, or another path condition. A faithful engine would need to preserve enough move history to evaluate that route, and the original page should be checked before choosing the exact interpretation. The reviewed rules do not specify other draw rules, so orthodox move-count or insufficient-material rules should not be imported without evidence. ([French full-rules PDF](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/Rollerball_Fr.pdf), [Moddable rules transcription](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/))

## No digital-adaptation permission surfaced

Cazaux is the likely rules contact, but no public license, written digital-adaptation permission, or rights assignment was found. The original Chess Variant Pages page could not be inspected for its footer or page-level terms. General U.S. and EU guidance distinguishes game mechanics from expressive rules text and art, but it does not grant a license or establish global clearance. Even if mechanics-only implementation has a legal basis in some jurisdictions, `AGENTS.md` requires permission or an applicable license before scheduling the faithful digital project. The title also references the 1975 film, so public product naming needs a separate review; do not use film imagery or imply affiliation. ## Conclusion

Rollerball’s rules are coherent enough to identify the intended variant, but the inaccessible primary page leaves source verification for the setup and King-race condition outstanding. More decisively, no adaptation permission or applicable license is documented. Keep implementation blocked until rights are cleared; if it clears, verify the original diagram and movement examples, define the clockwise path condition from an authoritative source, and author all app text and artwork independently.
