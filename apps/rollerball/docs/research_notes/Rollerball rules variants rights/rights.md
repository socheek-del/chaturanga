# Rollerball digital-use rights

## Identity and attribution

### Takeaway
The roadmap's Rollerball is Jean-Louis Cazaux's 1998/1999 chess variant, submitted to the Chess Variant Pages 40-squares contest. It is a separate abstract chess game inspired by the 1975 film; I found no evidence that the variant was licensed by the film owner or published commercially by a separate game publisher.

### Cited Findings
- The Chess Variant Pages entry is attributed to Jean-Louis Cazaux and describes the game as an entry in the 40-squares contest; a secondary rules library dates it 1999 and links the original page: [Chess Variant Pages: Rollerball](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html), [Moddable rules library](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/).
- Cazaux's publisher/author profile identifies him as the inventor of Rollerball chess: [Lulu author profile](https://www.lulu.com/spotlight/cazaux).
- A 2011 rules write-up identifies Rollerball as Cazaux's creation and describes the 40-square, 7×7-minus-centre board: [Abstract Strategy Games: Rollerball Chess](https://abstractstrategygames.blogspot.com/2011/01/arena-chess.html).
- The name is borrowed from/inspired by the 1975 science-fiction film in the variant descriptions; those sources do not indicate any licensed relationship: [Moddable rules library](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/), [Cazaux's Rollerball page mirrored in French](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/rollerball.html).

### Inferences
- Cazaux is the relevant first contact for a game-specific license unless he identifies an assignee or successor; no separate Rollerball publisher or rights transfer surfaced in the sources reviewed.
- The variant's inspiration from a film does not establish that the film's owner controls Cazaux's rules, and the variant attribution does not establish permission to use the film's name, imagery, characters, logos, or other expressive material.

### Gaps
- The Chess Variant Pages URL was not fetchable during this review, so I could not inspect its footer, page-level notice, or any linked license. Search results and independent citations identify the page and author, but cannot prove what rights language appears on the page itself.
- No assignment, publisher agreement, registration, or current rights declaration for Rollerball was located. No rights-holder or film-owner contact was made.

## Digital-adaptation permission

### Takeaway
I found no public license or written permission that covers a faithful digital Rollerball adaptation. The fact that Cazaux's rules are publicly viewable or playable in other software is not itself an adaptation license.

### Cited Findings
- The original rules page is presented as a rules-and-diagram publication, with no license surfaced in search or the secondary references reviewed: [Chess Variant Pages: Rollerball](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html), [Moddable rules library](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/).
- Cazaux's 2024 co-authored *More Chess & More Than Chess* page lists the book as “All Rights Reserved”; that reservation covers the book's expression and does not itself resolve ownership of the underlying game mechanics: [Lulu, *More Chess & More Than Chess*](https://www.lulu.com/shop/jean-louis-cazaux-and-harm-geert-muller-and-paul-rapoport/more-chess-more-than-chess/paperback/product-e7py22e.html).
- The U.S. Copyright Office states that a game's idea, title, and method of play are not copyright-protected, while sufficiently original rules text and board art may be: [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html).
- The European Commission's IP Helpdesk likewise distinguishes unprotectable mechanics from copyrightable rule manuals and other expression, while describing the limits of its discussion: [EU IP Helpdesk: Board Games and IP](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/board-games-revival-and-its-intellectual-property-challenge-pt-1-2023-11-06_en).

### Inferences
- Those legal sources suggest that a clean-room implementation of mechanics may have a legal basis in some jurisdictions when the code, prose, tutorial, diagrams and art are original. They are not a global opinion on this particular variant, and they do not grant a license from Cazaux.
- The repository's `AGENTS.md` sets a narrower project gate: establish permission or an applicable license covering a faithful digital project before scheduling implementation. No such permission/license was found, so recommend **no-go before implementation** under the repo rule. Seek written clearance or document an applicable license first.

### Gaps
- Whether Cazaux intends his CVP rules page to permit digital adaptations, or whether he assigned any rights, could not be determined from public materials reviewed.
- No jurisdiction-specific review was made for the public Melon-hosted site. Do not treat U.S. or EU general guidance as worldwide clearance.

## Rules text, art and branding boundaries

### Takeaway
Even if an independently authored mechanics implementation becomes permissible, reuse no CVP prose, diagrams, images, book illustrations, or film artwork. Public branding needs its own name review because “Rollerball” is also the title of the film that inspired the variant.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office identifies rules descriptions and gameboard graphics as potentially protected literary/pictorial expression: [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html).
- EU IP Helpdesk guidance describes copyright protection for original game text and art and separately discusses trademarks in titles/logos; its scope is an explanatory overview, not a clearance search: [EU IP Helpdesk: Board Games and IP](https://intellectual-property-helpdesk.ec.europa.eu/news-events/news/board-games-revival-and-its-intellectual-property-challenge-pt-1-2023-11-06_en).
- The variant's published descriptions explicitly connect its name/inspiration to the 1975 film: [Moddable rules library](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/), [Cazaux-attributed rules mirror](https://www.zpag.net/Jeux/Echec/Rollerball_Fr.pdf).

### Inferences
- If the rights gate clears, write every tutorial/rules explanation independently, draw an original board and piece set, and use no imagery, logos, plot/character elements, or marketing that implies film affiliation.
- Search the intended launch territories for relevant “Rollerball” marks and seek legal/name clearance before using it as the product brand. This research did not establish infringement or ownership of a conflicting mark.

### Gaps
- No trademark database search or attorney clearance for “Rollerball” was completed.
- No permissions for Cazaux's source prose/diagrams or any film-related expression were identified.
