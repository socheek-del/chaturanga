# Rollerball Identity, Variants, and Adoption

## Which Rollerball is the roadmap entry?

### Takeaway
The intended entry is Jean-Louis Cazaux’s two-player Rollerball chess variant: a 40-square board made from a 7×7 grid with the central 3×3 removed. The strongest catalog source dates it to 1998; one later summary says 1999, so retain the late-1990s attribution and note the discrepancy.

### Cited Findings
- Pritchard’s 2007 *Classified Encyclopedia of Chess Variants* lists “Rollerball (J.-L. Cazaux, 1998)” and describes the 7×7 board minus its central nine squares, the setup, movement bends, and king-race victory. The chapter is hosted with permission by the editor of the encyclopedia: [Pritchard, chapter 16, entry 16.3](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc/138146.pdf).
- The original [Chess Variant Pages Rollerball entry](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html) is the named source in the Pritchard entry. A 2001 index snapshot lists “Rollerball Chess by Jean-Louis Cazaux” in its CVP mirror: [CVP game index snapshot](https://www.jatektan.hu/_2018_vissza/2011_ig/uj2001/isakk1.html).
- The [author’s book page](https://www.lulu.com/spotlight/cazaux) identifies Cazaux as an inventor of chess variants including Rollerball. A modern rules-library entry gives 1999 and calls it a Chess Variant Pages 40-squares contest entry: [Moddable Rollerball entry](https://rules.moddable.games/chess/variants/rollerball/).
- “Roller Ball” also names a separate 1975 Invicta tilt-maze dexterity game, listed on [BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/boardgame/338743/roller-ball). This is not the roadmap’s chess variant.

### Inferences
- Use “Jean-Louis Cazaux’s Rollerball chess variant” in research and product documentation to distinguish it from the film, sport, and same-name dexterity game.
- Prefer 1998 for the creation date because the Pritchard catalog explicitly gives that date and was based on chess-variant sources; treat 1999 as a conflicting later secondary account.

### Gaps
- I did not locate a separate published Rollerball chess edition with different rules. The exact creation/submission date within 1998–1999 is not resolved by the sources reviewed.

## Are there multiple rule variants or a demonstrated popularity winner?

### Takeaway
The sources reviewed point to one identifiable Cazaux ruleset, rather than several competing Rollerball chess editions. There is not enough evidence to call it the most popular version; the roadmap’s singular Rollerball entry is the only version identified.

### Cited Findings
- Pritchard’s 2007 entry cites the Chess Variant Pages as its source and records one rule description; it does not identify an alternate Rollerball edition or report popularity data: [Pritchard, chapter 16](https://www.jsbeasley.co.uk/encyc/138146.pdf).
- Cazaux’s CVP entry is hosted as an individual 40-square game, and the 2001 CVP index attributes it to him: [original CVP entry](https://www.chessvariants.com/40.dir/rollerball/index.html), [CVP index snapshot](https://www.jatektan.hu/_2018_vissza/2011_ig/uj2001/isakk1.html).
- A 2011 article presents Rollerball as a Cazaux creation and reproduces the same 40-square, two-player rules: [Abstract Strategy Games, “Rollerball Chess”](https://abstractstrategygames.blogspot.com/2011/01/arena-chess.html).
- A software project lists Rollerball Chess among its supported games, which establishes at least one digital implementation, not the number of players: [JoclyBoard supported games](https://github.com/jcfrog/joclyboard/blob/master/README.md).
- A 2019 City University of Hong Kong thesis documents an iOS app with a Rollerball mode and reports a student-festival demonstration; this establishes one published app/project and a small event exposure, not general usage: [CityUHK thesis](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/oaps/cs2019-4514-cky542.pdf).

### Inferences
- The sensible catalog choice is the documented Cazaux ruleset, with no “most popular” claim. A 40-square contest submission, reference-book entry, later explanatory coverage, and digital port indicate exposure but do not measure adoption.
- As search results mix the chess variant with the film and other Rollerball-titled games, title matches alone cannot serve as a popularity signal.

### Gaps
- No tournament participation, sales, representative player counts, variant-specific ratings, or other comparative popularity data were located. The cited sources document authorship and circulation, not a popularity ranking.
- No reliable evidence establishes whether the design has meaningful rule variants beyond descriptions that reproduce the same game.

## What evidence is there of sustained play?

### Takeaway
Rollerball has been catalogued and made playable digitally, but evidence found does not show sustained tournament or broad community play.

### Cited Findings
- The Chess Variant Pages hosts the game as part of its noncommercial collection; the site describes its purpose as publishing chess-variant rules and encouraging gameplay: [The Chess Variant Pages overview](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Chess_Variant_Pages).
- Cazaux’s games have been supported in the Jocly software family, including the Rollerball Chess entry in its public game list: [JoclyBoard README](https://github.com/jcfrog/joclyboard/blob/master/README.md).
- A 2011 rules post and an academic project include the same Cazaux variant, showing ongoing secondary coverage and experimentation: [2011 rules article](https://abstractstrategygames.blogspot.com/2011/01/arena-chess.html), [academic thesis](https://lbms03.cityu.edu.hk/oaps/cs2019-4514-cky542.pdf).

### Inferences
- Treat Rollerball as an established but niche published variant. Digital availability should not be confused with evidence of an active player base.

### Gaps
- The reviewed material gives no reliable usage analytics for Jocly, CVP, or the student app, and no dedicated Rollerball chess tournament or club was found.
