# Digitize Suffragetto with the archival rules

The strongest defensible default is the surviving **Sargeant Bros. second-edition, two-player Suffragetto**, attributed to the Women’s Social and Political Union (WSPU). Available sources do not establish a statistically most-played rules variant: they describe one historic game, a second edition, and one known complete surviving set, but provide no sales or circulation figures. The archival rules describe 21 pieces per side, ordinary eight-direction movement, chained hops, role-specific arrests or disabling, exchanges from Prison and Hospital, and victory by placing six pieces in the opposing building. A later digital reconstruction changes or muddies the rules for hopping over opponents and exchange consent, so it should not override the surviving edition. The board’s geometry and cell markings must be reconstructed from the board image because the rules transcription does not state a grid size. The underlying procedures can guide an original implementation, but the Bodleian’s clarity-edited transcription, scans, photographs and artwork should not be reused as product expression; this review does not establish worldwide rights clearance.

## The surviving second edition is the strongest rules anchor

The Bodleian attributes the game to the WSPU, identifies the Sargeant Bros. copy as a second edition, and dates its catalogue copy to about 1917. A specialist Games Board Association catalogue instead dates the complete set to circa 1909 and reports a 1909 stamp on the rules sheet; an Oxford exhibition essay dates the game’s creation to about 1907. These records establish historical provenance more confidently than an exact first-publication year, and their date discrepancy remains unresolved. The Bodleian transcript itself says its rules wording was updated for clarity, so it is a useful reading aid rather than the original wording. ([Bodleian Libraries exhibit](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf), [Bodleian exhibition essay](https://www.glam.ox.ac.uk/files/shout-out-women), [Games Board Association catalogue](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples))

The existence of a second edition suggests that the game was reprinted, and the Oxford essay cautiously says this suggests popularity. That evidence is not a popularity ranking: the same essay and the catalogue describe only one known complete surviving copy, and neither reports print runs, sales, or player counts. No separate historical variant with demonstrably different rules was established in the research. The implementation should therefore call its choice the **best-documented surviving edition**, not the “most popular variant.” ([Bodleian exhibition essay](https://www.glam.ox.ac.uk/files/shout-out-women), [Games Board Association catalogue](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples))

## The setup gives each side twenty-one pieces

The original rules transcription describes two players, each with **16 small rank-and-file pieces and five larger pieces**: suffragettes in green, police in dark blue, with a Leader and four assistants on one side and an Inspector with four constables on the other. A coin toss chooses who starts. The setup places the small pieces in the marked S or P cells; one large piece occupies the middle of its front row and the other four occupy the ends of the front and second rows. The surviving board is described as a 17-by-17 square layout, with a central Arena, the House of Commons and Albert Hall goal buildings, plus Prison and Hospital holding areas and their staging grounds. The grid dimensions come from later board descriptions and reproductions, not from the rules prose, so the original board diagram must settle cell coordinates and labels. ([Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules), [Games Board Association object record and photos](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples), [board-layout description](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragetto), [Vassal module board](https://vassalengine.org/library/projects/Suffragetto))

Players alternate moving one piece. A piece can step to an empty adjacent square in any of eight directions, or hop over one occupied adjacent square to the empty square immediately beyond; a turn can continue as a chain of hops. The archival transcription says pieces of either side may be hopped. Ordinary play bars both stepping and hopping into Prison, Prison Yard, Hospital, and Hospital Grounds; suffragettes cannot enter Albert Hall, and police cannot enter the House of Commons. Once a piece reaches its opponent’s goal building, it can move within the building but cannot leave. Because the exact board mask is supplied by the surviving marked board rather than text coordinates, coordinate-level setup should be transcribed from an authorized reference image without copying that image into the product. ([Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules), [Games Board Association photos](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples))

## Arena hops create arrests, disabling, and exchanges

An opposing piece can be arrested or disabled only while it stands in the pink Arena; pieces outside it are safe even though they can still be hopped over. Rank-and-file pieces capture only with a diagonal hop, while Leaders and Inspectors can capture in any direction. A captured suffragette goes to Prison and a captured police piece goes to Hospital. A hop that passes over an opponent but does not meet the Arena and piece-direction conditions remains a move rather than a capture. These distinctions make the Arena and the large leadership pieces central to both attack and defense. ([Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules))

When both Prison and Hospital each contain at least 12 pieces, either player may insist on an exchange of up to six equal-value pieces: a Leader for an Inspector, or rank-and-file for rank-and-file. Released pieces restart from Prison Yard and Hospital Grounds, and the rules prohibit another exchange while pieces remain in either staging area. The original transcript gives the eligible player the right to insist; a 2016 online reconstruction adds opponent consent, a clause absent from that transcription. The archival edition should control this choice. The transcript does not spell out the order for selecting several exchanged pieces or exactly when an arrested piece leaves the board during a multi-hop chain; a digital engine should choose and document deterministic conventions for those cases. ([Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules), [2016 online reconstruction](https://suffrajitsu.com/play-suffragetto-online-the-1909-suffragettes-vs-police-board-game-meets-the-digital-age))

## Six pieces in the opposing building end the game

The rules give victory to the first player to introduce six pieces into the opponent’s building. Each goal building is described as containing six cells, so the natural reading is that the player must fill all six spaces; the text does not separately phrase the condition as “occupy every cell.” The original transcript and the reproduced board layout support this reading, but the building’s relationship to the pink Arena should be checked against the original board image because Arena pieces are capturable. No original rule was found for repetition, immobility, agreed draws, or a turn limit. If the product needs terminal behavior for these cases, it should label that behavior as a digital convention instead of presenting it as historical fact. ([Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules), [board-layout description](https://de.wikipedia.org/wiki/Suffragetto))

A 2016 reconstruction preserves the two-player, 21-piece setup, exchanges, holding areas and six-piece objective, but its wording conflicts with the archival transcript: it says ordinary hops pass over friendly pieces while also describing opponent-piece hops for arrest or disabling, and it makes exchanges conditional on both players’ agreement. The archival transcript expressly permits hopping pieces of either side and says either player may insist on an eligible exchange. The later page calls itself “closely based” on the historic game rather than an authoritative new edition, so use it as corroboration only where it agrees with the second-edition transcript. ([2016 online reconstruction](https://suffrajitsu.com/play-suffragetto-online-the-1909-suffragettes-vs-police-board-game-meets-the-digital-age), [Original rules transcription](https://www.scribd.com/document/486464154/Suffragetto-Rules))

## Historical procedures do not grant image or text rights

The U.S. Copyright Office explains that game ideas, titles and methods of play are not protected by U.S. copyright, while sufficiently expressive rules text, board art and container art can be. UK guidance gives different duration rules depending on authorship, publication history and work type, including a separate term for a published edition’s typographical arrangement. Because the original author, precise publication history and rights status of each component have not been established, the records reviewed do not support a worldwide public-domain claim. ([U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html), [UK Intellectual Property Office: Copyright duration](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term), [U.S. Copyright Office: Copyright duration FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-duration.html))

The Bodleian’s displayed transcript is marked © 2018 and says it updates the original rules for clarity. The library also requires separate permission for non-academic reproduction of its collection images. The safe project boundary is to implement the historically described procedures in independently written rules and tutorial text, redraw the board and create original pieces, illustrations and branding, and avoid collection scans or photography unless licensed. This scoped review did not search trademark records, U.S. registrations or renewals, or every jurisdiction’s term rules; it is a product boundary, not legal clearance. ([Bodleian Libraries exhibit](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf), [Bodleian Libraries: Reproducing images](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/about/work-with-us/imaging-services/copyright-and-reproducing-images), [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html))

## Conclusion

The second-edition rules offer enough structure for a faithful digital game, but not a complete software specification. The next engineering decision should be to reproduce the board mask from the archival board reference and explicitly label edge-case conventions—especially removal during chained hops, simultaneous goal completion, repetition, and no-legal-move endings—before implementing the Variant. The popularity evidence is too thin to justify a stronger claim than “surviving, best-documented edition.”
