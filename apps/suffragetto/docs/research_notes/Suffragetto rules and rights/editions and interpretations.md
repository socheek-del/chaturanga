# Suffragetto editions and interpretations

## What published editions are documented?

### Takeaway

The surviving evidence describes one Suffragetto game made for two players, with a Sargeant Bros second-edition rules sheet and one known complete surviving set. The source record does not establish distinct gameplay variants or exact first-publication year.

### Cited Findings

- The Oxford-hosted transcript is explicitly presented as rules from a second edition by the Women’s Social and Political Union and Sargeant Bros.; the transcript says its wording was updated for clarity. It identifies the title as a two-player game. [Bodleian Libraries, Suffrage in a Box source scan](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)
- The GARD catalogue record dates the complete Sargeant Bros. set to circa 1909, describes its two sets of 21 pieces and second-edition copyright rules, and says this is the only known copy. Its note says the rules sheet was stamped 1909. [GARD, Suffragetto object record](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples)
- An Oxford University exhibition essay dates the game’s creation to about 1907, calls the extant object a second edition, and says this suggests it was popular; the same essay says it is the only known complete copy. [Bodleian Libraries, Shout Out to Women](https://www.glam.ox.ac.uk/files/shout-out-women)
- The GARD record’s “circa 1909” and the Oxford essay’s “about 1907” do not pin an exact first-edition year. [GARD object record](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples); [Oxford exhibition essay](https://www.glam.ox.ac.uk/files/shout-out-women)

### Inferences

- The “second edition” is evidence of at least one earlier printing, but the sources examined do not show that the second edition had different rules from the first.
- The best reproducible historic default is the extant Sargeant Bros. second-edition rules, using the Oxford transcript as a clarity-edited transcription and the surviving board diagram.
- Current print-and-play assets reproduce the traditional 21-piece-per-side setup; their existence does not document another historic rules edition. [Suffragetto, Print Your Own](https://playsuffragetto.org/print)

### Gaps

- Exact date of the first edition, edition-specific rule changes, print run, and sales totals were not established by the records found.
- The Oxford transcript’s bibliographic date line is not clear enough in text extraction to settle the first publication year.

## Do later reconstructions create gameplay variants?

### Takeaway

A 2016 digital rules page says its game is “closely based” on the original and modernizes or paraphrases the rules. Its wording conflicts with the Oxford transcript on what a hop may pass over, so it should not replace the documented second-edition rules as the implementation authority.

### Cited Findings

- The Oxford transcript says a piece may hop over any piece, including its own, subject to legal landing squares; it separately limits rank-and-file capture/disabling to diagonal hops, while leaders/inspectors can capture in any direction. [Bodleian Libraries rules transcript](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)
- Tony Wolf’s 2016 rules page explicitly calls its digital game “closely based” on the 1909 game. It describes ordinary hops as passing over one’s own piece, but elsewhere discusses hopping over an opponent’s piece to arrest or disable it; the phrasing is internally unclear beside the Oxford transcript. The page also says its former Tabletop Zen online host is no longer operational. [Suffrajitsu, Play Suffragetto Online](https://suffrajitsu.com/play-suffragetto-online-the-1909-suffragettes-vs-police-board-game-meets-the-digital-age/)
- The 2016 page retains the two-player, 21-piece sides, six-piece base victory condition, prison/hospital, leaders/inspectors, and exchange mechanic from the Oxford transcript. [Suffrajitsu online rules](https://suffrajitsu.com/play-suffragetto-online-the-1909-suffragettes-vs-police-board-game-meets-the-digital-age/); [Bodleian Libraries rules transcript](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)
- A modern print kit offers 16 small and five large green pieces and 16 small and five large blue pieces, matching the documented historic piece counts. [Suffragetto, Print Your Own](https://playsuffragetto.org/print)

### Inferences

- The online version is best treated as a reconstruction of the original title, not as evidence that a different historic variant became standard.
- For code, resolve the hop wording from the Oxford second-edition transcript, and record any engine convention needed where the scan remains ambiguous. Do not silently import the 2016 paraphrase.

### Gaps

- The digital implementation’s complete original board/rules provenance and any intentional changes are not fully documented on the modern print page.
- No second historic rule sheet was found for a move-by-move comparison of first and second editions.

## Is one edition demonstrably the most popular?

### Takeaway

Popularity cannot be ranked across variants because the evidence identifies one historic game and provides no sales, circulation, or usage counts. The Oxford curator’s statement that a second edition “suggests” popularity supports only a cautious historical inference, not a claim that it was the most popular Suffragetto rules variant.

### Cited Findings

- Oxford’s exhibition essay says the second edition suggests the game was popular, but also says only one complete copy is known. [Bodleian Libraries, Shout Out to Women](https://www.glam.ox.ac.uk/files/shout-out-women)
- The GARD catalogue identifies the surviving rules sheet as second edition and the Sargeant Bros. set as the only known copy, without a print-run or sales figure. [GARD, Suffragetto object record](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples)
- The 2016 page establishes that a later online reconstruction existed and was used for two-player remote play, but it does not report adoption figures and notes that its host is no longer operational. [Suffrajitsu online rules](https://suffrajitsu.com/play-suffragetto-online-the-1909-suffragettes-vs-police-board-game-meets-the-digital-age/)

### Inferences

- Select the source-backed Sargeant Bros. second-edition rules as the one documented edition; describe it as the surviving, best-documented form rather than a statistically proven popularity winner.
- The second-edition label makes earlier printing plausible, but cannot establish how many players or editions existed.

### Gaps

- No reliable comparative popularity data, sales records, print-run counts, or evidence of multiple substantially different Suffragetto rules variants was found.
