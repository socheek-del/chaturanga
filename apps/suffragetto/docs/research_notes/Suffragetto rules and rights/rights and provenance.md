# Rights and provenance

## Original provenance and source artifacts

### Takeaway
Suffragetto is attributed by the Bodleian Libraries to the Women’s Social and Political Union (WSPU); Sargeant Bros is associated with manufacture/publication of the surviving edition. The date record conflicts: Bodleian’s collection list dates its Sargeant copy to 1917 and its exhibit calls it a second edition, while a specialist game catalogue records c.1909 and a rule-sheet copyright stamp from 1909.

### Cited Findings
- The Bodleian’s “Suffrage in a Box” material labels its copy “Created by the Women’s Social and Political Union,” “ce. 1917 2nd ed.,” and “Sargeant Bros”; it says the displayed transcription’s rules “have been updated for clarity.” The same PDF marks the transcription and its collection reference © Bodleian Libraries, University of Oxford, 2018. [Bodleian Libraries exhibit PDF](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)
- The Bodleian John Johnson Collection’s Ballam catalogue lists *Suffragetto*, maker “Sargeant,” date “1917,” in “1910s (31.1),(31.2).” [Bodleian Ballam collection list](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodreader/documents/media/jjcol-ballam-collection-alphabetical.pdf)
- The Games Board Association’s specialist catalogue lists “Manufacturer/Publisher Sargeant Bros,” date “c1909,” and describes the complete surviving set as including a second-edition rules sheet, with the final picture said to show a 1909 copyright stamp. This is a secondary catalogue report, not a verified transcription of the imprint. [Games Board Association catalogue](https://www.gamesboard.org.uk/cgi-pub/gardpub.cgi?command=view&pk=13815&table=examples)
- The Bodleian exhibit identifies the collection copy as a two-player game made up of 21 pieces per side and a rules sheet, and names the WSPU as its creator. [Bodleian Libraries exhibit PDF](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)

### Inferences
- Use “WSPU-created historical game” and describe Sargeant Bros as the surviving edition’s publisher/manufacturer; do not attribute individual authorship or claim a single definitive first-publication year from the conflicting records.
- The 1917 record likely describes a later or surviving second-edition copy, while the 1909 stamp/catalogue may evidence an earlier issue. The sources reviewed do not settle this distinction.

### Gaps
- The surviving object’s original imprint, copyright wording, and edition history need direct examination of the physical item or an authorized high-resolution scan; the Bodleian’s accessible 2018 transcription is expressly updated and is not the original rule sheet.
- No individual rule author, board artist, or piece designer was identified in the sources reviewed.

## Copyright term and expressive material

### Takeaway
The rules method and game title are not protected by U.S. copyright, but expressive rule text and board/container art can be. UK duration depends on work type, authorship, and publication history; the available attribution to the WSPU does not by itself resolve the authorship status of each textual or artistic element. Do not claim worldwide public-domain clearance.

### Cited Findings
- The U.S. Copyright Office says a game’s idea, name/title, and methods of play are not protected by copyright, while sufficiently expressive rules text and gameboard/container art may be copyrightable. [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)
- The UK Intellectual Property Office’s notice gives the usual term for known authors as life plus 70 years, and for unknown-authorship works generally 70 years from creation or first public availability, depending on the statutory conditions. It emphasizes that term depends on work type, author, and creation/publication facts and that exceptions can apply. [GOV.UK: Copyright duration](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term)
- UK guidance separately gives a 25-year term for typographical arrangement of a published edition. [GOV.UK: Copyright duration](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term/copyright-notice-duration-of-copyright-term)
- The U.S. Copyright Office says pre-1978 terms vary according to multiple factors; the modern 95-year/120-year rule described for anonymous/work-for-hire material is not a complete answer for every older foreign-published item. [U.S. Copyright Office: Copyright duration FAQ](https://www.copyright.gov/help/faq/faq-duration.html)
- Bodleian’s Suffragetto transcription is marked © 2018 and says the rules were updated for clarity, so it contains a later layer of expression separate from the historic game. [Bodleian Libraries exhibit PDF](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)

### Inferences
- The game’s historical procedures, board goals, and piece behavior can inform an independently written implementation; do not copy the Bodleian transcription’s prose, layout, illustrations, photographs, or redrawn board.
- A U.S. publication-year cutoff should not be applied automatically to this UK-published game: its origin and place of first publication, author status, notice, and any relevant foreign-work rules require separate analysis.
- A second-edition imprint or later scan may carry rights different from the original rules’ underlying mechanics. Treat each layer separately.

### Gaps
- This review did not establish the copyright status of every element in the 1909/1917 physical editions or the applicable result in every jurisdiction.
- No U.S. registration/renewal or U.S. publication history search was completed.
- No trademark clearance/search was completed; “not copyrightable as a title” is not a trademark conclusion.

## Safe use boundary for the product

### Takeaway
Proceed with the historically documented rules while independently creating all product expression. Do not reuse Bodleian or other collection photography/scan assets without the applicable license; public access to an image is not a reuse license.

### Cited Findings
- The Bodleian states that non-academic electronic or print reproduction of images from its collections requires its separate permission; underlying works can have their own rights, and its terms apply whether the underlying material is in copyright or public domain. [Bodleian Libraries: Reproducing images](https://www.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/about/work-with-us/imaging-services/copyright-and-reproducing-images)
- The Bodleian PDF expressly marks its transcription © Bodleian Libraries, University of Oxford, 2018. [Bodleian Libraries exhibit PDF](https://visit.bodleian.ox.ac.uk/sites/default/files/bodwhatson/documents/media/suffrage_in_a_box_sources_combined_compressed.pdf)
- The U.S. Copyright Office distinguishes uncopyrightable game procedures/titles from protectable expressive rules text and pictorial material. [U.S. Copyright Office: Games](https://www.copyright.gov/register/tx-games.html)

### Inferences
- Write new rules prose and tutorial prompts from the selected mechanics; create original board, pieces, icons, typography, illustrations, and product identity. Use the historical title only as the game’s identifier and avoid branding that suggests Bodleian, WSPU, or Sargeant Bros endorsement.
- A rights note should describe the reviewed sources and limits, not state “cleared worldwide” or promise the app is free of all rights risk.

### Gaps
- The Bodleian’s detailed electronic-use terms for the particular Suffragetto image asset were not reviewed because the implementation should not use it.
- No individualized legal advice was sought.
