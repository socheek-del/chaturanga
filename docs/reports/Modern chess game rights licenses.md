# Rights checks keep four games blocked

As of 2026-11-27, new primary-source checks do not clear the repository’s digital-adaptation gate for Djambi, Martian Chess, Apocalypse, or Game of the Generals. The project policy requires applicable permission or a license before faithful implementation, so all four remain blocked before code; this is a repository decision, not a legal conclusion. Djambi’s publisher enabled one named online adaptation, while Looney Labs’ published route for Martian Chess asks developers to seek permission before publishing. Ludii’s software terms and public Apocalypse entry do not establish a game-specific grant, and the Game of the Generals sources identify no current licensor or public adaptation license. No external contact was made. The required next evidence is a public license that covers this project or written permission from a verified rights holder.

## Existing adaptations do not grant Djambi or Martian Chess rights

Board Game Arena identifies L’Impensé radical as Djambi’s publisher and acknowledges that the publisher enabled its own adaptation, but the page does not publish a transferable license for independent developers. The current review did not verify who now controls the relevant rights or find a public digital-adaptation grant. Under the repository gate, the acknowledgment is not enough to start a separate implementation. ([Board Game Arena: Djambi](https://fr.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=djambi), [Règle du Jeu: Djambi](https://www.regledujeu.fr/djambi/))

Looney Labs’ official Electronic Arcade policy, still labeled “Updated July 2019,” directs hobby developers to request permission before publishing a freeware electronic version and states conditions for adaptations it permits. Its current Martian Chess product page offers no public digital license. Existing online versions listed by the publisher do not authorize this project, so Martian Chess also remains blocked until the owner authorizes a request and the publisher grants permission, or a public applicable license appears. ([Looney Labs Electronic Arcade](https://www.looneylabs.com/looney-labs-electronic-arcade), [Martian Chess product page](https://www.looneylabs.com/games/martian-chess))

## Ludii’s Apocalypse entry does not license the game

Ludii’s Apocalypse page identifies C. S. Elliott and 1976, but shows no game-specific license. Ludii’s download page describes a noncommercial CC BY-NC-ND 4.0 license for the Ludii player source code, while the Digital Ludeme Project data note separately discusses reuse of database data and proprietary-game exceptions. None of those statements grants Chaturanga rights from Apocalypse’s rights holder; the current rights chain and the linked ruleset’s license remain unverified. Keep the implementation gate closed. ([Ludii: Apocalypse](https://ludii.games/details.php?keyword=Apocalypse), [Ludii downloads and license](https://ludii.games/download.php), [Digital Ludeme Project database note](https://ore.eu/article/view/467))

## Game of the Generals has no verified current licensor

The official organization publishes rules and a creator-attributed history, but the pages provide no digital-adaptation license or current rights chain. A distributor describes its role in physical sets without claiming all rights or offering a digital grant; historical Arbitek licensing only documents past activity. The public Android repository checked reports no detected license, and Henerals describes itself as independent without offering third-party rights. These materials do not clear the project under repository policy. ([Official rules](https://philggo.wixsite.com/home/rules), [PhilGGO history](https://philggo.wixsite.com/home/history), [Elmer Francisco archive](https://elmerfrancisco.org/game-of-the-generals/), [GitHub repository metadata](https://api.github.com/repos/rogermadjos/GotGClient), [Henerals history](https://henerals.com/history/))

## Conclusion

The recheck narrows the next step to rights clearance rather than more rules research: no new public source makes any of these four titles eligible for implementation under the repository’s gate. The documented permission route is clearest for Martian Chess, but it still requires owner-authorized outreach; the other three need a verified rights holder and a project-specific grant or an applicable public license. No contact has been made. ([Repository roadmap](../CHESS-LIKE-ROADMAP.md), [Martian Chess plan](../../apps/martian-chess/docs/PLAN.md))
