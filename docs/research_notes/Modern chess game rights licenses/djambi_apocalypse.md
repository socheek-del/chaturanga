# Djambi and Apocalypse rights update

## Djambi: does current evidence provide an applicable digital-adaptation license?

### Takeaway

No new source found grants Chaturanga an independent license for a faithful Djambi digital product. Board Game Arena's current game page identifies L'Impensé radical as publisher and says the publisher enabled *that* adaptation; it publishes no general, transferable license for other developers.

### Cited Findings

- Board Game Arena lists Jean Anesto as author, L'Impensé radical as publisher, and 1975 as the publication year for its four-player Djambi adaptation. [Board Game Arena: Djambi](https://fr.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=djambi)
- Board Game Arena states that L'Impensé radical made its adaptation possible. The page does not state that the permission is public, transferable, or available to other developers. [Board Game Arena: Djambi](https://fr.boardgamearena.com/gamepanel?game=djambi)
- The rules reference already cited by the product review distinguishes square and hexagonal/player-count forms but contains no rights grant. [Règle du Jeu: Djambi](https://www.regledujeu.fr/djambi/)

### Inferences

- The acknowledgement is evidence of permission for Board Game Arena's implementation only; it does not, on its own, authorize a separate Chaturanga implementation. This is an inference from the wording and scope of the acknowledgement.
- Under the repository's documented gate, Djambi remains blocked before implementation until an applicable license or written permission for this project is documented.

### Gaps

- The current rights holder and any successor to L'Impensé radical were not established from primary sources reviewed.
- No public license for an independent digital adaptation was found. No contact was made.

## Apocalypse: does Ludii's public rules entry provide an applicable license?

### Takeaway

No game-specific adaptation grant was found. Ludii publicly identifies an Apocalypse entry by C. S. Elliott (1976), but the available official licensing information distinguishes the Ludii software license from DLP database data and does not identify a license for Apocalypse itself. The product remains blocked under the repository's rights gate.

### Cited Findings

- Ludii's official Apocalypse page identifies C. S. Elliott as author, 1976 as the creation date, and links a Ludii description file. It does not display a game-specific license or adaptation permission. [Ludii: Apocalypse](https://ludii.games/details.php?keyword=Apocalypse)
- Ludii's download page says the *Ludii player source code* is available for non-commercial use under CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. The page does not extend that software license to the underlying game or provide a separate Apocalypse license. [Ludii downloads and license](https://ludii.games/download.php)
- The Digital Ludeme Project database note states that its data are reusable without restriction except for categories of proprietary or sensitive data kept private, including ruleset descriptions for proprietary games for which the project lacks permission to disseminate. It defines `ProprietaryGame` as a game metadata field. [Digital Ludeme Project Database data note](https://ore.eu/article/view/467)

### Inferences

- The public Ludii rules entry and software license are not sufficient evidence that the original rights holder licensed Chaturanga to publish a faithful adaptation. In particular, the DLP note's general data-reuse statement does not establish the licensing status of the Apocalypse entry or confer rights from its author/rightsholder.
- Under the repository's documented gate, implementation should not begin until a game-specific applicable license or written permission is established.

### Gaps

- No primary source reviewed identifies the current rights holder for Elliott's game or grants independent digital-adaptation rights.
- The accessible public materials did not establish Apocalypse's DLP `ProprietaryGame` value or a game-specific license for the linked `.lud` file. No contact was made.
