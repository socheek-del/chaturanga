# Xiangqi rules (as implemented)

`packages/xiangqi` follows **Fairy-Stockfish's `xiangqi` variant**, the way `packages/makruk` and
`packages/sittuyin` follow theirs. Every rule below is checked against ffish 0.7.10 (Fairy-Stockfish compiled to
WASM). The game-end code is a port of the C++, not a reimplementation from a rulebook: `src/bitboard.ts`,
`src/chase.ts` and `src/gameEnd.ts` follow `src/position.cpp` and `src/apiutil.h` at commit `705dd366`. That is
the last upstream commit touching `position.cpp` before ffish 0.7.10 was published three days later, and
`position.cpp` on `master` is still identical to it.

Tests:

- `src/fen.test.ts`, `src/movegen.test.ts`: board, FEN and every piece rule.
- `src/perft.test.ts`: perft counts against `src/testing/perft-reference.json`, generated by ffish.
- `src/gameEnd.test.ts`: one named test per game-end ruling. Each test also plays its moves on ffish and
  checks `isGameOver(true)` and `result(true)`, so every case doubles as a probe.
- `src/reference.test.ts`: lock-step games against ffish, comparing legal moves, SAN, FEN and the result on
  every ply (details at the end).
- `src/variant.test.ts`: the shared `describeVariantConformance` suite.

## Board and pieces

A 9x10 grid of points, files a–i and ranks 1–10. Red (`w`, upper case in FEN) sits on ranks 1–5 and moves first.
Black (`b`) sits on ranks 6–10. The river runs between ranks 5 and 6. Each side's palace is files d–f of its first
three ranks.

| FEN | SAN | Red | Black | English | Moves |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| K | K | 帥 | 將 | General | one step orthogonally, inside its palace |
| A | A | 仕 | 士 | Advisor | one step diagonally, inside its palace |
| B | E | 相 | 象 | Elephant | exactly two steps diagonally, blocked by a piece on the point between ("eye"); never crosses the river |
| N | H | 傌 | 馬 | Horse | one step orthogonally then one diagonally outward, blocked by a piece on that first point ("leg") |
| R | R | 俥 | 車 | Chariot | any distance orthogonally |
| C | C | 炮 | 砲 | Cannon | moves like a chariot; captures by jumping exactly one piece ("screen") of either colour |
| P | P | 兵 | 卒 | Soldier | one step forward; after crossing the river, also one step sideways; never backward, never promotes |

- **Flying general:** the two generals may never face each other on a file with nothing between them. A move
  that leaves them facing is illegal, which also pins a lone piece standing between them.
- Start FEN: `rnbakabnr/9/1c5c1/p1p1p1p1p/9/9/P1P1P1P1P/1C5C1/9/RNBAKABNR w - - 0 1`.
- SAN spells the Horse `H` and the Elephant `E`, not their FEN letters.
- The halfmove clock resets only on a capture. Fairy-Stockfish's `nMoveRuleTypes` is the chess pawn type,
  which Xiangqi doesn't use, so a soldier move does not reset it.

## Game end

`Game.status()` checks in the order ffish's `result(true)` uses (with a draw claimed):

1. **Insufficient material → draw** (`insufficient-material`). This applies when neither side has anything but
   its general, advisors and elephants. Those pieces can never reach the enemy palace. One chariot, horse,
   cannon or soldier is enough to play on, even alone, because stalemate is not a draw in this variant
   (`has_insufficient_material`).
2. **No legal moves.** In check it is checkmate (`checkmate`). Otherwise it is stalemate
   (`{ kind: 'stalemate', winner }`), and **the stalemated side loses** (`stalemateValue = -VALUE_MATE`).
3. **50-move rule → draw** (`fifty-move`), once the halfmove clock passes 99. AXF offset: if 20 or more plies
   have been played, count the checks each side gave over the last `min(halfmove clock, plies played)` plies.
   The draw then waits 2 more plies for every check beyond 10 by the side that checked more
   (`offset = 2 x max(checks - 10, 0)`).
4. **Threefold repetition.** The game ends when the current position (board and side to move) occurs for the
   third time within the plies since the last capture. How it ends depends on what both sides did over the
   plies back to the first occurrence:
   - **Perpetual check:** one side gave check with every one of its moves. That side loses
     (`perpetual-check`). If both sides did, it is a draw.
   - Otherwise, **perpetual chase:** one side chased the same enemy piece with every one of its moves. That
     side loses (`perpetual-chase`). If both sides did, it is a draw.
   - Otherwise an **idle repetition** is a draw (`repetition`).

### What counts as a chase (`src/chase.ts`, `Position::chased()`)

Computed after every move, from the mover's point of view, as a set of victim points:

- **Candidate attacks:**
  - Direct attacks of the moved piece. A general or soldier never chases. A chariot or cannon only counts
    points off the line it moved along.
  - Discovered attacks: attacks a horse, elephant, chariot or cannon gains because the move vacated its leg,
    eye or line, or placed a new cannon screen. These use the attack pattern without the elephant's river
    limit, exactly as the C++ does.
- **Never chased:** the general (attacking it is check), and a soldier that has not crossed the river.
  A crossed soldier can be chased.
- **Always a chase**, defended or not:
  - a horse or cannon attacking a chariot;
  - an elephant or advisor attacking a chariot, cannon or horse.
- **Mutual attacks don't count:** an attack on a piece of the same type as the attacker (for example chariot
  on chariot) is not a chase. There are two exceptions:
  - the victim is pinned;
  - a horse attacks a horse that can't attack back because that horse's leg is blocked.
- **Otherwise** an attack is a chase when the victim has no real defender. A defender doesn't count if it is
  pinned to its general (including a lone piece between the two generals). It also doesn't count when the
  only defender is the general and recapturing would face the enemy general along a line.
- **Fake defenders:** a victim piece that the move newly pins no longer defends. Any piece it was
  protecting that the mover attacks counts as chased.
- **Discovered checks:** a mover piece that newly screens a check on the victim's general counts all its
  attacks as chases, except captures the victim's general could safely answer.

A side chases a piece "with every move" when that piece, followed through its own moves
(`undo_move_board`), is in the chased set after each of that side's moves back to the first occurrence. That
check is skipped for the very first position of a game, which has no previous move.

### Named cases (`src/gameEnd.test.ts`)

| Case | Result |
|---|---|
| `3k5/9/8R/9/9/9/9/9/9/4K4 w`, `i8i9` | stalemate, Red wins |
| Horses out and back twice from the start | idle repetition draw on the 8th ply, not at the 2nd occurrence |
| `3k5/R8/9/9/9/9/9/9/9/4K4 w`, chariot checks on ranks 10/9 twice | perpetual check, Black wins on the 8th ply |
| `3k5/9/9/9/c8/9/9/9/9/1R2K4 w`, chariot follows cannon a↔b twice | perpetual chase, Black wins |
| Same with a Black chariot on a10 defending a6 | draw (only every other attack is a chase) |
| `3k5/9/9/9/9/9/p8/9/9/1R2K4 w`, chariot follows a crossed soldier | perpetual chase, Black wins |
| `3k5/9/9/9/r8/9/9/9/9/1R2K4 w`, chariot follows chariot | draw (mutual attack) |
| `3k5/9/9/9/9/9/9/9/9/R3K4 w - - 98 60`, two quiet moves | 50-move draw at 100 |
| `3k5/9/9/9/n8/9/9/9/9/R3K4 w - - 70 60`, 38 plies with 14 Red checks | 50-move draw at 108, not 100 |
| Generals, advisors and elephants only | insufficient material |
| An advisor captures the last horse | insufficient material |
| A lone crossed soldier against advisors | game goes on |

### Lock-step coverage (`src/reference.test.ts`, `npm run test:deep`)

- **Random games:** 400 in the deep run, 60 in `npm test`. Every other game uses a repetition-biased picker
  that steps back along its own last move or gives check.
- **Forced cycles:** 4000 deep, 400 normal. From a random middlegame each side plays a quiet move and back
  twice.
- **Cycles found by ffish:** 1000 deep, 60 normal. For a random middlegame, ffish alone tries every pair of
  quiet moves and keeps the pairs whose 8-ply cycle ends decisively. One of those cycles is then replayed
  through both engines. The positive chase and check cases come from the reference engine, not from the
  code under test.

Deep run on 2026-09-17:

| Test | Endings |
|---|---|
| Random games | 91 checkmate, 3 stalemate, 205 repetition, 1 perpetual check, 11 fifty-move, 8 insufficient material, 81 hit the ply cap |
| Forced cycles | 2689 repetition, 10 perpetual check, 2 perpetual chase, 1205 broken cycles |
| ffish-found cycles | 190 perpetual chase, 71 perpetual check |

**Limit:** ffish reports only the result string (`1-0`, `0-1`, `1/2-1/2`), not why the game ended. The kind
(`perpetual-check` or `perpetual-chase`, `repetition` or `fifty-move`) comes from the ported branch that
produced the result. The named tests pin the kind for each case above.
